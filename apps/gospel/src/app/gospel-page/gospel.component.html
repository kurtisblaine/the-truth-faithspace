The Danger - Futility

Futility of Pleasure

Alcohol and food, which are wholesome in themselves, which are given to us by God for our enjoyment, but are often perverted into drunkenness and gluttony, and drugs, which are often used for an escape from reality, a false refuge, which are often corrupted for one's own enjoyment rather than medicine, and entertainment, which is vain, which is child's play, which wastes our already short lives, and all other like pleasures which come from our lust, which wages war against our soul, these can never and will never sustains us - they only mask the deeply rooted issues we have for a little while and make life bearable for a short time. They come quickly and vanish quickly; the same way they come, the same way they go. Is life so terrible that we need these? We all had our share in these things during some time in our life; and if it was not then, perhaps it is now? But where is the light of life? For the way we feel, living in such things, surely that is not life. Perhaps we don't know truly what life is. Living for pleasure masks our issues; it is like hitting the snooze button on the alarm clock: you feel better now, but you will have to deal with the same problem later - but it has gotten worse. Even if we feel 'satisfied' or 'fulfilled' with these, it is never enough: it is like we are feeding a beast within us. The more we feed it, the bigger and stronger it gets; it requires more and more of us & it is never satisfied. Even our gut growls like a dog when its not fed; how much more sin living within us which is a hound from hell?

We eat food, but we are not content; we drink, but we are not sustained; we eat, and are hungry again later; we drink, and become thirsty again; as it says, All a man's labor is for his mouth and yet the appetite is not satisfied. And these, being for our enjoyment and nourishment, we corrupt into something it was never intended for. Food for the body, but God forbid for gluttony; drink to make the heart merry, but God forbid for drunkenness; sex for reproduction, but God forbid for immorality. Lust corrupts God given necessities into dissipation and waste: for over-eating is not for hunger, and drunkenness is not for thirst, and immorality is not for reproduction; all of these are for pleasure; and from wisdom it does not come, The mind of the wise is in the house of mourning, while the mind of fools is in the house of pleasure. So pleasure is not of wisdom; and pleasure is fleeting - only lasting for a short while. But prudence makes the most of her time. No matter how much the eye sees, it is not enough; no matter how much the ear hears, it is not content. If these things truly make us happy, why wouldn't we express it outwardly? If we are truly happy, shouldn't our happiness resemble a child's? Children are overcome with laughter and express joyfulness and run around expressing their happiness, jumping with joy. If these things truly make us happy, we surely don't show it. Most often we are chained by such things: we look miserable on the outside, but inwardly, we become less miserable - these are only masking our pain for a short while. In the beginning, we used to be happy, when sin wasn't alive, in the innocence of our childhood, but later, sin came alive and we died. All of us have become enslaved to our own desires which corrupt - for all lust corrupts. Who is exempt from this? None of us. We are all under sin. We are lead by the hand by this world into a cycle of dependence: we think we need these things to keep us in check: "I don't feel normal without them", we say to ourselves. I've been chained by many things for years of my life, and looking back at my experience, surely that was not life. Whatever a person is overcome by, that he is also enslaved. And how weak are we when something inaminate chains us! "I serve this, and that, and I can't stop!" Whatever we offer ourselves to as obedient servants, by that, we are enslaved. I was enslaved to everything a man could be ensalved by: food, alcohol, drugs, money, & many other such things. These things are not alive, but somehow I was enslaved to them; I loved the things of the world and worked for them. I wouldn't call myself a man, for I couldn't get out by my own strength. It was dark and cold: loveless. I wanted to get out, but I couldn't: it was surely a jail cell. I wanted to stop sinning, but I couldn't; I wanted out, but couldn't redeem myself. Hope, I had none. "Where is real pleasure and satisfaction?", I asked; and worse yet, what I experienced I didn't know how to utter it; for perhaps if I could tell someone, they could help. But instead, guilt and shame keep me from sharing, as if I were the only one on the planet dealing with such things; just as Solomon, who had great wisdom from God, wrote, All things are full of weariness; a man cannot utter it; the eye is not satisfied with seeing, nor the ear filled with hearing.

Solomon in his wisdom wrote, I said in my heart, “Come now, I will test you with pleasure; enjoy yourself.” But behold, this also was vanity. I said of laughter, “It is mad,” and of pleasure, “What use is it?” I searched with my heart how to cheer my body with wine—my heart still guiding me with wisdom—and how to lay hold on folly, till I might see what was good for the children of man to do under heaven during the few days of their life. (...) And whatever my eyes desired I did not keep from them. I kept my heart from no pleasure, for my heart found pleasure in all my toil, and this was my reward for all my toil. Then I considered all that my hands had done and the toil I had expended in doing it, and behold, all was vanity and a striving after wind, and there was nothing to be gained under the sun. Wisdom is insight: speaking out things which are from within with the correct words; and Solomon's words are insightful. Every sinful habit begins with lust; and lust entices the eyes. All sin begins here: with lust. And how computer technology stimulates all sorts of lust through images! It is satisfying while on it, but afterwards, it's like waking from a sleep. We live in an age where media and screens are everywhere: it started with televisions in the living room, then escalated to monitors in our pockets. Surely these feed our eyes. This is what I meant when I said we are led into sin by the world; for who creates advertisements? Who makes accessible the desires of our heart? But still, it's our desire and we will be the ones who will suffer for indulging in it. Even a computer can create entire simulations for people to live a new life inside. Can the copy ever be as good as the real? Which do you like better: name brand goods or knock offs? Yes, knock offs are cheaper and require less of us, but they are not better, even in the slightest. In the same way, these simulations are cheaper: you can sit on the couch and be at a sports game! So lust shows itself as something great, but is actually less; it makes great boasts, but fulfills none of them. It tricks you into wanting something you did not desire; and it fulfills that desire with a fake, not with what its image portrays. Does an image every portray reality perfectly? Of course not! The artist always makes it better than reality, but this is never what you're getting.

The natural use is better than the unnatural; the real is better than the copy; good is better than evil, even in the meanwhile if you think, "Ah! But it feels so good", in the end you will not be thinking that! Everything we sow, we will reap. A person does not sow an apple and get an orange tree; neither does a person breed pigs and get cows; so why do we think, "I will sow corruption and get life"? Everything corrupts through lust; and lust conceived gives birth to sin; and all sin is a dysfunction; and a dysfunction is uselessness. And that which is useless is put into a pit and burned. A new sofa will become fettered, a new car will become a tin of rust, even our bodies are becoming old and gray and worn out and will be soon in the grave. Evil will inherit evil in the end and goodness will inherit goodness. This is true, even if, in the meanwhile, we see the wicked prospering and the righteous suffering; as it says, I have seen everything during my lifetime of futility; there is a righteous man who perishes in his righteousness and there is a wicked man who prolongs his life in his wickedness. And, There is futility which is done on the earth, that is, there are righteous men to whom it happens according to the deeds of the wicked. On the other hand, there are evil men to whom it happens according to the deeds of the righteous. I say that this too is futility. But, I said to myself, "God will judge both the righteous man and the wicked man," for a time for every matter and for every deed is there. Everyone will get what their deeds deserve in the end because God will cause His righteous judgement to be upheld. So take heed! But she who lives in pleasure is dead while she lives. If we live for pleasure only, our life is a waste: that is dissipation. Instead, fear God. Enjoy what God has given you, without grumbling or complaining; as it says, So I commended pleasure, for there is nothing good for a man under the sun except to eat and to drink and to be merry, and this will stand by him in his toils throughout the days of his life which God has given him under the sun. And, I know that there is nothing better for them than to rejoice and to do good in one's lifetime; moreover, that every man who eats and drinks sees good in all his labor - it is the gift of God.

Futility in Toil

Our lives are filled with work and toil: we work, then sleep, to do it all over again the next day. Our days are short, yet we spend them in much pain and agony because of our labors. And what drives us? I have seen that every labor and every skill which is done is the result of rivalry between a man and his neighbor. This too is vanity and striving after wind. So we work for envy which will not matter in the future; we work for the sake of man that perishes; we work for objectives that are not ours, for fruit that we will not eat of. We leave the fruit of our labors to another who comes after us. The things we do will not matter, even years from now, it will become old and outdated. In the future, people will look back and say, "Who are they?", just as we presently do not care what happened in times past, so it will be in the future. Even if we think, "I care", what does your boasting accomplish? No-one can bring back a day which has been spent. Our constant striving and labor, yet we don't receive our due wages; he who does little work, harvests much; and we are dumb-founded. We work hard for the holes in our pockets. We plant, and the locusts eat; we plant much, and gather very little. It is a grievous task: And I set my mind to seek and explore by wisdom concerning all that has been done under heaven. It is a grievous task which God has given to the sons of men to be afflicted with. I have seen all the works which have been done under the sun, and behold, all is vanity and striving after wind. And, There was a certain man without a dependent, having neither a son nor a brother, yet there was no end to all his labor. Indeed, his eyes were not satisfied with riches and he never asked, "And for whom am I laboring and depriving myself of pleasure?" This too is vanity and it is a grievous task.

We were born into travail, born for trouble: "For man is born for trouble, As sparks fly upward." (Job 5:7) All our days are stricken with evil. We don't know where we are going; and we don't remember where we have come from; we aren't fully aware of even today - we're in a box without the ability to look out. And even if we had 1000 lives of 1000 years, we wouldn't be able to discover all that is before us - how much less the things we don't see! We chase things we think will satisfy us, but we end up forever chasing without ever catching them. Are we any better than the animals? For they come in futility and leave in futility; as it says, For what happens to the sons of men also happens to animals; one thing befalls them: as one dies, so dies the other. Surely, they all have one breath; man has no advantage over animals, for all is vanity. Everything that we work for, we do not keep; we strive for beauty, but it is fading away. Our possessions will become the possessions of another. Even our own lives are borrowed, for we will return to the dust. The King of Israel, Solomon, wrote toward the end of his life, I hated all my toil in which I toil under the sun, seeing that I must leave it to the man who will come after me, and who knows whether he will be wise or a fool? Yet he will be master of all for which I toiled and used my wisdom under the sun. This also is vanity. So I turned about and gave my heart up to despair over all the toil of my labors under the sun, because sometimes a person who has toiled with wisdom and knowledge and skill must leave everything to be enjoyed by someone who did not toil for it. This also is vanity and a great evil. What has a man from all the toil and striving of heart with which he toils beneath the sun? For all his days are full of sorrow, and his work is a vexation. Even in the night his heart does not rest. This also is vanity. Futility is worthlessness; it is emptiness, not full, aimless, not having a point or direction, wandering about in blackest darkness. It does not see; it is always doing much, but accomplishing little, speaking much, but getting nothing done, dreaming, but living as a passing shadow. What then, do we despair?

Life is short, but it feels long. In life, we despair, but without it, there is no hope. We spend our lives wasting on things that will pass away. We are passing away. In the place of the dead, where we are going, there is nothing: no toil, no labor, no speech, no sight, nothing. We have inherited this life, not because we asked for it, for we did not ask even to be born, but because of the sin of Adam in the garden, we have been given this kind of life. All people on earth came from him and we inherit sin and death from him; he transgressed the commandment and judgement was pronounced on the earth because of him: Cursed is the ground because of you; In toil you will eat of it all the days of your life. Both thorns and thistles it shall grow for you; And you will eat the plants of the field; By the sweat of your face You will eat bread, Till you return to the ground, Because from it you were taken; For you are dust, And to dust you shall return. For, the LORD says, See now that I, I am He, And there is no god besides Me; It is I who put to death and give life. I have wounded and it is I who heal, And there is no one who can deliver from My hand. And, I am the LORD, and there is no other; Besides Me there is no God. (...) The One forming light and creating darkness, Causing well-being and creating calamity; I am the LORD who does all these. And who are we to quarrel with our Maker? But rather, There is nothing better for a man than to eat and drink and tell himself that his labor is good. This also I have seen that it is from the hand of God. For who can eat and who can have enjoyment without Him? For to a person who is good in His sight He has given wisdom and knowledge and joy, while to the sinner He has given the task of gathering and collecting so that he may give to one who is good in God's sight. This too is vanity and striving after wind. And, One hand full of rest is better than two fists full of labor and striving after wind.

Therefore, it is better to accept your lot in life: be happy with it. Search and seek for Him; acknowledge your futility and search and seek for fullness. As the Lord promised, You will seek Me and find Me when you search for Me with all your heart. Be not happy with your futility, but search for answers. Do not follow the example of the disobedient who love their futility. See how they try to fight against God, trying to reverse the curses that God has pronounced on the earth? Thinking their life is forever, they fall into error; thinking their home is forever, they believe a lie. See how the world tries to reverse things: God gave woman pain in child-bearing, but they give numbing shots; God made woman desire for her husband, yet he rules over her, but they start feminist movements; God cursed the ground so that in toil it will produce its fruit, but they create technology with works at the push of a button; God makes man to return to the dust in old age, but they have beauty products and wrinkling agents and plastic surgery; but whatever we try to do, we cannot reverse death: all of us will return to Him in the end, slave and free, rich and poor.

Futility of Wealth

There once was a man that went into a pub with everything he owns. He sits down at a gambling table to gamble what he owns. At first, he bets only a little of what he has. He rolls the dice and a miracle happens: all the dice line up. The man yells, "Today is my lucky day. I will bet even more next time!" So he rolls again, but this time with more of his possessions on the table. A miracle happens again: all the dice line up. The man continues this over and over many times and everytime, he wins; eventually, everything he owned was on the table, even the shirt off his back. He thought to himself, "Surely I have rolled these dice over and over, and they haven't failed me yet!" However, the dealer of the dice sees the man and thinks to himself, "You fool! One day these dice will not line up and everything you have will be mine, for I have worked a long time in this pub and I see it happen everytime - the dice will one day not line up and everything you have will be mine." Sure enough, the day came when the dice did not line up, just like the dealer said, and everything on the table was taken from him. Then, because the man didn't even have enough money for a beer, he was cast out of the pub into darkness where he wept because everything he had was gone. And so it is with every man who thinks only of his life and his possessions and money; for, There is an evil which I have seen under the sun and it is prevalent among men— a man to whom God has given riches and wealth and honor so that his soul lacks nothing of all that he desires; yet God has not empowered him to eat from them, for a foreigner enjoys them. This is vanity and a severe affliction.

Solomon wrote, He who loves money will not be satisfied with money, nor he who loves wealth with his income; this also is vanity. When goods increase, they increase who eat them, and what advantage has their owner but to see them with his eyes? Sweet is the sleep of a laborer, whether he eats little or much, but the full stomach of the rich will not let him sleep. There is a grievous evil that I have seen under the sun: riches were kept by their owner to his hurt, and those riches were lost in a bad venture. And he is father of a son, but he has nothing in his hand. As he came from his mother's womb he shall go again, naked as he came, and shall take nothing for his toil that he may carry away in his hand. This also is a grievous evil: just as he came, so shall he go, and what gain is there to him who toils for the wind? Moreover, all his days he eats in darkness in much vexation and sickness and anger.

Look: point to something that you can take when you leave earth. Not even your own body can go with when you die: it goes back to the dust from which it came; as it is written, Then the LORD God formed man of dust from the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living being. And, Then the dust will return to the earth as it was, and the spirit will return to God who gave it. When we return to Him, it will be a single moment to Him, but a lifetime to us. Everything we think we have, we don't have - we leave it to someone after us who did not work nor toil for it; and even if it's not so, what do we really own? For, The earth is the LORD's and the fullness thereof, the world and those who dwell therein We are merely stewards. Riches do not deliver on the day of wrath; and all the silver in the world cannot forgive even a single sin; as it says, No man can by any means redeem his brother Or give to God a ransom for him — For the redemption of his soul is costly, And he should cease trying forever — That he should live on eternally, That he should not undergo decay. As it says, "Cease trying forever", for that which is costly, is freely given, and that, to humiliate the rich. And amassing riches, brings hurt to oneself; and trusting in them is a futile attempt for saftey. Many pierce themselves with sorrows chasing after riches; as it says, Like the partridge that gathers a brood that she did not hatch, so is he who gets riches but not by justice; in the midst of his days they will leave him, and at his end he will be a fool. And, There is a grievous evil that I have seen under the sun: riches were kept by their owner to his hurt, and those riches were lost in a bad venture. So, For the love of money is a root of all sorts of evil, and some by longing for it have wandered away from the faith and pierced themselves with many griefs. Hording wealth and being rich towards oneself, keeping one's own fat, not sharing with others, displeases God; and who do we think we hurt by doing so? If we give, it is to the benefit of others; if we withhold, it is to the hurt of others; even yet, we bring harm to ourselves through dishonest gain by not paying those who are due wages; as it says, Your gold and silver have corroded, and their corrosion will be evidence against you and will eat your flesh like fire. You have laid up treasure in the last days. Behold, the wages of the laborers who mowed your fields, which you kept back by fraud, are crying out against you, and the cries of the harvesters have reached the ears of the Lord of hosts. You have lived on the earth in luxury and in self-indulgence. You have fattened your hearts in a day of slaughter.

Rather, be rich toward God: And he said to them, “Take care, and be on your guard against all covetousness, for one’s life does not consist in the abundance of his possessions.” And he told them a parable, saying, “The land of a rich man produced plentifully, and he thought to himself, ‘What shall I do, for I have nowhere to store my crops?’ And he said, ‘I will do this: I will tear down my barns and build larger ones, and there I will store all my grain and my goods. And I will say to my soul, “Soul, you have ample goods laid up for many years; relax, eat, drink, be merry.”’ But God said to him, ‘Fool! For when an animal offering was made, the fat of the offering was burnt up to God: so the fat is the LORD's. So seek the gift of God: Furthermore, as for every man to whom God has given riches and wealth, He has also empowered him to eat from them and to receive his reward and rejoice in his labor; this is the gift of God. For he will not often consider the years of his life, because God keeps him occupied with the gladness of his heart.

Futility in Death

There is a man who lives in a tent. All his days are spent in constant agony in his tent since it is not comfortable: the flies bite, the sun beams, the moon strikes. Nevertheless, he hoped forward to his permanent home which was far away in a distant land being prepared for him. Night and day he longed for it and thought about it, dreaming for it. After many, many years, his home was ready, and his tent tore, so he went away to his home in which he hoped. The tent was destroyed; the man went to his home; he rejoiced and spent the rest of his days there forever more. Amen. For man goes to his eternal home while mourners go about in the street.

We have this present life, but it is not ours to keep; if our life is for eternity, then we wouldn't have to die, but because we will one day die, we know this isn't our home. Our lives are entrusted to us for only a short while. Since we know we will die, we look forward to our next life: our permanent home. How we end will determine where we spend eternity. The time in the tent is a time of testing; and we always have two choices: good or evil. If there were only one choice, we wouldn't have free will - since we would be forced into choosing the only option - but there are two choices. We will reap what we sow; we gain what we have put in; we will be recompensed according to what we have done in our bodies, that is, our tents. There is life after life, which is eternal; and there is death after death, which is the second death; the second death is hell, the lake of fire. Anyone whose name is not written in the Book of Life will die a second death, which is eternal shame and torment. Paradise is the Kingdom of God: the new Jerusalem, Mount Zion, the new heaven and the new earth, where righteousness dwells. The present heaven and earth are subjected to evil and violence; and all will soon pass away as this present world is passing away, as we ourselves are passing away. The new heaven and the new earth will never pass away: a kingdom that cannot be shaken. After the great judgement, when the LORD will judge the living and the dead according to their deeds, and recompense, we will go to our permanent place. God's judgement will be based on righteousness - and who can argue? He will reward all who do good for the good done and repay harm for the harm done. For certain, God has sown everlasting life in the human heart so that we would covet life, but we die; we desire life, yet we are without knowledge; as it says, He has made everything appropriate in its time. He has also set eternity in their heart, yet so that man will not find out the work which God has done from the beginning even to the end. (...) I said to myself, “God will judge both the righteous man and the wicked man,” for a time for every matter and for every deed is there. I concluded that God is testing us, to show us that we are no better than animals. After all, the same fate awaits human beings and animals alike. One dies just like the other. They are both the same kind of creature. A human being is no better off than an animal, because life has no meaning for either. They are both going to the same place — the dust. They both came from it; they will both go back to it. How can anyone be sure that the human spirit goes upwards while an animal's spirit goes down into the ground? I have seen that nothing is better than that man should be happy in his activities, for that is his lot. For who will bring him to see what will occur after him?

In the state that we die is the same state that we will be raised: if a sinner dies, he will be raised a sinner and if a righteous man dies, he will be raised a righteous man. The LORD shows us through sleep what death is like: we sleep and hours have passed without us knowing. In sleep, we've accomplished nothing, we've done nothing, except rest; those whom the Lord is pleased, He teaches them in their sleep; while they dream in the night, He ministers in parables; in the place of the dead, there is nothing. While we live, there is hope for repentance; in the place of the dead, there is no hope; as it does says, Also, the hearts of the children of man are full of evil, and madness is in their hearts while they live, and after that they go to the dead. But he who is joined with all the living has hope, for a living dog is better than a dead lion. For the living know that they will die, but the dead know nothing, and they have no more reward, for the memory of them is forgotten. Their love and their hate and their envy have already perished, and forever they have no more share in all that is done under the sun. (...) Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with your might, for there is no work or thought or knowledge or wisdom in Sheol, to which you are going. We see generations of our descendants grow up before our eyes, at the prime of our joy and delight, we leave and fly away. Not one of us chose our family, no-one the body in which they were born, not one what country they would live, yea, no one even asked to be born! We are all given without a say, and it is all taken away without our gesture; as it does say, No man has authority to restrain the wind with the wind, or authority over the day of death; and there is no discharge in the time of war, and evil will not deliver those who practice it. And, Moreover, man does not know his time: like fish caught in a trecherous net and birds trapped in a snare, so the sons of men are ensnared at an evil time when it suddenly falls on them. We have all been dealt cards that cannot be taken back, we will all be judged based on what God has given us; and do we dare quarrel with our Maker? Woe to him who strives with Him who formed him, a pot among earthen pots! Does the clay say to him who forms it, ‘What are you making?’ or ‘Your work has no handles’? Woe to him who says to a father, ‘What are you begetting?’ or to a woman, ‘With what are you in labor?’ It is by God's grace that He gives us our lives. If we are dissatisfied with our cards, do we dare try to take them back? Knowing where they come from, can we take them back as goods bought in a store? Can we have a do-over in this life or trade our cards for someone else's? Surely God is just: wouldn't He show us a way to redemption and forgiveness? Does He not know that we are wayward, in need of a Shepherd? One day we will return our cards to the Dealer - for our hand is only for a short while. Should we go all in for something that is temporary? No! Rather, for that which is everlasting! But where is the everlasting path? Where the ancient road? Will someone show the way to life? If there is a way, surely only God could reveal it; but rather, LORD, make me to know my end And what is the extent of my days; Let me know how transient I am. Behold, You have made my days as handbreadths, And my lifetime as nothing in Your sight; Surely every man at his best is a mere breath. And, So teach us to number our days, that we may present to You a heart of wisdom.

The Danger - Sin

God is a Consuming Fire

We desire to do good with our minds, but with our bodies, we don't do what we intend to do. We see it every year: we make a new year's resolution and say, "This year will be nothing like the last, I am going to do this and do that!" And we do 'this' and 'that' very well for the first few months, but the time comes when we inevitably go back to our old ways. What we desire in our mind, we have not the strength in our flesh to carry it out; in our mind we want to do good, but in our flesh, we don't do the good we want; we have good intentions, but the power of our sinful nature carries us away. If we did everything we wanted to do every new year, as time goes on, we would be made more and more perfect; but in fact, we become more and more sinful, storing up our wrongs and being wronged. We sin, piling up more and more transgression into our accounts, which is a debt against us. And who is omitted from such? Who can say, "I have cleansed my heart, I am pure from my sin"? And, Indeed, there is not a righteous man on earth who continually does good and who never sins. Or even, What is man, that he should be pure, or he who is born of a woman, that he should be righteous? Behold, He puts no trust in His holy ones, and the heavens are not pure in His sight; how much less one who is detestable and corrupt, man, who drinks iniquity like water! And, Is there any number to His troops? And upon whom does His light not rise? How then can a man be just with God? Or how can he be clean who is born of a woman? Jesus, the Lamb of God

Under the Old Covenant, the Israelites obtained atonement by God for their sins by sacrificing an animal; when they sinned, blood needed to be shed for cleansing: the animal had to be perfect (no defects, blemishes, etc), it also had to be a clean animal, and it had to be innocent of all wrong (like goring, etc). Blood is cleansing to the spirit just as water is cleansing to the body; with sin comes death, for sin and death walk hand-in-hand; death is the result of sin, and life is the result of righteousness; therefore death is needed for atonement, blood needs to be shed for forgiveness. When the substitute sacrifice of an animal occurred, the one offering was covered of his fault; afterwards, it was as if the fault had never happened, for it was forgiven; the wrong was offset, but the continuing sacrifice causes remembrance. The Israelites could only give sacrifice for unintentional sin: that is, sins that were done in ignorance. There was no sacrifice under the Law for intentional sin. These sins were expiated in another way: either excommunication (being cut off), punishment, or death. There were also certain sins that could never be atoned for, except with the blood of the slayer: murder, for instance. Rebellion was also not forgiven, for this is the nature of rebellion: you know what is right, but you rebel against it; as it says in the old covenant, Behold, I am going to send an angel [Christ] before you to guard you along the way and to bring you into the place which I have prepared. Be on your guard before Him and obey His voice; do not be rebellious toward Him, for He will not pardon your transgression since My name is in Him. And the same in the new covenant, For if we go on sinning willfully after receiving the knowledge of the truth, there no longer remains a sacrifice for sins, but a terrifying expectation of judgment and THE FURY OF A FIRE WHICH WILL CONSUME THE ADVERSARIES. For all rebellion is willful, and such sins are punished through chastening; but, When we are judged, we are disciplined by the Lord so that we will not be condemned along with the world. So we see that the Israelites had to sacrifice a perfect lamb, or a similar clean animal, to be forgiven of their unintentional sins; the clean animal cleansed them from their filth; the unblemished beast bore their blemish. The sacrifice did not cleanse the offerer's conscience though, because they still had guilt that the innocent animal had to die in their place; the sacrifice did not take away their sin, but only covered their sin temporarily; as it says in Hebrews, But in those [sacrifices] there is a reminder of sins year by year. For it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins. A goat is not equivalent to a human, so it cannot be substituted for a human; but rather, life shall be for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot.

The animal was the offerer's substitute, and the sin offering was brought into the Holy Place when the congregation as a whole sinned, and the bodies of those animals were burnt outside the camp and not eaten by the offerer; as it says, Although the high priest brings the blood of animals into the Holy Place as a sacrifice for sin, the bodies are burned outside the camp. And we don't have guilt because our sins have caused Christ to be slain, for He lives, and that, forevermore, seated in the heavens - by His resurrection, we have justification with God. His one act of obedience is the work that matters in the sight of God, united with our work of faith. But Christ was sacrificed apart from our works, by the grace of God to be received by faith. Jesus, the Lion from the Tribe of Judah

Then Samson went down with his father and mother to Timnah, and they came to the vineyards of Timnah. And behold, a young lion came toward him roaring. Then the Spirit of the Lord rushed upon him, and although he had nothing in his hand, he tore the lion in pieces as one tears a young goat. But he did not tell his father or his mother what he had done. Then he went down and talked with the woman, and she was right in Samson's eyes. After some days he returned to take her. And he turned aside to see the carcass of the lion, and behold, there was a swarm of bees in the body of the lion, and honey. He scraped it out into his hands and went on, eating as he went. And he came to his father and mother and gave some to them, and they ate. But he did not tell them that he had scraped the honey from the carcass of the lion. (...) Out of the eater came something to eat. Out of the strong came something sweet.

A lion is strong and powerful, no doubt about that. No-one certainly takes a lion into their home so that their children can play with it - no, they don't. A lion is dangerous. A lion isn't sweet; it won't let you pet it and it won't give you any kisses (not without taking off some of your flesh first). In a similar way, Jesus Christ is mighty and powerful being the Son of God in the exact likeness of God with the name which is above every other name, and one of His names: the Lion from the Tribe of Judah; as it says, Then one of the elders said to me, 'Do not weep! See, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has triumphed. He [Jesus] is able to open the scroll and its seven seals.' He walked in power while on earth: He healed many, He spoke with authority, by the power of His Word, the devils cried aloud and were driven out of their hosts, the people trembled behind Him as they followed. Yet, He died a criminal's death although He was not a criminal and there was no fault found in Him. His life was not taken from Him, but He laid it down willingly; He had authority to take it back up again, as He said before He was captured, Or do you think that I cannot appeal to My Father, and He will at once put at My disposal more than twelve legions of angels? How then will the Scriptures be fulfilled, [which say] that it must happen this way? So although He is powerful, He willingly became weak for our sakes. He did many mighty miracles: walking on water, hushing the wind and waves with a word, and many other such things. Yet, from the Strong came something sweet; and this is the sweetness of the gospel: eternal life through His name. By His death came reconciliation and justification, just as the bees produced honey in the carcass of the lion.

And also, the Father God is the Eater of sacrifices. For whenever a sacrifice was given to the LORD, He consumed them; He consumed them with fire, for He is a consuming fire; and all sacrifices were burnt on the altar unto God as a soothing aroma. So you see, from the Eater of sacrifices came a Sacrifice, from the death of the Mighty came something sweet. From His death came forgiveness of sins and justification with God, our friendship restored with God, the promise of the Holy Spirit, and everlasting life to all who persist in the grace of God. Although He is as a powerful lion, He also became as a lamb which is meek and lowly; Jesus is the Great Shepherd of the flock, and He is also the Lamb of God. And although God is the Eater of sacrifices, He doesn't need us to eat, nor does He need us to serve Him as if He needed anything from our hands, but rather, He gives us a Sacrifice so that we would honor Him with righteous sacrifices; as it says in the Psalms, Hear, O My people, and I will speak; O Israel, I will testify against you; I am God, your God. I do not reprove you for your sacrifices, And your burnt offerings are continually before Me. I shall take no young bull out of your house Nor male goats out of your folds. For every beast of the forest is Mine, The cattle on a thousand hills. I know every bird of the mountains, And everything that moves in the field is Mine. If I were hungry I would not tell you, For the world is Mine, and all it contains. Shall I eat the flesh of bulls Or drink the blood of male goats? Offer to God a sacrifice of thanksgiving And pay your vows to the Most High; Call upon Me in the day of trouble; I shall rescue you, and you will honor Me. As the riddle from Samson says, Out of the eater came something to eat; out of the strong came something sweet. God, the Father, the consumer of sacrifices, gave a Sacrifice, which is Christ, for us to eat: His flesh and His blood (which is Communion - not in the literal sense as the Catholics believe). He being strong as a lion, has been 'sweet' to us in Christ Jesus. Although He is greatly gracious toward us, that is not an excuse for us to do evil, for He is powerful, but became weak for our sakes; therefore, do not take His kindness for weakness - He's mighty in His dealing with us, specifically for our building up, not for our tearing down. If we neglect His cloak of righteousness for a cloak of maliciousness, certainly He won't treat us differently than the Gentiles, would He? His kindness towards us causes us to repentence.

And we, being His younger brother in God's family, are redeemed by Him; we are of His family if we do the will of God, the Father; as Christ says, "Who is My mother and who are My brothers?" At the appointed time, the day of Atonement, the high priest confessed the people's sins onto a goat by laying his hands on its head. That goat was to carry the confessed sins far away to a remote place in the wilderness and die, bearing sins upon itself. And listen to what the apostle says about when this happened, "While we were still helpless, at the appointed time", For while we were still weak, at the right time Christ died for the ungodly. For scarcely for a righteous man will one die; yet perhaps for a good man someone would even dare to die. But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. Much more then, having now been justified by His blood, we shall be saved from wrath through Him. For if when we were enemies we were reconciled to God through the death of His Son, much more, having been reconciled, we shall be saved by His life. And not only that, but we also rejoice in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom we have now received the reconciliation. He has removed our sins from us to a remote place, not to be remembered, passing them off to a distant land, a wasteland; likewise, Therefore Jesus also, that He might sanctify the people through His own blood, suffered outside the gate. Oh, that we would fear God, so that we would stay away from all appearances of evil; oh, that we would know what is good for us; for the fear of the Lord is to depart from iniquity: By lovingkindness and truth iniquity is atoned for, And by the fear of the LORD one keeps away from evil.

Jesus, the True Food & the True Drink

The food we eat goes into the mouth, then into the stomach and out into the sewer; and yet, we constantly strive for such food, although we are never fully satisfied. The spiritual food we eat goes in through the eyes and hears, then into the heart and out of the mouth - this is what I call the spiritual digestive system. The eye is the lamp of the body; so then if your eye is clear, your whole body will be full of light. But if your eye is bad, your whole body will be full of darkness. If then the light that is in you is darkness, how great is the darkness! And, Does not the ear test words, As the palate tastes its food? So, the things that proceed out of the mouth come from the heart, and those defile the man. Therefore let us live in God, abiding in Him for our life, filling ourselves with His Word so that, by His Word, He would live in us. He has given us a beautiful promise: You are already clean because of the word which I have spoken to you. Abide in Me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself unless it abides in the vine, so neither [can] you unless you abide in Me. I am the vine, you are the branches; he who abides in Me and I in him, he bears much fruit, for apart from Me you can do nothing. And, If you continue in My word, then you are truly disciples of Mine; and you will know the truth, and the truth will make you free. If we abide in His Word, we abide in His love. When we eat food, it fills us up and gives us strength for a short while; but God's grace is our strength and food for the spirit. I ask, when does food work the best? When you are full or empty? When you are empty a piece of bread tastes like a steak! So it says, A satisfied soul loathes the honeycomb, But to a hungry soul every bitter thing is sweet. And similarily Christ says, My grace is sufficient for you, for power is perfected in weakness. For God opposes the proud, but gives grace to the humble. Being proud is being full of oneself, and God's grace is not useful to a proud soul, for he tramples over the word of His grace; however, if he empties himself in humility, God's grace works best in such a man, filling up, not with the air of himself, but with the grace of Christ, just as it is with food to an empty stomach; as the writter of Hebrews says, Do not be carried away by varied and strange teachings; for it is good for the heart to be strengthened by grace, not by foods, through which those who were so occupied were not benefited. We have an altar from which those who serve the tabernacle have no right to eat. For God's grace is truly our food, and the table of the altar is the cross. As athletes say, "I eat and drink the gym", meaning, "That's all I do." If an athlete competes, training in such a way for a crown that perishes, how much more should we, eat and drink Christ, for a crown of eternal life that does not perish? In that case, Christ will live in you because you live in Him - and you will not stop speaking about Him! As He testified and His testimony is true, Truly, truly, I say to you, unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood, you have no life in you. Whoever feeds on my flesh and drinks my blood has eternal life, and I will raise him up on the last day. For my flesh is true food, and my blood is true drink. Whoever feeds on my flesh and drinks my blood abides in me, and I in him. How is it that Moses fasted for 40 days and 40 nights without food nor water? I tell you: they had their nourishment from the Father, from His Presence. So if we eat and drink Him, we are truly sustained. When we eat food in this world, we eat something that has died: either a plant that has been plucked, or an animal that has been slain, or fruit that has been harvested. Everything that is cut off from its life source undergoes death; after some time, food begins to rot and grow hairs of mold, being consumed from within, since it is not comsumed by the living in due time; but while it is on the tree, or in the ground, or has the breath of life within, it does not perish, for life preserves it. Therefore, if we live by eating the death of a thing, how much more do you suppose we would live eating the life of Christ, who is indeed Life? When an animal is sacrificed on the altar, they eat the meat of the sacrifice and gain strength for a short time; how much longer do you suppose we would live, who eat of the Sacrifice of God and the grace thereof, who eat the Lamb which has been slain, rather, which has been raised to everlasting life? If we know a person by their works, or a tree by its fruit, what type of tree do you discern resembles Jesus by His fruit, except the Tree of Life? As Moses prayed, Now therefore, I pray You, if I have found favor in Your sight, let me know Your ways that I may know You, so that I may find favor in Your sight. And by the fruit of Jesus Christ, we have been saved through the knowledge of Him, our Savior, who is to us the Tree of Life, which yields food imperishable; and to us the River of Living Water, being His Spirit, which is living drink. For they who eat the things of this world, eat death; but we who consume Christ, eat life! They who live in the things which are seen, which are passing away, likewise pass away within sight; but we who live in Him who is unseen, when we are no longer seen, will be found in Him, in eternal life, when He is revealed to all the world. For the things of this world which seem to be living, when put up alongside Him, are dead, for He is exceedingly alive. So we who sow to the Spirit will reap life, and the mind set on the Spirit is life and peace.

The testimony of communion is given so that we would not forget, but retain Christ in our heart until the end. For the holy days in the Law offered as reminders at the anniversary of an event; but communion is not given on a day, but any day; as it says, "Today if you hear His voice..." (Hebrews 3:15) And, "AT THE ACCEPTABLE TIME I LISTENED TO YOU, AND ON THE DAY OF SALVATION I HELPED YOU." Behold, now is "THE ACCEPTABLE TIME," behold, now is "THE DAY OF SALVATION" For His favor is available to us everyday, lest we harden ourselves toward God, being ignorant of His ways and erring in our heart. Remember His ways. Remember Christ, the Way. This is the key to reamin: remember. And the key to abiding: remembering. God's grace is a gift to us, but to the Father, costly. He gave up His Son; He lost Him for a short while; He gained Him back forever. Do not recieve God's grace in vain by forgetting these wonderful truths, but treat them precious and hold fast to them - even when faced with death, hold fast; for in vain it does not say, You have not yet resisted to bloodshed, striving against sin. And, And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony, and they did not love their lives to the death.

The Spirit and the Bride says, "Come!" All come! As it says, Come, everyone who thirsts, come to the waters; and he who has no money, come, buy and eat! Jesus, the Bronze Serpent

Then the Lord sent fiery serpents among the people and they bit the people, so that many people of Israel died. So the people came to Moses and said, “We have sinned, because we have spoken against the Lord and against you; intercede with the Lord, that He will remove the serpents from us.” And Moses interceded for the people. Then the Lord said to Moses, “Make a fiery serpent, and put it on a flag pole; and it shall come about, that everyone who is bitten, and looks at it, will live.” So Moses made a bronze serpent and put it on the flag pole; and it came about, that if a serpent bit someone, and he looked at the bronze serpent, he lived.

God sent fiery serpents to punish the people for their sins, then gave them provision through His grace so that they would be rescued from the sting of death. God did not remove the serpents like the people asked, but opened up a way by grace through which the people could believe in Moses' word and look upon the sepent in faith. It's through faith since they had to look at the serpent and believe that in doing so, they would be saved, hoping for what they did not yet have, namely salvation from the serpents. The serpent was made of bronze and so was the altar in the courtyard of God's tent, showing us that it's first through sacrifice we enter into God's sancturary; and there is no other way, but through sacrifice; as it is written, Just as Moses lifted up the snake in the desert, the Son of Man must also be lifted up. So that everyone who believes can have eternal life in him. God saved the Israelites from the venom of the serpents through the bronze image of that same serpent; that image is dead, but they had to hope and believe it would give life. They had to believe that dead image would give life; likewise, we believe by Christ's death, we will have life eternal - and not by His death alone, but rather, by His resurrected life, by the power of the Holy Spirit. For if Christ remained dead, we would be no better than a medium who consults the dead when we pray to Him; as it says, Should not a people inquire of their God? Why consult the dead on behalf of the living? Yet Christ, who was dead, is now alive; and we consult God through Him. And I beheld, and, lo, in the midst of the throne and of the four beasts, and in the midst of the elders, stood a Lamb as it had been slain, having seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God sent forth into all the earth. God commanded the people not to make an image to worship, but here, God commanded Moses to make an image of a fiery serpent, the same serpent in which the people feared because of its sting; but the representation is not the same as the thing it represents, for whenever an image is made, life is taken from it. So, in the same way, God saves us from the sting of death through the visible image of Himself, Jesus, through His death on a cross; for although in Him dwells the fullness of the Diety in bodily form, He emptied Himself for our sakes to free us from the one who has the power of death; as it says, Therefore, since the children share in flesh and blood, He Himself likewise also partook of the same, that through death He might render powerless him who had the power of death, that is, the devil, and might free those who through fear of death were subject to slavery all their lives. For although Christ was lifted up on a cross, He did not suffer defeat by that cross, but the serpent was defeated; for Christ was raised to new life, and the serpent suffered the head wound. So, this is why we see a serpent lifted up during the time of Israel's wanderings in the desert: for Christ was glorified through the cross, and satan defeated by the cross. We may be full of fearful to look upon Christ on the cross because of our great sin and God's wrath toward sin, but we must look and trust God that if we look upon Jesus Christ and Him crucified, we will be saved from sin and death and will surely live. Now, we will all die one day, but those who die in Christ sleep and will awake on the last day. Everyone dies; but He who looks upon Jesus through the eyes of faith, when he dies, he merely sleeps, because he is alive to God, having loved Him although not seeing Him physically; for it is not possible to hate God having seen the love of Him towards us through Christ; and the one who loves God is known by Him and all those who God knows are His children; such a one will be raised up on the last day to eternal life just as He has promised. The eyes of faith are sharpened through hearing God's Word; and our faith in Him unseen should be so strong that it's as if we have seen Him; as Paul said to the Galatians, For the meaning of Jesus Christ's death was made as clear to you as if you had seen a picture of his death on the cross. Jesus, the Son of God

Jesus is the Son of God; someone may ask, "What does that mean?" We have a proverb that says, "Like mother like daughter; like father like son." So it is with Jesus, the Son of God; He is like the Father God, and perfectly so; so much so, He said, Whoever has seen Me has seen the Father. How can you say, "Show us the Father"? Do you not believe that I am in the Father and the Father is in Me? The words I speak are not My own, but My Father who lives in Me does His work through Me. And, The Son is the visible image of the invisible God, the firstborn over all creation. And who is it that is called Everlasting Father? For a child will be born to us, a son will be given to us; And the government will rest on His shoulders; And His name will be called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Eternal Father, Prince of Peace. And what was the work the Father did in Christ? in Christ God was reconciling the world to himself, not counting their trespasses against them, and entrusting to us [apostles] the message of reconciliation.

He demonstrated His love for us on the cross, not while we were good people, but while we were yet sinners. For He is gracious to the ungrateful and the evil And, For He makes His sun rise on the evil and on the good, and sends rain on the just and on the unjust. If this is how God treats His enemies, how much more kindly does He deal with them who have been reconciled to Himself through the cross of Christ! By the mercies of Him, go to Him! Negligence will be wrath in the end. For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of His Son, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by His life.

When Abraham offered His son Isaac on the altar to God, by God's own command, not that this thought came from God, but the enemy planted it; as it says, And have built the high places of Baal to burn their sons in the fire as burnt offerings to Baal, which I did not command or decree, nor did it come into My mind. So the thought did not come from God, but the command did; all such thoughts come from the enemy, for God is good, and nothing evil ever crosses His mind. However, when Abraham offered His son Isaac on the altar in obedience, God used it to test Abraham and point to Christ Jesus to come; as it says, In your seed all the nations of the earth shall be blessed, because you have obeyed My voice. When it says, "in your seed", it does not say, "seeds", as with many, but one, which is Christ; so because of the obedience of one man, came a blessing to the world; and that blessing which was promised, came from Christ, the Seed; and Christ, being one, blesses the whole world by His one act of obedience. When Abraham was going to sacrifice his son, God provided another sacrifice in place of his son, a ram caught in the thicket; so Abraham received his son back from the dead; as it says, He [Abraham] considered that God is able to raise people even from the dead, from which he also received him [Isaac] back as a type. And Abraham, being a prophet, saw this was going to happen beforehand and said, God will provide for Himself the lamb for the burnt offering, my son. And, Stay here with the donkey, and I and the lad will go over there; and we will worship and return to you. Also thereafter, looking in the future to Christ, he said, Abraham called the name of that place The LORD Will Provide, as it is said to this day, "In the mount of the LORD it will be provided." And no-one took Christ's life, but He laid it down willingly, as Isaac did; Christ laid down His life as commanded by the Father. And it is not unrighteousness on God's behalf, for Christ was handed over to death for our sakes, once for all, as the propitiation for our sins. Everything Christ did was as if the Father was doing it through Him, for He does the commands of the Father perfectly, resembling His exact likeness.

Jacob had two sons from Rachel, but to him, Joseph was dead, although he was only in Egypt. But Jacob let Benjamin, his only beloved son, go into the hands of his brothers to save his family from the famine, for they were going to Egypt to buy food. God is not mocked; we will reap what we sow. For Jesus has loved us, even more than Himself, giving up Him body for us; so therefore, As obedient children, do not be conformed to the former lusts which were yours in your ignorance, but like the Holy One who called you, be holy yourselves also in all your behavior; because it is written, "YOU SHALL BE HOLY, FOR I AM HOLY." If you address as Father the One who impartially judges according to each one's work, conduct yourselves in fear during the time of your stay on earth; knowing that you were not redeemed with perishable things like silver or gold from your futile way of life inherited from your forefathers, but with precious blood, as of a lamb unblemished and spotless, the blood of Christ. Here is a comparison between sons and slaves.

He said to the people numerous times, "I came from the Father", "the Father and I are One". And when the time came, the very hour which He came for, He confirmed His testimony, even with His own blood; as prophesied by Job, And the indictment which my adversary has written, surely I would carry it on my shoulder, I would bind it to myself like a crown. For He was blameless, but the indictment which the adversary had written against Him, JESUS OF NAZARETH, THE KING OF THE JEWS And Christ bore on His shoulder the cross, and on His head He wore the crown of thorns; and He went to the cross, confirming His testimony in His own blood. This is what it means when it is said, "This is the one who came by water and blood. There are three that testify: the Spirit, the water, and the blood." (1 John 5:6-9) He was blameless, even Pilate could find fault in Him for he was determined to release Him, but the accusation they had against Him, He affirmed, even though it meant death. He testified, not with word only, but with deed: carrying the cross and wearing the crown of thorns. By the cross and the humiliation that He underwent, He confirmed to all what He said, not denying, but confirming. If anyone speaks lies, before the end of their life, they confess them, or if their life depends on it, they will put a stop to their lies; but Christ did not shrink back when faced with death, but testified to the truth; as Paul says, "and of Christ Jesus, who testified the good confession before Pontius Pilate." (1 Timothy 6:13) It was for the end of His life that He came; and by the cross, He affirms to all that He is the Son of God, confirming with His own blood; and it stands today as a witness to all, that Christ is the Son of God, transforming the cross, a device of torture, into a signal of salvation. For what was once an object of horror, has become a sign and symbol of redemption: it is now hung around necks, put on the tops of buildings, and pierced on ear lobes. Are the sons of men so wicked that they put tortuous symbols on themselves? No! Rather, God is so good that He has turned the destestable thing into a glorious sign of redemption and salvation. No-one can do such a thing, except God, who turns the corrupt person into the believing, the detestable into the gracious, the abominable into the saint, by His one act of love on the cross. Christ did so, so that we would not lose heart nor grow faint: believe in the Son. And do not fall into the trap of worshipping a dead cross, bowing low to it, but worship your Lord and Savior and God, Jesus, living in the heavens, exalted. Beware of idols.

Our strength will not be physical, but spiritual; it will not wax old or fade as does human strength. These are only my speculations; I do believe I have some wisdom.

Everlasting Life

We, being in the tent of our body, look forward to our permanent home and eternal life which God has promised to us in Christ Jesus. The tent is not a permanent home, but the tent is temporary and taken down one day; so it is with our bodies, but we look forward to our heavenly home and eternal life in our new bodies. When we are unclothed, we will be clothed with our heavenly dwelling and heavenly body. When you follow God, He makes you different, being in the midst of a crooked and perverse generation, you will stick out; nevertheless as the One who called you is holy, be holy. This is what's holiness: different, special, set apart. The world also has a word for this: weird. (They say it derogatorily) However, the world is from below, and we are from above, certainly we will be as aliens or foreigners to them, a strange and peculiar people; as the Spirit of God says in Hebrews, All these died in faith, without receiving the promises, but having seen them and having welcomed them from a distance, and having confessed that they were strangers and exiles on the earth. For those who say such things make it clear that they are seeking a country of their own. And indeed if they had been thinking of that country from which they went out, they would have had opportunity to return. But as it is, they desire a better country, that is, a heavenly one. Therefore God is not ashamed to be called their God; for He has prepared a city for them. And as Peter says, Beloved, I urge you as aliens and strangers to abstain from fleshly lusts which wage war against the soul. For this present life is not our home - we are only passing through for a short time as a foreigner - we have received the truth and have come to know the truth. There is a lie that our hearts deceive us with: Their inner thought is that their houses will last forever, Their dwelling places to all generations; They call their lands after their own names. But knowing the truth, we look forward to an everlasting dwelling which is beyond corruption and decay, which is new and will remain new forever.

Know that there is only one Way: that is Jesus Christ. There are many paths, but all those paths lead to an end which is dead - they are not eternal and do not go on forever. However, there is a Way which is eternal and the Way is narrow. The Way is Christ Jesus, and He has gone before us to be the first to be raised from the dead - and He lives forever more. This gives us assurance that if we live as He lived, we will also inherit the same life - we follow Him to receive the same reward, the same end. Those who believe in Him, also trust and follow Him; and those who follow Him, do as He did; and those who do as He did, will receive the reward He received. Those who do not pick up their cross and follow Him, are not worthy of Him (Matthew 16). Not many will follow after Him, but we are not like the many, but like the few that persist in faith unto the end; as it is written, Enter through the narrow gate. For wide is the gate and broad is the road that leads to destruction, and many enter through it. But small is the gate and narrow the road that leads to life, and only a few find it. And, For the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord. There is one life and the life is in the Son. If we be in the Son, then we have the life; if we be without the Son, we do not have the life. Live in God, abiding in Him until the end. Living in Him is life indeed.

This concept is similar to spiritual milk: milk is good when you're young, but when you are mature in Christ, you move onto solid food. Likewise, as children, we first learn how to walk; then as adults, to run. In the faith, we first walk; then as we walk in the light and gain confidence, we begin to run the race of faith. Walking may be easy, but when you begin to run, you're exerting a lot more effort. And those who run in a race, do so to win a crown in the end that will perish, but we run for a crown of everlasting life that will never perish. Let us really consider how hard it is to be saved, but how great the reward is to those who persevere until the end; as Peter admonishes, For it is time for judgment to begin with the family of God; and if it begins with us, what will the outcome be for those who disobey the gospel of God?
