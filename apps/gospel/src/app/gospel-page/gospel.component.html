The Danger - Futility

Futility of Pleasure

Alcohol and food, which are wholesome in themselves, which are given to us by God for our enjoyment, but often perverted into drunkenness and gluttony, and drugs, which are often used for an escape from reality, a false refuge, which are often corrupted for one's own enjoyment rather than medicine, and entertainment, which is vain, which is child's play, which wastes our short lives, and all other pleasures which come from our lust, which wages war against our soul, can never sustains us - they only mask our deeply rooted issues for a little while and make life bearable for a short time. They come quickly and vanish quickly; the same way they come, the same way they go. Is life so terrible that we need these? We've all had our share in these during some time in our life; and if it was not then, perhaps now. But where is the light of life? For the way we feel, living in such things, surely it is not life. Isn't life supposed to be good? Isn't death the bad? Yet our lives are not joyous, but are like death. Perhaps we don't know what life truly is. Living for pleasure is not life; our trouble may be gone for a moment, but it is not forever. It is like hitting the snooze button on an alarm clock - you may feel better now, but you will have to deal with the same problem later - but it has gotten worse. Even if we feel 'satisfied' or 'fulfilled', it is never enough; it is like we are feeding a beast within us - the more we feed it, the bigger and stronger it gets; it requires more and more of us - it is never satisfied. Even our gut growls like a dog when its not fed; how much more sin living within us which is a hound from hell? And sin is deathly, living in the sons of men; we are living, but sin is working death within us.

We eat food, but we are not content; we drink water, but we are not quenched; we eat and we are hungry again later; we drink and become thirsty again; as it says, All a man's labor is for his mouth and yet the appetite is not satisfied. And these, being for our enjoyment and nourishment, are corrupted into something it was never intended for. Food for the body, but God forbid for gluttony; drink to make the heart merry, but God forbid for drunkenness; sex for reproduction, but God forbid for immorality. Lust corrupts God given necessities into dissipation and waste: for over-eating is not of hunger, and drunkenness is not of thirst, and immorality is not of reproduction; all of these are for pleasure; and from wisdom it does not come; as it says, The mind of the wise is in the house of mourning, while the mind of fools is in the house of pleasure. So pleasure is not of wisdom; and seeking for pleasure is fleeting because it lasts only for a short while; but prudence makes the most of her time, and looks to the future. No matter how much the eye sees, it is not enough; no matter how much the ear hears, it is not content. If these things truly make us happy, why wouldn't we express it outwardly? If we are truly happy, shouldn't our happiness resemble a child's happiness? Children are overcome with laughter and express joyfulness and run around expressing their happiness, jumping with joy. If these things truly make us happy, why don't we show it? Most often we are chained by such things: we look miserable on the outside, but inwardly we become less miserable - these only mask our pain for a short while. In the beginning, we used to be happy, when sin wasn't alive, in the innocence of our childhood, but later, sin came alive and we died. All of us have become enslaved to our own desires which corrupt - for lust corrupts. But who is exempt from this? None of us. We are all under sin. And we are lead by the hand of this world into a cycle of dependence; we think we need these things to keep us in check: "I don't feel normal without them", we say to ourselves. And I testify that I've been chained by many sins and pleasures for years of my life, and looking back at my experience, surely it was not life. Whatever a person is overcome by that he is also enslaved. And how weak are we when something inaminate chains us! "I serve this, and that, and I can't stop!" Whatever we offer ourselves to as obedient servants, by that, we are enslaved. And I was enslaved to everything a man could be ensalved by: food, alcohol, drugs, money, sex, and many other things. These things are not alive, but somehow I was enslaved to them as if they were; they are not gods at all, so why should I serve them? I must do for them, but what are they doing for me? I wouldn't call myself a man, for I can't get out. I want out, but I can't go out; I am surely in a jail cell. I want to stop sinning, but I can't; I want to be released, but I can't redeem myself. Hope, I have none. "Where is real pleasure and satisfaction?" And worse yet, I didn't know how to utter it; for perhaps if I could tell someone, they could help. But instead, guilt and shame keep me from sharing, as if I am the only one dealing with such things without anyone to relate. Solomon, who had great wisdom from God, writes, All things are full of weariness; a man cannot utter it; the eye is not satisfied with seeing, nor the ear filled with hearing. And so it was with me, I couldn't utter. I have sinned and perverted what is right, And it is not proper for me. He has redeemed my soul from going to the pit, And my life shall see the light.

Solomon in his wisdom searched out pleasure, and said, I said in my heart, “Come now, I will test you with pleasure; enjoy yourself.” But behold, this also was vanity. I said of laughter, “It is mad,” and of pleasure, “What use is it?” I searched with my heart how to cheer my body with wine—my heart still guiding me with wisdom—and how to lay hold on folly, till I might see what was good for the children of man to do under heaven during the few days of their life. (...) And whatever my eyes desired I did not keep from them. I kept my heart from no pleasure, for my heart found pleasure in all my toil, and this was my reward for all my toil. Then I considered all that my hands had done and the toil I had expended in doing it, and behold, all was vanity and a striving after wind, and there was nothing to be gained under the sun. "My heart still guiding me with wisdom", for wisdom is insight: speaking the things which are from within with the correct words; and Solomon's words are insightful; and wisdom originates from the fear of God. And every sinful habit originates with lust; and lust entices the eyes. As he says, "And whatever my eyes desired I did not keep from them." All sin begins here; and the end: "And behold, all was vanity and a striving after wind." So by the things of this world, no man will be satisfied. Even by images, which are copies, men have tried to find satisfaction. But if we aren't satisfied with the real, then why would we be with an image, a fake? But rather, images stimulate more lust, for an image portrays something greater than reality. And how computers stimulate desire through pictures! It is satisfying while on it, but afterwards it's like waking from a sleep. We live in an age where media screens are everywhere: it started with televisions in the living room, then escalated to monitors in the pocket. Surely these feed our eyes. This is what I meant when I said, "We are led by the hand of this world", for who creates advertisements? Who makes ready the fulfillment of our desires? But still, the desire belongs to us and we will be the ones who suffer for indulging it. We should not long for the things of the world, but the One who gives all good things is God. Want further proof that we are dissatisfied and do not know it? A computer can create entire simulations for living a new life inside. If life were so good, then why does it exist? Can a copy ever be as good as the real? Which do you like better: name brand goods or knock offs? Yes, knock offs are cheaper and require less of us, but they are not better, even in the slightest. In the same way, these simulations are cheaper: you can sit on the couch and be at a sports game! But it is certainly not better. So lust shows itself as something great, but is lesser; it corrupts the natural into something seemingly more; and fulfills the desire with the real, not with what it is portraying. Desire is like an advertisement image: and does the image ever portray true reality? The artist makes it better than reality by skill, but it is not the true norm. And these images are fantasy, imagination, from one's mind; and so is lust. Lustful desire is not reality, for when something is desired, it is made great in one's mind, but it remains the same in reality. On the television screen men can put their imaginations so that you can say, "It must be, for I have seen it!" But it is not; it is trickery. It is merely an image someone made in their mind, in their imagination, making it visible for you to see; for it is what they want you to see and what they don't want you to see; and these images, which are from imaginations, entice lustful desire in those who see them. For why would someone make an image unless it is to be gazed at? So much so, coveteousness is called idolitry; and coveteousness is lust.

The natural use is better than the unnatural; the real is better than the copy; good is better than evil, even in the meanwhile if you think, "Ah! But it feels so good", in the end you will not be thinking that! Everything we sow, we will reap. A person does not sow an apple and get an orange tree; neither does a person breed pigs and get cows; so why do we think, "I will sow corruption and get life"? Lust reaps corruption; and righteousness reaps blessing. Everything corrupts through lust; and lust conceived gives birth to sin. All sin is dysfunction; and a dysfunction is useless. And what is useless is put into a pit and awaits burning. A new sofa will become fettered, a new car will become a tin of rust, even our bodies are becoming old and gray and worn out - it will be soon in the grave. Evil will inherit evil and goodness will inherit goodness. This is true, even if in the meantime we see the wicked prospering and the righteous suffering; as it says, I have seen everything during my lifetime of futility; there is a righteous man who perishes in his righteousness and there is a wicked man who prolongs his life in his wickedness. And, There is futility which is done on the earth, that is, there are righteous men to whom it happens according to the deeds of the wicked. On the other hand, there are evil men to whom it happens according to the deeds of the righteous. I say that this too is futility. But, I said to myself, "God will judge both the righteous man and the wicked man," for a time for every matter and for every deed is there. Everyone will get what their deeds deserve in the end because God will cause His righteous judgment to be upheld. So take heed! But she who lives in pleasure is dead while she lives. If we live for pleasure, our life is waste: which is dissipation. Instead, fear God. Enjoy what God has given you, without grumbling or complaining; as it says, So I commended pleasure, for there is nothing good for a man under the sun except to eat and to drink and to be merry, and this will stand by him in his toils throughout the days of his life which God has given him under the sun. And, I know that there is nothing better for them than to rejoice and to do good in one's lifetime; moreover, that every man who eats and drinks sees good in all his labor - it is the gift of God.

Futility in Toil

Our lives are filled with work and toil: we work, sleep, and do it all over again. Our days are short, yet we spend them in much pain and agony because of our labors. And what drives us? I have seen that every labor and every skill which is done is the result of rivalry between a man and his neighbor. This too is vanity and striving after wind. So we work for envy which will not matter in the future; we work for the sake of man that perishes; we work for objectives that are not ours, for fruit that we will not eat of. We leave the fruit of our labors to another who comes after us. The things we do will not matter, even years from now, it will become old and outdated. In the future, people will look back and say, "Who are they?", just as we presently do not care who were in the past, so it will be in the future. Even if we think, "I care", what does your boasting accomplish? No-one can bring back a day which has been spent. Our constant striving and labor, yet we don't receive our due wages; he who does little work, harvests much; and we are dumb-founded. We work hard for the holes in our pockets. We plant, and the locusts eat; we plant much, and gather very little. It is a grievous task: And I set my mind to seek and explore by wisdom concerning all that has been done under heaven. It is a grievous task which God has given to the sons of men to be afflicted with. I have seen all the works which have been done under the sun, and behold, all is vanity and striving after wind. And, There was a certain man without a dependent, having neither a son nor a brother, yet there was no end to all his labor. Indeed, his eyes were not satisfied with riches and he never asked, "And for whom am I laboring and depriving myself of pleasure?" This too is vanity and it is a grievous task.

We were born into travail, born for trouble: "For man is born for trouble, As sparks fly upward." (Job 5:7) All our days are stricken with evil. We don't know where we are going; and we don't remember where we come from; we aren't fully aware of even today - we're in a box without the ability to look out. And even if we had 1000 lives of 1000 years, we wouldn't be able to discover all that is before us - how much less the things we don't see? We chase things we think will satisfy us, but we end up forever chasing them even when we catch them. Are we any better than the animals? They come in futility and leave in futility; as it says, For what happens to the sons of men also happens to animals; one thing befalls them: as one dies, so dies the other. Surely, they all have one breath; man has no advantage over animals, for all is vanity. All living creatures die; what lives forever? Everything we work for, we do not keep; we strive for beauty, but it is fading away. Our possessions become the possessions of another. Even our own lives are borrowed, for we will return to the dust. The King of Israel, Solomon, wrote toward the end of his life, I hated all my toil in which I toil under the sun, seeing that I must leave it to the man who will come after me, and who knows whether he will be wise or a fool? Yet he will be master of all for which I toiled and used my wisdom under the sun. This also is vanity. So I turned about and gave my heart up to despair over all the toil of my labors under the sun, because sometimes a person who has toiled with wisdom and knowledge and skill must leave everything to be enjoyed by someone who did not toil for it. This also is vanity and a great evil. What has a man from all the toil and striving of heart with which he toils beneath the sun? For all his days are full of sorrow, and his work is a vexation. Even in the night his heart does not rest. This also is vanity. Futility is worthlessness; it is emptiness, aimlessness, not having a point or direction, wandering about in blackest darkness. It does not see; it is always doing much, but accomplishing little, speaking much, but getting nothing done, dreaming, but living as a passing shadow. What then, do we despair? But isn't that too vanity?

Life is short, and feels long. In life, we despair, without it, there is no hope either. We spend our lives wasting on things that will pass away. We are passing away. In the place of the dead, where we are going, there is nothing: no toil, no labor, no speech, no sight, nothing. We have inherited this life, not because we asked for it, for we did not ask even to be born, but because of the sin of Adam in the Garden, we have been given this kind of life. All people on earth come from him and we inherit sin and death from him; he transgressed the commandment, and judgment was pronounced on the earth because of him: Cursed is the ground because of you; In toil you will eat of it all the days of your life. Both thorns and thistles it shall grow for you; And you will eat the plants of the field; By the sweat of your face You will eat bread, Till you return to the ground, Because from it you were taken; For you are dust, And to dust you shall return. And the LORD God says, See now that I, I am He, And there is no god besides Me; It is I who put to death and give life. I have wounded and it is I who heal, And there is no one who can deliver from My hand. And, I am the LORD, and there is no other; Besides Me there is no God. (...) The One forming light and creating darkness, Causing well-being and creating calamity; I am the LORD who does all these. So who are we to quarrel with our Maker? If we speak to Him, would He answer? If He calls to us, we must answer. But rather, There is nothing better for a man than to eat and drink and tell himself that his labor is good. This also I have seen that it is from the hand of God. For who can eat and who can have enjoyment without Him? For to a person who is good in His sight He has given wisdom and knowledge and joy, while to the sinner He has given the task of gathering and collecting so that he may give to one who is good in God's sight. This too is vanity and striving after wind. And, One hand full of rest is better than two fists full of labor and striving after wind.

Therefore, it is better to accept your lot in life: be happy with it. Search and seek for Him; acknowledge your futility and search and seek for fullness in God. As the Lord promised, You will seek Me and find Me when you search for Me with all your heart. Be not happy with your futility, but search for answers. Do not follow the example of the disobedient who love their futility. See how they try to fight against God, trying to reverse the curses that God has pronounced on the earth? Thinking their life is forever, they fall into error; thinking their home is forever, they believe a lie. See how the world tries to reverse things: God gave woman pain in child-bearing, but they give numbing shots; God made woman desire for her husband, yet he rules over her, but they start feminist movements; God cursed the ground so that in toil it will produce its fruit, but they create technology that works at the push of a button; God makes man to return to the dust in old age, but they have beauty products and wrinkling agents and plastic surgery; but whatever we try to do, we cannot reverse death: all of us will return to Him in the end, slave and free, rich and poor.

Futility of Wealth

There was a man who went into a pub with everything he owned. He sat down at the gambling table to gamble. At first, he bets only a little of what he has. He rolls the dice and a miracle happens! All the dice line up. The man yells, "Today is my lucky day. I will bet more next time!" So he rolls again, but this time with more on the table. A miracle happens again! All the dice line up. The man continues this over and over and everytime he wins; eventually, everything he owned was on the table, even the shirt off his back. He thought to himself, "Surely I have rolled these dice over and over and they haven't failed me." However, the dealer seen the man and thought to himself, "You fool! One day these dice will not line up and everything you own will be mine, for I have worked a long time in this pub and I see it everytime - the dice will one day not line up, and everything you have will be mine." Sure enough, what the man said came true and the day came when the dice did not line up and everything on the table was taken from him. And because the man didn't have enough money for even a beer, he was cast out of the pub into darkness where he wept because everything he owned was gone. And so it is with every man who trusts in his life and loves his possessions; for, There is an evil which I have seen under the sun and it is prevalent among men— a man to whom God has given riches and wealth and honor so that his soul lacks nothing of all that he desires; yet God has not empowered him to eat from them, for a foreigner enjoys them. This is vanity and a severe affliction.

Solomon wrote, He who loves money will not be satisfied with money, nor he who loves wealth with his income; this also is vanity. When goods increase, they increase who eat them, and what advantage has their owner but to see them with his eyes? Sweet is the sleep of a laborer, whether he eats little or much, but the full stomach of the rich will not let him sleep. There is a grievous evil that I have seen under the sun: riches were kept by their owner to his hurt, and those riches were lost in a bad venture. And he is father of a son, but he has nothing in his hand. As he came from his mother's womb he shall go again, naked as he came, and shall take nothing for his toil that he may carry away in his hand. This also is a grievous evil: just as he came, so shall he go, and what gain is there to him who toils for the wind? Moreover, all his days he eats in darkness in much vexation and sickness and anger.

Is there anything we can take with us when we die? Not even our body goes with us! For the body goes back to the dust from which it came; as it is written, Then the LORD God formed man of dust from the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living being. And, Then the dust will return to the earth as it was, and the spirit will return to God who gave it. When we return to Him, it will be as a single moment to Him, but a lifetime to us. Everything we think we have, we don't have - we leave it to someone who comes after us, him who did not work nor toil for it. And likewise, that person will leave it behind as well. So what do any of us really own? As it says in truth, The earth is the LORD's and the fullness thereof, the world and those who dwell therein. We are merely stewards. Riches do not deliver on the day of wrath; and all the silver and gold and costly stones cannot forgive even a single sin; as it says, No man can by any means redeem his brother Or give to God a ransom for him — For the redemption of his soul is costly, And he should cease trying forever — That he should live on eternally, That he should not undergo decay. As it says, "He should cease trying forever", for that which is costly is freely given by God, and that, to humiliate the rich. For God is gracious, He gives freely what is most costly; He who owns everything and gives to everyone, breath and life and all things, continues in giving. So amassing riches brings pain to oneself, and trusting in them is a futile attempt for saftey. Many pierce themselves with many sins and sorrows chasing after riches deceitful; as it says, Like the partridge that gathers a brood that she did not hatch, so is he who gets riches but not by justice; in the midst of his days they will leave him, and at his end he will be a fool. And, There is a grievous evil that I have seen under the sun: riches were kept by their owner to his hurt, and those riches were lost in a bad venture. So the love of money is truly a snare among us; as it says, For the love of money is a root of all sorts of evil, and some by longing for it have wandered away from the faith and pierced themselves with many griefs. And being rich to oneself, or keeping one's fat, by not sharing with others, displeases God; this is not the nature of God, for He does not store up for Himself, but freely gives - for He is generous, even to the disobedient and unthankful. And who do we really hurt by being rich towards ourselves only? If we give, it is to the benefit of others; if we withhold, it is to the hurt of others; and even yet, we bring harm to ourselves by being greedy for gain, bringing about the judgment of God; as it says, Your gold and silver have corroded, and their corrosion will be evidence against you and will eat your flesh like fire. You have laid up treasure in the last days. Behold, the wages of the laborers who mowed your fields, which you kept back by fraud, are crying out against you, and the cries of the harvesters have reached the ears of the Lord of hosts. You have lived on the earth in luxury and in self-indulgence. You have fattened your hearts in a day of slaughter.

Rather, be rich toward God; as it says, And I will say to my soul, “Soul, you have ample goods laid up for many years; relax, eat, drink, be merry.” But God said to him, "Fool! This night your soul is required of you, and the things you have prepared, whose will they be?" So is the one who lays up treasure for himself and is not rich toward God. For when an animal offering was made, the fat of the offering was burnt up to God, so the fat is always the LORD's. And what we have in excess is fat. Judge for yourselves: is it good to see the poor suffering while indulging yourself? Listen to the parable and fear, And a poor man named Lazarus was laid at his gate, covered with sores, and longing to be fed with the crumbs which were falling from the rich man's table; besides, even the dogs were coming and licking his sores. Now the poor man died and was carried away by the angels to Abraham's bosom; and the rich man also died and was buried. In Hades he lifted up his eyes, being in torment, and saw Abraham far away and Lazarus in his bosom. And he cried out and said, 'Father Abraham, have mercy on me, and send Lazarus so that he may dip the tip of his finger in water and cool off my tongue, for I am in agony in this flame.' But Abraham said, 'Child, remember that during your life you received your good things, and likewise Lazarus bad things; but now he is being comforted here, and you are in agony. And besides all this, between us and you there is a great chasm fixed, so that those who wish to come over from here to you will not be able, and that none may cross over from there to us.' And he said, 'Then I beg you, father, that you send him to my father's house— for I have five brothers—in order that he may warn them, so that they will not also come to this place of torment.' But Abraham said, 'They have Moses and the Prophets; let them hear them.' But he said, 'No, father Abraham, but if someone goes to them from the dead, they will repent!' But he said to him, 'If they do not listen to Moses and the Prophets, they will not be persuaded even if someone rises from the dead.'

Instead, seek the gift of God, the reward of righteousness; as it says, Furthermore, as for every man to whom God has given riches and wealth, He has also empowered him to eat from them and to receive his reward and rejoice in his labor; this is the gift of God. For he will not often consider the years of his life, because God keeps him occupied with the gladness of his heart.

Futility in Death

There is a man who lives in a tent. All his days are spent in constant agony since the tent is not comfortable: the flies bite, the sun beams, the moon strikes. Nevertheless, he hoped forward to his permanent home which was far away in a distant land being prepared for him. Night and day he longed for it and thought about it, dreaming of it. After many, many years, his home was ready, and his tent tore, so he went away to his home in which he hoped for. The tent was destroyed; the man went to his home; he rejoiced and spent the rest of his days there forever-more. For man goes to his eternal home while mourners go about in the street.

We have this present life, but it is not ours to keep; if our life is for eternity, then we wouldn't have to die, but because we will die one day, we know this isn't our home. Our lives are entrusted to us for only a little while. Since we know we will die, we look forward to our next life: our permanent home. In the state we end will determine where we spend eternity. The time in the tent is a time of testing; and we always have two choices: good or evil. If there were only one choice, we wouldn't have free will - since we would be forced into choosing the only option - but there are two choices. We will reap what we sow; we gain what we have put in; we will be recompensed according to what we have done in our bodies. There is life after life, which is eternal; and there is death after death, which is the second death; the second death is hell, the lake of fire. Anyone whose name is not written in the Book of Life will die a second death, eternal shame and torment. Paradise is the Kingdom of God, the new Jerusalem, Mount Zion, the new heaven and the new earth, where righteousness dwells. The present heaven and earth are subjected to evil and violence; but all is passing away as this present world is passing away, as we ourselves are passing away. The new heaven and the new earth will never pass away; it is an eternal home which cannot be shaken. The LORD will judge the living and the dead according to their deeds, and recompense all according to what they have done, and will go to their permanent place. God's judgment will be based on righteousness - and who can argue? He will reward all who do good for the good done and repay harm for the harm done. For certain, God has sown everlasting life in the human heart so that we would covet life, but we still die; we don't want to go to hell, but we desire life, yet we are without knowledge; as it says, He has made everything appropriate in its time. He has also set eternity in their heart, yet so that man will not find out the work which God has done from the beginning even to the end. (...) I said to myself, “God will judge both the righteous man and the wicked man,” for a time for every matter and for every deed is there. I concluded that God is testing us, to show us that we are no better than animals. After all, the same fate awaits human beings and animals alike. One dies just like the other. They are both the same kind of creature. A human being is no better off than an animal, because life has no meaning for either. They are both going to the same place — the dust. They both came from it; they will both go back to it. How can anyone be sure that the human spirit goes upwards while an animal's spirit goes down into the ground? I have seen that nothing is better than that man should be happy in his activities, for that is his lot. For who will bring him to see what will occur after him?

In the state that we die is the same state that we will be raised: if a sinner dies in his sins, he will be raised a sinner and if a righteous man dies in his righteousness, he will be raised a righteous man. The LORD shows us through sleep what death is like: we sleep and hours have passed without us knowing. In sleep, we've accomplished nothing, we've done nothing, except rest; those whom the Lord is pleased, He teaches them in their sleep; while they dream in the night, He ministers in parables; but in the place of the dead, there is nothing. While we live, there is hope for repentance; in the place of the dead, there is no hope; as it says, Also, the hearts of the children of man are full of evil, and madness is in their hearts while they live, and after that they go to the dead. But he who is joined with all the living has hope, for a living dog is better than a dead lion. For the living know that they will die, but the dead know nothing, and they have no more reward, for the memory of them is forgotten. Their love and their hate and their envy have already perished, and forever they have no more share in all that is done under the sun. (...) Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with your might, for there is no work or thought or knowledge or wisdom in Sheol, to which you are going. We see generations of our descendants grow up before our eyes, yet at the prime of life, we leave and fly away. Not one of us chose our family; no-one chose the body in which they would be born; not one chose which country they would live, yea, no one even asked to be born! We are all given without a say, and it is all taken away without our gesture; as it does say, No man has authority to restrain the wind with the wind, or authority over the day of death; and there is no discharge in the time of war, and evil will not deliver those who practice it. And, Moreover, man does not know his time: like fish caught in a trecherous net and birds trapped in a snare, so the sons of men are ensnared at an evil time when it suddenly falls on them. We have all been dealt cards that cannot be taken back, we will all be judged based on what God has given us; and do we dare quarrel with our Maker? Woe to him who strives with Him who formed him, a pot among earthen pots! Does the clay say to him who forms it, ‘What are you making?’ or ‘Your work has no handles’? Woe to him who says to a father, ‘What are you begetting?’ or to a woman, ‘With what are you in labor?’ But rather, LORD, make me to know my end And what is the extent of my days; Let me know how transient I am. Behold, You have made my days as handbreadths, And my lifetime as nothing in Your sight; Surely every man at his best is a mere breath. And, So teach us to number our days, that we may present to You a heart of wisdom.

It is by God's grace that He gives us life. If we are dissatisfied with our cards, do we dare try to take them back? Knowing where they come from, can we take them back as goods bought in a store? Can we have a do-over in this life or trade our cards for someone else's? Surely God is just: wouldn't He show us a way to redemption and forgiveness? Does He not know that we are wayward, in need of a Shepherd? One day we will return our cards to the Dealer - for our hand is only for a short while. Shall we go all in for something that is temporary? But rather, for that which is everlasting! But where is the everlasting path? Where is the ancient road? Will someone show us the way to life? If there is a way, surely only God could reveal it.

The Danger - Sin

God is a Consuming Fire

We desire to do good with our minds, but with our bodies, we don't do what we intend to do. We see it every new year: we make a resolution and say, "This year will be nothing like the last, I am going to do this and do that!" And we do 'this' and 'that' very well for the first few months, but the time comes when we inevitably go back to our old ways. What we desire in our mind, we have not the strength in our flesh to carry out; in our mind we want to do good, but in our flesh, we don't do the good we want to do; we have good intentions, but the power of our sinful nature carries us away. If we did everything we wanted to do every new year, as time went on, we would be made more perfect; but in fact, we become more sinful, storing up our wrongs and being wronged as a result; we sin, piling up more transgression into our accounts, which is a debt against us. And who is omitted from such a thing? Who can say, "I have cleansed my heart, I am pure from my sin"? And, Indeed, there is not a righteous man on earth who continually does good and who never sins. Or even, What is man, that he should be pure, or he who is born of a woman, that he should be righteous? Behold, He puts no trust in His holy ones, and the heavens are not pure in His sight; how much less one who is detestable and corrupt, man, who drinks iniquity like water! And, Is there any number to His troops? And upon whom does His light not rise? How then can a man be just with God? Or how can he be clean who is born of a woman? If even the moon has no brightness and the stars are not pure in His sight, how much less man, that maggot, and the son of man, that worm! Or, as it says, If You, LORD, should mark iniquities, O Lord, who could stand? But there is forgiveness with You, That You may be feared. Yet someone may argue, "I am righteous, despite my unrighteousness. Is not God faithful to forget?" However, it says, "Without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness." (Hebrews 9:22) And another may say, "I am good always." But what does the law say? He who kidnaps a man, whether he sells him or he is found in his possession, shall surely be put to death. For the kidnapper tried to hide his crime under the cover of darkness, so his punishment is more severe, as if he murdered. Is that not what you do when you say, "I have not sinned"? Saying to yourself, "I have peace though I walk in the stubbornness of my heart in order to destroy the watered with the dry." (Deuteronomy 29:19) Will that person, even if hidden from the eyes of man for a time, escape the light of day? The moon sets and the sun rises - when he is caught, it is worse for that person than he who sins in the day and does not cover his transgression; as the Lord says, "[the Pharisees] who devour widows' houses, and for appearance's sake offer long prayers; these will receive greater condemnation." (Matthew 23:14, Mark 12:40, Luke 20:47) For they sought to deceive, appearing righteous before men, but God knew their hearts. Therefore, no-one is just in the sight of God, and we certainly cannot justify ourselves before Him; the cloak we cover ourselves with is too short; our lies surely cannot decieve God, can they?

We are in a great debt and God's anger burns against sin, for it corrupts what He has created; as it says, God is a just judge, And God is angry with the wicked every day. And, For our God is a consuming fire. As Israel seen with their own eyes, And to the eyes of the sons of Israel the appearance of the glory of the LORD was like a consuming fire on the mountain top. Someone might say, "Isn't the devil fire, but not God?" But it is the devil that masquerades himself as an angel of light - himself truly being darkness; but God, being fire and light, shrouds Himself in a dark cloud lest man see Him and die - for no one can see Him and live. The devil doesn't rule over hell, but God prepared hell for the devil and his angels; and He has prepared a place for us, a heavenly place, provided we do not follow demons, but God. For who we follow, we will also receive the same's end: demons unto hell or God unto Paradise. For who-ever we follow, we follow to a place, to an end, to a destination; and when we follow, we do the same things as him that we follow. But someone might say, "I only follow, but they are deceiving me." But, "A blind man cannot guide a blind man, can he? Will they not both fall into a pit?” (Luke 6:39) And, "They will bear the punishment of their iniquity; as the iniquity of the inquirer is, so the iniquity of the [false] prophet will be.” (Ezekiel 14:10) So all sin must be punished; and God is just, He cannot let sin go unpunished. But you still might say, "Sin in me causes me to do wrong, but I myself want to do good. How is God just in punishing me?" Sin is inseperable from the sinner; we are the ones to allow sin, inclining our hearts toward sin rather than to God; being led astray by our sinful desires, we err out of the way. But yet, If you say, "See, we did not know this," Does He not consider it who weighs the hearts? And does He not know it who keeps your soul? And will He not render to man according to his work? For God knows the heart of man, and He is kind and compassionate towards those who have acted in ignorance. All sin is against God - for sin is transgression against God's law, the written and the unwritten code. The law is written on the heart of man, and He knows what we know. Yet, this thing I know: God, the Most High, dwells with the lowly and contrite; He is rich, but He dwells with the poor; as He says through Isaiah, the prophet, But on this one will I look: On him who is poor and of a contrite spirit, And who trembles at My Word. And, What is desirable in a man is his kindness, And it is better to be a poor man than a liar. So lie not against the truth, but rather acknowledge your sins; be poor and lowly, and tremble.

God loves truth, justice, and righteousness; He hates evil, sin, and wrong-doing. He gives us His commands so that we would know what is good, and to walk in His ways - His directives surely direct us. God never changes: He is, was, and will be. He fills all, yet in all, nothing compares to Him; but there is one in all which He is like, namely, a consuming fire. This is the fear of the LORD: to abhor evil. If you love your life, you will fear the LORD. If you want wisdom, Solomon in His wisdom says, The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom. This is how we obtain wisdom, and also how we perfect wisdom: by fearing God. The more we fear Him, the wiser we become. If wisdom starts by the fear of the Lord, how much more is it perfected by the fear of the Lord? The wisest thing we can do: fear God; the wisest thing to do: get wisdom. Revere Him like you would a Father or a King, for He is the Father of fathers and the King of kings, the Ancient one, who establishes kingdoms and overthrows them. On the other hand, the one who does not fear God is foolish - for only a fool destroys himself. The left hand is the weaker of the two hands, so strive for what is right; just as light is better than darkness, so wisdom is better than folly. The one who is wise has eyes in his head, seeing, He walks in the day-time; a man that walks in darkness, does not know where he is going, such a one does not know his way back to the city - the eye of his understanding is darkened. Therefore, fear not the devil, nor demons, nor principalities, nor powers, but fear God.

God is the First and the Last; He has no beginning or end; He is the Beginning and the End; He lives from everlasting to everlasting; all things He has created. He alone has power to take up the sword and destroy, yet He gives life and relents in destruction - He is patient with the wicked everyday. He sets all things upright and gives all a habitation to live: He gives fish to the sea, birds to the air, and animals to the land. He created them and gives them their food; He doesn't create so that they would be in want, but everything He established, even from the beginning, so that all would be nurtured by Him, so that they would depend on Him, so that they would trust Him. If He does this with the animals, how much more we? Are we not more precious than many animals? He created once by wisdom, and all things have been established through wisdom; yet all things we presently see, are passing away - they will come to an end. The end has been mentioned from the beginning - all ages have spoken about the end in times past; but, The Lord is not slow about His promise, as some count slowness, but is patient toward you, not wishing for any to perish but for all to come to repentance. So God has shown us grace; He has given us time so that we would repent: and His goodness leads us to repentance. God is not taught good, He is good; God is not taught love, He is love; God is not taught anything, for He knows all things and all things come from Him. He is served by no-one, as if He needed anything, but He gives life and breath to all. He has power and authority; He does what He pleases - and He chooses to do good. He is Good, and outside of Him there is no goodness; in Him, there is the fullness of all goodness. Love abounds in Him, for He alone is Love. With love is jealousy, and God's jealousy is a fire; as it says, For you shall worship no other god, for the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God. And, For the Lord your God is a consuming fire, a jealous God. The LORD God shows us love in truth, and through truth, we find love: He does not love in word alone, but in deed and truth; as it says, For love is as strong as death, Jealousy is as severe as Sheol; Its flashes are flashes of fire, The very flame of the LORD. Many waters cannot quench love, Nor will rivers overflow it; If a man were to give all the riches of his house for love, It would be utterly despised. And, Or do you think that the Scripture speaks to no purpose: "He jealously desires the Spirit which He has made to dwell in us"?

Using a human example, if a husband loves his wife and his wife cheats with another man, he is going to have two strong emotions: anger and jealousy. Are these emotions evil? They can be, but are they in this case? They are powerful emotions; they come from his love for his wife. If he did not love his wife, there would be no reason to be angry or jealous; but because he loves his wife, Jealousy enrages a husband, and he will show no mercy in the day of vengeance. He will not be appeased by any ransom, or persuaded by lavish gifts. For, Wrath is fierce and anger is a flood, But who can stand before jealousy? Using another human example: a father has a child and if the child continually plays with dangerous things like snakes and knives; the father will discipline his child and start to hate the snakes and knives which cause his child's harm. Is the discipline done because the father hates his child? Of course not! In fact, the discipline is proof that he loves his child and the father considers him his own. If the father did not discipline his child, then the child would be considered illegitimate by him, unloved. Does not discipline purge the inmost folly? Discipline will not kill a child, but an undisciplined life will. Every loving father disciplines his child. So the father's hatred comes from his love for his child. Likewise, the LORD God, the Creator of us all, hates sin, because He loves you; and sin harms you.

Similarily, one who loves children will hate abortion; a man who loves truth hates lies. And the LORD God is Jealous; if we put anything else first in our lives above Him, tremble. Is anything greater than Him? Even if we esteem something highly, isn't He higher still? For He is the creator of all things; aren't all things from His hand? will we love them more than He? He loves us greatly; can't it be terrifying? As He says, They have made Me jealous with what is not God; They have provoked Me to anger with their idols. So I will make them jealous with those who are not a people; I will provoke them to anger with a foolish nation. And look: the LORD God smote the Israelites in His jealousy when they committed adultery with Baal, a god which is no god at all. And it is said of Phinehas, Phinehas the son of Eleazar, the son of Aaron the priest, has turned away My wrath from the sons of Israel in that he was jealous with My jealousy among them, so that I did not destroy the sons of Israel in My jealousy. And Jehu, who smote these false gods in the land of Israel with the jealousy of the Most High, with the zeal of God, was God's chosen instrument. Therefore, fear the LORD! Does not man fear fire? He respects its power, not feeding it too much wood, not getting too close. How much more should we fear God who is Creator of all, even creator of the burning fire of the sun, Himself being a consuming fire? How much less should we stoke the fire of God's fierce jealousy with idolitry? How much more should we stand at a distance, and make petitions from afar, hearing Him through another? As it says, All the people perceived the thunder and the lightning flashes and the sound of the trumpet and the moutain smoking; and when the people saw, they trembled and stood at a distance. Then they said to Moses, "Speak to us yourself and we will listen; but let not God speak to us, or we will die." If they feared death while hearing Him, and no-one can see Him and live, how much more should we fear the whisper of another who speaks in His name? If we may lose our eyes looking at the light of the sun, what do you suppose would happen if we beheld God? If they who looked into the ark of the LORD which bore His testimony, and were struck, how much more severer a judgment do you suppose if we beheld Him with our natural eyes? As it says, He struck down some of the men of Beth-shemesh because they had looked into the ark of the LORD. He struck down of all the people, 50,070 men, and the people mourned because the LORD had struck the people with a great slaughter. But now the way to see God is revealed, for we must see God through faith; and faith is seeing Him without the ability to see Him with our natural eyes, which is seeing Him with the eye of our understanding by hearing the Word of Him. All who see Him through faith will live and not die, where-as the ones who see Him naturally do die; so we see why faith is necessary, because He is Holy.

God Sees & Hears

God is living; He is the creator of all living things. He sees the thoughts and the intentions of the human heart; He is Spirit and sees not what man sees, but spiritual things; He sees the human heart, for it is spirit, even though it is carnal. When we ourselves do not know our motives and intentions, He knows them. We may think in our heart, "My motives are right; I will stand before Him and He will agree." Are our thoughts God's thoughts? Are our ways God's ways? As the heavens are higher than the earth, so are God's ways higher than our ways and His thoughts higher than our thoughts. What we think can never be so, unless we think with the mind of God, according to the Word of God. We can't even begin to think how He thinks, unless we know His Word. The heart of us is deceitfully wicked, and who can understand it? Will it not even deceive us, saying, "I can understand"? Our heart is always flattering us; as it says, Transgression speaks to the ungodly within his heart; There is no fear of God before his eyes. For it flatters him in his own eyes concerning the discovery of his iniquity and the hatred of it. If we're led by our own thoughts or our own understandings, we're in darkness, since our heart is deceitful, filled with iniquity, darkened because of the ignorance that is in us; and where there is deceit, there is no truth, there is no light. Our hearts are not trustworthy servants; as the apostle says, I do not even judge myself. For I am not aware of anything against myself, but I am not thereby acquitted. It is the Lord who judges me. Therefore do not pronounce judgment before the time, before the Lord comes, who will bring to light the things now hidden in darkness and will disclose the purposes of the heart. But rather, For has anyone said to God, 'I have borne chastisement; I will not offend anymore; Teach me what I do not see; If I have done iniquity, I will not do it again'?

God's Word is light, pure, refined as silver in a furance, even seven times; just as a sword slices, disclosing our hidden guts, sinews, and organs, by it's division of the flesh, so does God's Word expose our hidden motives and thoughts to the dividing asunder even of soul and spirit. We're an open scroll to God; He knows our words before we speak them, He knows our going up and our sitting down, nothing is hidden from His sight. I charge you to read Psalm 139, and see how David speaks by the Spirit about all these things; and in Hebrews it says, For the word of God is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing to the division of soul and of spirit, of joints and of marrow, and discerning the thoughts and intentions of the heart. And no creature is hidden from his sight, but all are naked and exposed to the eyes of him to whom we must give account. Imagine your thoughts being put on facebook, publicised for all to see - the things you think, on public display. You're afraid of a camera, which can only see in the light; if it's dark, you must first shine a light to capture video. A camera can only see the outside of a man - if you want to see the inside, you must swallow it. From afar, what is it? Up close, what could it be? Not so with they eyes of the Father God: darkness is as light to Him, darkness and the light are the same. He who created the outside, made the inside as well. He is near, and yet far away; He is seated in the heights - nothing escapes His notice. When you want to get a shot of the city, you must go high; but who is higher than the Most High? What is outside of His panorama? What can He not zoom into? A hair falling from the head, He sees; all the stars in the skies are numbered; He gives them a name. What can you truly disclose from Him? Everything hidden in you is public to Him. What is your secret that He doesn't already know? He who created the eye of a falcon, are His eyes not greater? The falcon can see even from great heights. He who created the owl to see in the dark, can He not likewise see? He who made it possible for x-rays to see the inside of man, can't He see even to your inmost spirit, having created it? He is not seen, yet He sees all.

Is there any place on earth that you can hide from God? Is there any place where He isn't? If we make our hiding place in the depths of the earth, He is there; if we make our homes in the west, even in the east, He is there. Does He not fill all things? Isn't His eyes everywhere? He is Creator of every eye of every beast, of every man and of every angel. When you think you're alone, looking out through your eyes, He is there. His mighty Cheribum are under His throne; they are covered with eyes on their wings, inside and out. God sends His angels out and calls them His Watchers, for they watch. He sees all, but He is not seen by any. God seen what was happening below on Mount Sinai while the people were building a golden calf and worshiping it - He seen before Moses did with his eyes or even heard the sound of a party in the camp. God seen where Jonah went to get away from Him. He seen Saul hiding among the baggage. He hears the cries of the mouths of the humble on earth, and He Himself is their Avenger; He seeks for those who seek Him; listen to what the Psalmist says, He who planted the ear, does he not hear? He who formed the eye, does he not see? He who disciplines the nations, does he not rebuke? He who teaches man knowledge — the LORD — knows the thoughts of man, that they are but a breath. God's sovereignty can be a great encouragement to us, but it could also be a great discomfort to us; for it is a most fearful thing if we're sinning. Who are we that He is mindful of us? As the man of God, Job, in his distress says, What is man that You magnify him, And that You are concerned about him, That You examine him every morning And try him every moment? Will You never turn Your gaze away from me, Nor let me alone until I swallow my spittle? But yet, God does not look at man as mankind looks, according to outward appearance - no. Our eyes see natural things, but God sees spiritual things, for He Himself is Spirit; everything spiritual is unseen, and the spiritual is what God sees, for He Himself is not seen. Everything hidden will be brought to the light; all things concealed will be revealed and made know to all. What only He sees, will be seen by all; no-one sees Him now, but then, He will be seen by all. The dead will rise and behold their Maker; and our hearts, which men do not see, will be revealed to all at His visitation; and that which is secret and silent will be known and proclaimed throughout, that which is invisible and covered will be clear and exposed. The things we presently see will pass away, but the things unseen will remain forever; and God's judgment is eternal; the judgment of the LORD is from inward to outward, from the heart to the works of the flesh; and in the end, only the spirit will remain and all that which can withstand the fire. So knowing the fear of the Lord, we grasp onto faith, cleansing our hearts thereby. For if a man gives to the poor, but for a pretense, to receive praise of men - will it be well with him? Does God not see it? Or the innocent man who is beaten under false accusation - will not God see that he is innocent, although by human sight he is condemned? Is this not what the apostle means when he says, And he who is physically uncircumcised, if he keeps the Law, will he not judge you who though having the letter and circumcision are a transgressor of the Law? For circumcision is outward and physical, but true circumcision is of the heart, by the Spirit.

God's judgment is always in righteousness and justice; and on that day, He will bring all into judgment - the living and the dead. The Lord's judgment is not short of being executed today either; as it says, If the righteous will be rewarded in the earth, How much more the wicked and the sinner! All of His judgements which happened beforehand are for our admonishment today, that we wouldn't follow their bad examples; as it says, Now these things happened as examples for us, so that we would not crave evil things as they also craved. Do not be idolaters, as some of them were; as it is written, "THE PEOPLE SAT DOWN TO EAT AND DRINK, AND STOOD UP TO PLAY." Nor let us act immorally, as some of them did, and twenty-three thousand fell in one day. Nor let us try the Lord, as some of them did, and were destroyed by the serpents. Nor grumble, as some of them did, and were destroyed by the destroyer. Now these things happened to them as an example, and they were written for our instruction, upon whom the ends of the ages have come. But this was in regard with His people; He also judged the whole world in times past in a flood; and this is what God saw when He judged the earth, during the time of Noah: The LORD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every intention of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually. And the LORD regretted that he had made man on the earth, and it grieved him to His heart. So we see that God saw their heart and their thoughts and their intentions. And if the mind is sinful, how much more their actions? For if the mind governs all the members of the body and the mind is sinful, then the entire body is also given over to wickedness. Likewise, it is said over and over that the kings of Israel, which are the head of Israel, made Israel to sin (1 Kings 14:16, 1 Kings 15:26, 1 Kings 16:2, 1 Kings 21:22), for the king is head of Israel and Israel is the body; if the head be wicked, then so is the body. If we were honest with ourselves, we would see that our thoughts are no better than the days of Noah; and it is no laughing matter, for the LORD God does not laugh at sin. It says, “The devising of folly is sin, And the scoffer is an abomination to men.” (Proverbs 24:9) So even our thoughts are sinful, if they are set on folly.

Our Sin Nature

Everyone of us is of a fallen nature. All of us are alike: we do evil. None of us continually does what we are supposed to do; and no one never sins; not one of us always does what is right. We all have fallen short of the glory of God; we have all missed the mark; as it is written, None is righteous, no, not one; no one understands; no one seeks for God. All have turned aside; together they have become worthless; no one does good, not even one. Their throat is an open grave; they use their tongues to deceive. The venom of asps is under their lips. Their mouth is full of curses and bitterness. Their feet are swift to shed blood; in their paths are ruin and misery, and the way of peace they have not known. There is no fear of God before their eyes. Even if we were to decide to do better today, it would not cover our past faults; even if we declared, "I will change", it would only take us as far as our human strength will go; and aren't we becoming weaker day by day? If our nature is to do evil, how can our evil nature reform itself? Wouldn't trying make us even worse? What we need does not come from ourselves, nor is within ourselves; as it says, “I know, O LORD, that a man's way is not in himself, Nor is it in a man who walks to direct his steps.” (Jeremiah 10:23)

A new born baby is precious and innocent, but is a baby good? We were all once infants, and how we are born is how we will be; if a donkey is born, it cannot later become a horse, can he? As the LORD God says, “Can the Ethiopian change his skin Or the leopard his spots? Then you also can do good Who are accustomed to doing evil.” (Jeremiah 13:23) If we were good, then we wouldn't need to be taught good; but we see that we need to be taught good from an early age. If we were all good, then we wouldn't need to be taught, because we would already be. We are inherently evil and we all do that evil in which we know by nature; we all know evil intrinsically, and without any effort we do it. We do not have any knowledge of God who is Good; if we knew God from the womb, then we would be good, but we are born into ignorance, without any knowledge, except that of evil. And goodness is not of ourselves, but of God; and He is who we need to be taught, which is contrary to our nature; for we are evil, but He is Good, we walk by sight, but He is not seen. So the knowledge of God is our good; as it says, Did not your father eat and drink and do justice and righteousness? Then it was well with him. He judged the cause of the poor and needy; then it was well. Is not this to know me? declares the LORD. And, Beloved, do not imitate evil but imitate good. Whoever does good is from God; whoever does evil has not seen God. And without God, it's impossible to be good; as it says, “Without faith it is impossible to please Him, for he who comes to God must believe that He is and that He is a rewarder of those who seek Him.” (Hebrews 11:6) And, “For I know that nothing good dwells in me, that is, in my flesh; for the willing is present in me, but the doing of the good is not.” (Romans 7:18) “And those who are in the flesh cannot please God.” (Romans 8:8) And this flesh is our nature from the womb. We have all inherited wickedness from one man - that is Adam; we have inherited our evil nature from the first man. In the Garden, he sinned against God, eating the fruit of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, which was against the commandment of the LORD; so since one man sinned one time, all became sinners; and from sin, everyone dies as a result, even them who do not sin in the same way Adam did. And someone might say, " Injustice! If I were there, I would have obeyed." But such thinking is not wise, because it does not change anything.

Do we need further testimony about us, that is, our nature? As a child, we were taught good; and without question, we are only taught the things that we don't already know; and we already know evil from the womb, so we don't need to be taught this; we didn't need to learn evil, but what is good. If we were good, then why is self-discipline a thing? Is it against the law to love so much? Or against the command, "Do not do so much good"? But rather, discipline is needed for mastering over evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, fornications, thefts, false witness, slanders; these being evil need to be controlled; that is why self-discipline exists, and it is unto us a wall of protection; as it says, “Like a city that is broken into and without walls Is a man who has no control over his spirit.” (Proverbs 25:28) For this is our nature: evil. It's effortless for us to do bad and takes all our energy to do good and resist evil. We love wickedness and eat it up like bread; we eat, sleep and drink in iniquity. Even when we grow old and are not taught good, our parents are blamed. But why? People say, "I blame the parents for the child's behavior." And "Why didn't his parents teach him what is right?" So, we testify to ourselves about the nature we belong to, belonging to evil. For why would our parents be blamed if we were born righteous? In that case, our actions would be on our own head! Even more we say, "I am only human." So we testify to ourselves and to others that we belong to a nature that is fallen and broken. It is our nature; just as it's an eagle's nature to fly or a penguin's nature not to fly. We are sinful; and we are incapable of changing or reforming ourselves. If anyone claims they are not sinful, they show how sinful they really are, for they lie in regard to an obvious fact; as David said in a Psalm, Surely I was sinful at birth, sinful from the time my mother conceived me. Surely you desire truth in the inner parts; you teach me wisdom in the inmost place. Cleanse me with hyssop, and I will be clean; wash me, and I will be whiter than snow. Let me hear joy and gladness; let the bones you have crushed rejoice. And if that comes from King David, how much more we? We may think we are better than others by comparing ourselves with ourselves or measuring ourselves by ourselves, but we are without understanding. We think, "I am not in prison; I haven't killed anybody." Or even some might say, "I would never do such a thing!" The only difference between a prisoner and you, you weren't caught - for you know you've done things worthy of imprisonment. You who condemn theives, have you pirated movies? You who judge murderers, have you been angry and struck someone? Therefore you have no excuse, everyone of you who passes judgment, for in that which you judge another, you condemn yourself; for you who judge practice the same things. And we know that the judgment of God rightly falls upon those who practice such things. But do you suppose this, O man, when you pass judgment on those who practice such things and do the same yourself, that you will escape the judgment of God? What then? Are we better than they? Not at all; for we are all under sin; and the one who breaks one law has broken them all; as it says, For whoever keeps the whole law and yet stumbles in one point, he has become guilty of all. For He who said, "DO NOT COMMIT ADULTERY," also said, "DO NOT COMMIT MURDER." Now if you do not commit adultery, but do commit murder, you have become a transgressor of the law. So speak and so act as those who are to be judged by the law of liberty.

And sin is a living power within us that causes death - it's what makes us die in the end. It even has effect on all creation, for everything wastes away because of it, and even the animals die. When people think about sin, they think about an instance of disobedience; which is true, but it's also a power and a nature and a law. It's what causes us to be weak, it's what causes our muscles to ache with agony, it's what causes everything to decay and wear out. We can see what sin looks like by looking at death: it's gross, repugnant, a horrid stench, defiling all that is near. God shows us a tangible result of sin: death. If sin looks like this to our eyes, how much worse in the eyes of the living God? And it lives within us! It's a cancer that does not submit to the control of the mind, but spreads uncontrollably; it is without law; it's lawlessness which does not obey anyone; it's a hazard; it constantly does wrong. This thing causing death within us needs to be put to death, for it is killing us. It masters over us and never submits to our will. It enslaves us; it is hostile towards God; it's acts are acts of war against God, provoking Him to anger; it's thoughts are wicked; it girds up it's loins to haste after evil; it has never submitted to the law of God, for it is not able to do so and never will; those who are of the flesh can never please God. In our minds, we want to do good, but in the members of our flesh, we don't do the good in which we intend to do - because of sin. It does not submit to our direction; what an unruly animal it is! This nature is best spoken by the wisdom of the Spirit of God in the apostle Paul; and listen to what he has to say, The trouble is not with the law, for it is spiritual and good. The trouble is with me, for I am all too human, a slave to sin. I don’t really understand myself, for I want to do what is right, but I don’t do it. Instead, I do what I hate. But if I know that what I am doing is wrong, this shows that I agree that the law is good. So I am not the one doing wrong; it is sin living in me that does it. And I know that nothing good lives in me, that is, in my sinful nature. I want to do what is right, but I can’t. I want to do what is good, but I don’t. I don’t want to do what is wrong, but I do it anyway. But if I do what I don’t want to do, I am not really the one doing wrong; it is sin living in me that does it. I have discovered this principle of life-—that when I want to do what is right, I inevitably do what is wrong. I love God’s law with all my heart. But there is another power within me that is at war with my mind. This power makes me a slave to the sin that is still within me. Oh, what a miserable person I am! Who will free us from this life that is dominated by sin and death?

The Day of Judgement

Just as there was a world-wide judgment in Noah's day, so there will be at the end of time. If that judgment came with water, then how will it be in the end except with fire? For it will be like the time of Lot in Sodom, when the Lord will come from the heavens, it will be like Noah in the time of antiquity; as Peter says, For they (scoffers) deliberately overlook this fact, that the heavens existed long ago, and the earth was formed out of water and through water by the word of God, and that by means of these the world that then existed was deluged with water and perished. But by the same word the heavens and earth that now exist are stored up for fire, being kept until the day of judgment and destruction of the ungodly. But do not overlook this one fact, beloved, that with the Lord one day is as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day. The Lord is not slow to fulfill his promise as some count slowness, but is patient toward you, not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance. In that day, everything done in our body will be accounted for on that Day. God is just; and He is just such that He cannot let anyone go unpunished. Evil cannot persist, nor can a single lie. Every evil thing will be made right through judgment; as it is written, I will discipline you, but with justice; I cannot let you go unpunished. If He let just one sin go unpunished, He wouldn't be good and just. It is a good thing to judge evil or a lie: that's called justice. When justice is not upheld, people roit, because it is evil not to judge wrong-doing; it is right to render justice; it is good to do justice and show no partiality; as it says, Because the sentence against an evil deed is not executed quickly, therefore the hearts of the sons of men among them are given fully to do evil. The Lord God is Judge. And He will make all to rise from their grave, the living and the dead, and they will stand before Him on that great and terrible day. A day of wrath is that day, A day of trouble and distress, A day of destruction and desolation, A day of darkness and gloom, A day of clouds and thick darkness, A day of trumpet and battle cry Against the fortified cities And the high corner towers. I will bring distress on men So that they will walk like the blind, Because they have sinned against the LORD; And their blood will be poured out like dust And their flesh like dung. Neither their silver nor their gold Will be able to deliver them On the day of the LORD'S wrath; And all the earth will be devoured In the fire of His jealousy, For He will make a complete end, Indeed a terrifying one, Of all the inhabitants of the earth. And, Alas, you who are longing for the day of the LORD, For what purpose will the day of the LORD be to you? It will be darkness and not light; As when a man flees from a lion And a bear meets him, Or goes home, leans his hand against the wall And a snake bites him. Will not the day of the LORD be darkness instead of light, Even gloom with no brightness in it? All evil and all lies will be made right, and it is made right through judgment; wickedness is put in it's proper place and judged as being wrong; and righteousness will be exalted and rewarded. When evil is reprimanded, reproved, and rebuked it is made good through correction; and our God is of justice and righteousness. Right now is the time to receive correction and forgiveness, but in that day, judgment: condemnation or reward. If You, LORD, should mark iniquities, O Lord, who could stand? But there is forgiveness with You, That You may be feared.

Judgement is not merely in word, but in action as well; the result of judgment is either condemnation or praise; and to compress the lips is to allow evil to pass, to allow injustice; if sin goes uncorrected, it persists, and corrupts much that is good. Men riot when justice is not upheld; when someone who is to be punished gets off free, men go out and riot against the judge and police; they say, "They're no good! We want justice. The police aren't upholding it!" So we know it's good to render justice, for we ourselves want justice; but when it comes to our wrongs, we want mercy; however it says, So speak and so act as those who are to be judged by the law of liberty. For judgment will be merciless to one who has shown no mercy; mercy triumphs over judgment. For God's righteous judgment rewards all according to their works: to the one who judges, they will be judged, and to the one who shows mercy, they will be shown mercy. As it says, "For the day of the LORD draws near on all the nations. As you have done, it will be done to you. Your dealings will return on your own head. Because just as you drank on My holy mountain, All the nations will drink continually. They will drink and swallow And become as if they had never existed.” (Obadiah 1:15-16) God is not like any judge on earth - He is perfect. He will reward all with righteous judgment on that day when the judgment of God is revealed through the One He has appointed; He will reward those who by persistence seek immortality, life and peace with everlasting life; “but to those who are selfishly ambitious and do not obey the truth, but obey unrighteousness, wrath and indignation.” (Romans 2:8) And the apostle Peter testifies that even the righteous will scarcely be saved; as he says, For it is time for judgment to begin at the household of God; and if it begins with us, what will be the outcome for those who do not obey the gospel of God? AND IF IT IS WITH DIFFICULTY THAT THE RIGHTEOUS IS SAVED, WHAT WILL BECOME OF THE GODLESS MAN AND THE SINNER? If we cannot withstand the judgment of mere mortals, how can we withstand the judgment of the Almighty God, or even His Son, whose eyes are flames of fire?

So then, I ask, if we are sinful, as we are, and God sees and hears all, as He does, and He will not let anyone go unpunished, how then can anyone be saved? The Scriptures say, "few will be saved", but shouldn't there be none? As it says, Enter by the narrow gate. For the gate is wide and the way is easy that leads to destruction, and those who enter by it are many. For the gate is narrow and the way is hard that leads to life, and those who find it are few. And, Truly, I say to you, only with difficulty will a rich person enter the kingdom of heaven. Again I tell you, it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich person to enter the kingdom of God. (...) With man this is impossible, but with God all things are possible. So if there were no hope, Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die. Isn't the danger so evident? We're evil and God is Good; His anger burns against the wicked everyday. What must we do to be saved from the wrath which is to come! Wreched and miserable man that I am! Who will free me from this body of sin and death? Does someone need to send a rocket into space to find this cure, this knowledge to bring it down to us? Or does someone need to dig into the center of the earth to reveal the mystery? Where is the knowledge of salvation? In Whom does it exist? Moses wrote in the Book of the Law, It (The command) is not in heaven, that you should say, ‘Who will ascend to heaven for us and bring it to us, that we may hear it and do it?’ Neither is it beyond the sea, that you should say, ‘Who will go over the sea for us and bring it to us, that we may hear it and do it?’ But the word is very near you. It is in your mouth and in your heart, so that you can do it. The world thinks that the truth is hidden: in the depths of the ocean, in outer space, in the center of the earth. They spend countless billions of dollars to explore where no man has gone before, searching for the truth. Yet, God is widely known and everyone knows Him; for no-one needs to ask, “Who has ascended into heaven and descended? Who has gathered the wind in His fists? Who has wrapped the waters in His garment? Who has established all the ends of the earth? What is His name or His son's name? Surely you know!” (Proverbs 30:4) For He has made Himself renown to all His creation. Whose name is constantly blasphemed? Yes, they know Him but do not chose to honor Him. Whose symbol is the cross, which is put on buildings, hung around necks, pierced on the skin, and marked on grave stones? Did we forget that it was once a device of death? For Jesus was put on that cross, turning what the enemy meant for evil into good. Whose coming split our calender in half? Or who makes a week seven days, or hallows the seventh day? The same writings that declare a seven day week and a restful day on the seventh, tells of Him; the same writings that quote wisdom that we have all come to agree upon, even quoting it without knowing its original source, also speaks of Christ Jesus. Who is it that lived a great while ago, but yet died, and still proclaimed as a Man from the dead? That which once was a sect, heresy, a false teaching, and fought against, has now become world-wide good news. For who can fight against God and succeed? If this thing were not of God, it wouldn't have withstood. And yet, we shoot rockets into the heavens to reach God, just as they tried in times past at the Tower of Babel, by their own might to obtain to God, but they will fail; for God sees to it that no-one can come near Him by their own self-will or effort, so that no-one will be able to boast in the Presence of God; but rather, through the word of faith, through the word of truth, which says, "The word is near you, in your mouth and in your heart” (that is, the word of faith that we proclaim); because, if you confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved. For with the heart one believes and is justified, and with the mouth one confesses and is saved. It is just as the Scripture says: “Anyone who believes in Him will never be put to shame.” For there is no distinction between Jew and Greek; for the same Lord is Lord of all, bestowing his riches on all who call on him. For “everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved." For the way of life is through the knowledge of the Lord Jesus unto wisdom unto humility unto even greater grace; and He is the living Grace, the living Truth; and He has descended to us; and He has ascended; and He sends forth the Spirit of Truth; and He will come again with the clouds in the heavens, for He is always and forever. Every question is answered with a single saying; as Paul says in his letter to the Romans, THANK GOD! THE ANSWER IS IN JESUS CHRIST OUR LORD.

The Call - Grace

Jesus, the Servant of God

Hearing doesn't make us right with God, but hearing with faith; Christ Jesus, the holy one from God, suffered and died on a cross for our sins. He was handed over by evil men to crucifixion; being the Son of God, He submitted Himself to the Father's will; He went to the cross willingly to justify many by His blood. He was buried in a tomb for three days, but God rose Him up from the dead on the third day; it was the will of man to kill Him, but God rose Him from the grave. He appeared to many of His disciples, over 500 of them, proving with many signs that He is alive from the dead. Afterwards, He ascended to heaven to the Father's right hand where He is now exalted above every dominion and power and ruler and principality. He awaits until His enemies are made a footstool for His feet, for He will return on the last day to reign with His holy saints. His kingdom is everlasting and His rule is eternal. When we were deserving of punishment and wrath, God instead sent forth His Son, born of a woman, having appeared in the likeness of human flesh, He came to put to death sin in the flesh. He died for sin, not His own - as it is with a criminal - but He died as an innocent man. They put him to trial, trying Him, but found no fault in Him; Pilate determined to release Him, but the Jews wanted Him to be crucified; He was perfect, yet He died a criminal's death. When He died, He had the written charge against Him: "THE KING OF THE JEWS". For this is the only charge they had against Him, that He said He is the Son of God. With His birth, He put the king Herod to fright as a newborn (Matthew 2:1-22); while He lived demons shrieked at the sight of Him and His followers walked behind Him in fear (Mark 10:32); with His death, there was darkness and a great earthquake (Matthew 27:52). Yet, the Son of God came into the world to die, and the death He died was for us. He was sent by the Father bearing the message of His death, for He prophesied about His death many times, fortelling even how He would die. Uriah the Hittite was sent by David, bearing in-hand the letter of his death, even so, he was innocent of wrong, so Christ was sent into the world with the message of His own death; Uriah bore in his hand bad news, but it was impossible for Christ to bear bad news, but only good, for He is from the Father; and by His death, He died for us that we may be joined to Another, namely God. For David could only marry Bathsheba if she is released by the law of her husband by death, so we are made to die through the body of Christ that we may be joined to God (Romans 7:1-4). For even if a man sins, God is able to use it for a testimony to Christ; just as Paul, the apostle, uses Abraham's fault to speak allegorically of the two covenants: one of slavery and the other of freedom in Christ (Galatians 4:21-31). And men of God in times past prophesied to Christ with their words and works.

Jesus never sinned, yet He bore the transgression of us all upon Himself; as Isaiah says, And as for His generation, who considered That He was cut off out of the land of the living For the transgression of my people, to whom the stroke was due? For they wagged their head at Him and reviled Him while He was on the cross, but they did not consider His innocence, nor their own guilt; and yet, "He Himself bore the sin of many, And interceded for the transgressors." (Isaiah 53:12) For He cried out to God for them, saying, "Father, forgive them; for they do not know what they are doing." (Luke 23:34) For they were fulfilling Scripture, which they knew and read every Sabbath, but they did not understand; and they did not know it was for their sins He was dying. The sin we do secretely, He died for openly; for He had no chance to hide, as one does with shame, but He was brought out to the open; He was made a public spectacle; He was lifted up for all to see; even the whole earth, as He says, And I, when I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all people to myself. He endured such opposition from sinners, enduring through it, looking forward to us being made right with God. For God sent Christ into the world beforehand, before the great day of wrath, to make peace before the time; not because His hosts are insufficient for war, but He desires that none should perish, but all come to repentance; He has made a covenant with us in His own blood; for the time will come, and He will return, to execute judgment on all His enemies, even His enemies He died for, but they refuse to come to Him; they, hating Him without cause and repaying His kindness for evil, see His kindness as weakness; but in that day, He will prove that He is not weak. To us who believe, the cross is the power of God unto salvation, but to His enemies who refuse to believe, who delight in wickedness, His power to them will be against them to destroy them; for everyone who wars against God destroys his own self; saying, Who knows the power of Your anger? For as the fear of You, so is Your wrath. But look at the vastness of the mercy and lovingkindness of Him - beyond the heights of the heavens. Mercy triumphs over judgment and God has shown us mercy through the judgment of our sins through Another - His own Son; and who is worthy of such a thing? We will receive mercy from God on that great and terrible day, that is, if we are merciful to others through faith. How greatly should we fear, walking carefully in the fear of the Lord; for Christ hates sin, but became sin, so that we would have right standing with God - His great love towards us surpasses even His hatred for sin; and He died for all our sins; and how great His hatred toward our sin! How great is His wrath toward sin - yet, how much greater is His lovingkindness toward us who, for our sakes, endured the penalty of our sins, having died for us? Even wearing our sins and shame, so that we would put off sin and put Him on like new clothes. How much more should we hate our sin for His sake who loved us more than the hatred of even all our sin? For His lovingkindness and mercy is great.

He disarmed the rulers and authorities through the cross - for they have their power in the law; but Christ Jesus justifies us apart from the law, through faith, rendering the law ineffective for justification; as it says, For Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes. For the law could never justify, for it only condemns, but through the law comes the knowledge of sin; and in Christ Jesus comes the knowledge of God. He grants to us, who have been reconciled to God, overwhelming victory through His name. In His name, we obtain the victory; and only in the refuge of Him we are safe from the wrath of God. But, "How is it that we do nothing, and yet are justified?" By using a human example: yes, you do nothing, you believe, but yet you win justification through Christ. In a similar way, you watch a sports game, yet you do nothing, but watch and believe your team to win. You cheer on your favorite sport's team without doing a single thing - you believe in your team to win. You sit and watch on the sofa, hoping your team would win, while they, by sweat and tears, do all the work. When they score, you rejoice; when they are overcome, you lose heart. When they win, you celebrate with them; and when they lose, you suffer defeat as well. And yet, in Christ Jesus we have overwhelming victory in our trustworthy Savior who won and never loses, and even in His defeat gains us the victory! I say that He was defeated, but it's not as though He was defeated, it only appeared that way to the world. Is one defeated when He is alive and exalted? For God rose Him up. For, "The foolishness of God is wiser than men, and the weakness of God is stronger than men." (1 Corinthians 1:25) And, For everyone who has been born of God overcomes the world. And this is the victory that has overcome the world—our faith. It is by faith we enter into victory with Christ, according to the righteousness of God and none of our own. Through the cross, He made a public spectacle of them: the powers and authorities that govern this world, disarming them and triumphing over them through the cross. He took the written debt of sin which was stored up against us, and nailed it to the cross, paying for our transgressions and rescuing us from the elementary spiritual powers of this world, the rudimentary commands such as "Do not touch" and "Do not handle", to serve God in the new way of the Spirit and life - for those who will to do good, but do not have the power in themselves to do good, He gives power by His grace (Colossians 2:13-15). He is clean, but became dirty to make us clean; bathwater, which is clean, is made dirty since it takes upon itself the filth of the one it cleanses. One goes into the water, being dirty, and comes out clean - not without the cost of the water becoming dirty; and dirty water cannot cleanse, only pure; and Christ has become unto us pure water to cleanse us by faith in Him. He became dirty to make us clean; and we, being dirty, are made clean by Him, by His blood; for with the shedding of blood there is forgiveness of sins. Sanctificaiton is of God, of His Son, of His Spirit; as it says, You shall surely observe My sabbaths; for this is a sign between Me and you throughout your generations, that you may know that I am the LORD who sanctifies you. For on the sabbath, the Israelites did no work, but they entered into His sabbath rest, being sanctified by Him.

Jonathan, son of Saul, became accursed by his father when he ate some honey which was against his oath (1 Samuel 14:24-45); so Saul, in his zeal for his oath, sought to put him to death because of the oath of his curse (1 Samuel 14:39, 1 Samuel 14:44). But Jonathan did not die, rather he bore the curse and lived, because of another oath, the oath of life; even yet, the people who ate meat with the blood, which is contrary to the law of Moses (Leviticus 17:12-14) were not cut off (1 Samuel 14:32-34). And even when Saul sinned by making these foolish oaths and the people sinned by eating with the blood, it was Jonathan who was determined by lot to have sinned, and the rest were free'd. Even though Jonathan did no wrong, only he ate a little honey. But still yet, he lived thereafter; as it says about him, But the people said to Saul, "Must Jonathan die, who has brought about this great deliverance in Israel? Far from it! As the LORD lives, not one hair of his head shall fall to the ground, for he has worked with God this day." So the people rescued Jonathan and he did not die. Which is a picture of Christ who worked with God to bring about this great deliverance; for, Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us—for it is written, “Cursed is everyone who is hanged on a tree”— so that in Christ Jesus the blessing of Abraham might come to the Gentiles, so that we might receive the promised Spirit through faith. For in the law the curse stands even if it be God's own Son; but He was found with no wrong. And we are rightfully under the curse of the law, which says, "Cursed is he who does not confirm the words of this law by doing them." (Deuteronomy 27:26) Because we do not do what the law says. But Christ, who walked in the ways of life, was raised to life, for it was impossible for death to hold Him; and He went through no decay, according to the Word of the LORD. And He has become to us Savior from our sins; for He tasted death for us, as it is saying, But we do see Him who was made for a little while lower than the angels, namely, Jesus, because of the suffering of death crowned with glory and honor, so that by the grace of God He might taste death for everyone. That we who have tasted sin might be released from our sins, that we may know the way of salvation by the forgiveness of our sins, and that we may be delivered from our fear of death

So now, we not establish a righteousness of our own through obedience to the law, but instead, through faith in God's Son. Only through faith can we fulfill the law - for neither circumcision nor uncircumcision matters, but faith expressing itself through love; love does no wrong to a neighbor. This is the fruit of faith: love. Only through faith in Jesus can we fulfill the law; when trying to obtain righteouness through the law by works, righteousness becomes impossible; for righteousness does not come by the law, only the knowledge of sin. But if we are led by the Spirit of God, we are not under the law, for the Spirit is contrary to the flesh. But if you are carnally minded, watch out! The law still reigns over you, for the law is for the carnally minded; but rather, For sin will have no dominion over you, since you are not under law but under grace. For in grace, sin has lost its power. But you may say, "If sin has lost its power and I sin, then what does that mean?" Sin has been rendered powerless through the cross, but you have yieled to it, and you have offered yourself as an obedient servant to sin. And if you obey sin, offering yourself as a slave to it, in it's growth it's death. But Christ Jesus came to set us free from the power of sin and death, so that you would not be conformed to your own thoughts, to your own ways, but made new through the renewing of your mind by the Holy Scriptures, by walking in the Way of God. He has nullified the power of sin, stripping it of its power, for sin has power through the law; but He justifies us apart from the law by faith, by a law of the Spirit and life, by the law of Christ, a law of faith; and the Spirit of Him comes by faith, for all that is of faith is of the Spirit; faith is spiritual and everything spiritual is unseen. His Word is Spirit and Life, saying, It is the Spirit who gives life; the flesh is no help at all. The words that I have spoken to you are spirit and life. So faith is contrary to the law, which states, The person who does these things will live by them. But faith says, THE WORD IS NEAR YOU, IN YOUR MOUTH AND IN YOUR HEART —that is, the word of faith which we are preaching, that if you confess with your mouth Jesus as Lord, and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved; And those who put their trust in Him, in His great lovingkindness, will no longer fear death because He went to the grave and was raised alive, granting to us the same promise which was promised to Abraham: "I will give it to you and to your descendants forever." (Genesis 13:15); and if we are his descendants, we have the promise; and if we have the promise, we know that He is trustworthy and He will grant to us what He has promised on that day. He has promised to us a land flowing with milk and honey, an eternal kingdom; and if an eternal kingdom, everlasting life, considering we endure in His grace until the end. For we are the descendants of faithful Abraham, the father of faith, considering we have faith like Abraham; as it says, "So shall your descendants be." (Genesis 15:5) And as the proverb says, "Like father, like son"; so the descendants of Abraham are like Abraham, and he was a man of faith.

If the goodness of God would ever be manifest to us, it would be supremely good, such that we wouldn't have ever considered it - it would be heavenly, beyond human thought; man would never be able to conjure up the thought of it, for it wouldn't be of us. Yes, His love, not earthly, but heavenly; the love that we know says, "You love me, so love you; if you hate me, then I hate you." But the love from above is above all, beyond thought, beyond imagination, saying, "O my son Absalom, my son, my son Absalom! Would I had died instead of you, O Absalom, my son, my son!" (2 Samuel 18:33) And, Malicious witnesses rise up; They ask me of things that I do not know. They repay me evil for good, To the bereavement of my soul. But as for me, when they were sick, my clothing was sackcloth; I humbled my soul with fasting, And my prayer kept returning to my bosom. I went about as though it were my friend or brother; I bowed down mourning, as one who sorrows for a mother. Yes, the love of God as man, dying for the sins of sinners, bearing their shame, and bearing their burdens; He loved us to death, even death on a cross - the love He has for sinners, His enemies. If these being from David, a man after God's heart, how much more Christ, the Son of Man and the Son of God? For David prophesied to Christ, but Jesus is the fulfillment of prophesy. So, What then shall we say to these things? If God is for us, who is against us? He who did not spare His own Son, but delivered Him over for us all, how will He not also with Him freely give us all things? Who will bring a charge against God's elect? God is the one who justifies; who is the one who condemns? Christ Jesus is He who died, yes, rather who was raised, who is at the right hand of God, who also intercedes for us. Who will separate us from the love of Christ? Will tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword? But in all these things we overwhelmingly conquer through Him who loved us. For I am convinced that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor things present, nor things to come, nor powers, nor height, nor depth, nor any other created thing, will be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord. Oh, how they who refuse to believe are condemned already, for they make Him out to be a liar. We stand on God's Word which is a sure foundation for our feet. The Father God has testified to us in many ways concerning His Son; we may believe man's testimony, but how much more ought we to believe the testimony of God concerning His Son? The Psalms and the prophets which speak of Christ's sufferings are many (Isaiah 53, Psalm 22, Psalm 35, Psalm 89); and the Scriptures of His betrayal are likewise (Psalm 109, Psalm 69, Psalm 41, Psalm 55); for it was according to God's predetermined plan that Christ would suffer for us, and there-after be exalted. Time would fail if I began to tell about Joseph, who was betrayed by his brethren, and exalted to Pharaoh's right hand; or Job, who was righteous and smitten by God, and was restored twofold; or Samson, who was mocked by a great assembly of his enemies, and in his death, killing many; or Mordecai, who was predetermined to die on a stake, and was exalted second highest in all of Persia; or the prophets, who being sent by God, being disregarded, being rejected by their own, beaten and some killed, and obtained to a better hope, a better resurrection. Among these, there are many other types found in the Scriptures which bring strong conviction of the faith; as Paul says, It was before your eyes that Jesus Christ was publicly portrayed as crucified. And these eyes are the eyes of faith.

Jesus, the Rock of our Salvation

Israel, who God delivered out of Egypt with a powerful arm and a mighty hand, had stony hearts, and were constantly disbelieving Him; they had just been redeemed from Egypt by Moses, and then wanted to stone Moses who rescued them. As it is written, They said, “Why did you bring us up out of Egypt to make us and our children and livestock die of thirst?” Then Moses cried out to the Lord, “What am I to do with these people? They are almost ready to stone me.” The Lord answered Moses, “Go out in front of the people. Take with you some of the elders of Israel and take in your hand the staff with which you struck the Nile, and go. I will stand there before you by the rock at Horeb. Strike the rock, and water will come out of it for the people to drink.” So Moses did this in the sight of the elders of Israel. In the people's stony hearts is revealed the content of their stony hearts by desiring to physically stone Moses. Their anger, unbelief, and hardness of heart was toward Moses, for they wanted to stone him with hard stones; as the proverb says, "Out of the wicked comes forth wickedness" (1 Samuel 24:13) Moses was God's representative, he spoke on behalf of God, so the people's anger was directed toward God; they were angry with Him; as it says, for the LORD hears your grumblings which you grumble against Him. And what are we? Your grumblings are not against us but against the LORD. God could have rightfully given the people what they deserved, since they repaid Him evil for good; but instead of striking the rock of the people's stony heart and wounding them, God struck a different Rock through Moses, a spiritual Rock that traveled with them, which was Christ (1 Corinthians 10:4); and out of that Rock came spiritual water for the people to drink. (This is spiritual water because water doesn't normally flow from a rock, and anything abnormal is spiritual; everything miraculous is from the spirit, whether good or bad.) So by God's grace, the people were spared, but the Rock was struck instead; a spiritual drink came from the Rock, and it is spiritual because water doesn't normally flow from rocks - no. Water doesn't come from rocks, but from springs; and Jesus didn't come from earth, but from heaven; He who descends from heaven is spiritual, but we are from below, earthly. The water He gives is His Spirit which He gives, becoming a well of water inside, springing up to everlasting life; for our confession of Christ is salvation unto us who believe by the resurrection of Jesus Christ; and by this Living Water, we never thirst again. As Moses spent 40 days and nights on a mountain with God, drinking no food nor water, so he was the first to taste of the Living Water of Christ; on that mount he received tablets of the covenant, the Words of God written on stone. But to the people whose hearts were stone, the covenant had no effect on them; however by Christ, "I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you; and I will remove the heart of stone from your flesh and give you a heart of flesh.” (Ezekiel 36:26) And, "This is the covenant which I will make with the house of Israel after those days," declares the LORD, "I will put My law within them and on their heart I will write it; and I will be their God, and they shall be My people.” (Jeremiah 31:33) For the new heart and new spirit is made possible through Christ, who was struck by God in our stead, and by His going to the Father. It was God's good plan, and that, to preserve life rather than destroying it; as the prophesy in the Gospel of John says, “You know nothing at all! You do not realize that it is better for you that one man die for the people than that the whole nation perish.” Caiaphas did not say this on his own. Instead, as high priest that year, he was prophesying that Jesus would die for the nation, and not only for the nation, but also for the scattered children of God, to gather them together into one. The staff of God which Moses had in his hand is a piece of wood. And by the cross, which is wood, Christ saves. For a staff is no weapon at all, but God being powerful, works wonders by that which is nothing at all to show His great power. As it says, "Take in your hand the staff with which you struck the Nile", for it is the cross which opens up a way for us out of captivity of sin to God in Christ; as it says, But may it never be that I would boast, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through which the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world.

At another time, Moses struck the rock again when God told him to speak to the Rock to bring forth water (Numbers 20:1-13). Moses disobeyed; being angry with the people, he struck the rock twice; as a result he was banished from entering into the land promised because he did not honor God in front of the people and sanctify the miracle before them. And this as a warning for us today: Christ was sacrificed once. If we struggle against sin, let us pray to the Rock and get deliverance through the cross, instead of striking again by continuing in willful sin; for it is possible to crucify again the Son of God to oneself by falling away. Let us not be content in sin especially since Jesus saves us from sin; He has set us free to be free, and sin is not anything to coddle; for it is deadly and repugnant, it is the danger. Sin has lost its power, and it has been rendered powerless by the grace of God in Christ Jesus; therefore, if we continue to sin after receiving the knowledge of the truth, it's because, the light is come into the world, and men loved darkness rather than the light: for their works were evil. There is no sacrifice for willful sins, but a fearful looking for of judgment and firey indignation which will consume the adversaries of God (Hebrews 10:26-30). So take heed! That which is powerless shall not master over you, for we are no longer under law, but under grace. Sin can only be our master if we willingly submit to it; rather, obey righteousness, obey Christ. Test yourselves. Do you love the light of life? Do you hunger and thirst for righteousness? For Christ gives us the grace to do the good we want to do: to say, "No" to sin, and to say, "Yes" to God. The following warning ought to keep us from sin in the fear of the Lord, as the writer of Hebrews states, For in the case of those who have once been enlightened and have tasted of the heavenly gift and have been made partakers of the Holy Spirit, and have tasted the good word of God and the powers of the age to come, and [then] have fallen away, it is impossible to renew them again to repentance, since they again crucify to themselves the Son of God and put Him to open shame. For ground that drinks the rain which often falls on it and brings forth vegetation useful to those for whose sake it is also tilled, receives a blessing from God; but if it yields thorns and thistles, it is worthless and close to being cursed, and it ends up being burned. But, beloved, we are convinced of better things concerning you, and things that accompany salvation, though we are speaking in this way.

For this is contained in Scripture: "BEHOLD, I LAY IN ZION A CHOICE STONE, A PRECIOUS CORNER stone, AND HE WHO BELIEVES IN HIM WILL NOT BE DISAPPOINTED." This precious value, then, is for you who believe; but for those who disbelieve, "THE STONE WHICH THE BUILDERS REJECTED, THIS BECAME THE VERY CORNER stone," and, "A STONE OF STUMBLING AND A ROCK OF OFFENSE"; for they stumble because they are disobedient to the word, and to this doom they were also appointed. He is the Rock of our refuge, a city of refuge, in which one flees for protection from the onslaught of the enemy. Just as a man in the time of war after being defeated flees back to his stronghold, so Christ is our saftey from our enemies, from the powers of wickedness, from the wolves whose power is in their mouth, that is, their lies; and, And none of those who take refuge in Him will be condemned. And, Therefore there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus. Just as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings, so the shelter of the Most High is a protection against the striking of the sun under the shadow of His wings; as it says, The sun will not smite you by day, nor the moon by night. The LORD will protect you from all evil; He will keep your soul. For when the seasons change and the trees lose foliage, the grass withers and dies, but the Rock remains unchanged forever, saying, Those who trust in the LORD are as Mount Zion, which cannot be moved but abides forever. And, It is better to take refuge in the LORD than to trust in man. It is better to take refuge in the LORD than to trust in princes. Or, Do not trust in princes, in mortal man, in whom there is no salvation. But, "There is salvation in no one else; for there is no other name under heaven that has been given among men by which we must be saved." (Acts 4:12) For He is our defense, our shield, our shelter against the firey darts of the enemy; He is the protector of our lives, the tower of eternal salvation. He girds us with the whole armor of Himself so that we would withstand the enemy in the day of evil; He is the refuge of salvation to everyone who flees to Him. He is our Defense and that, our Offense, our weapon for battle, that we may take up the Word of Him, that we may fight against the enemy by Him; "Blessed be the LORD, my rock, Who trains my hands for war, And my fingers for battle; My lovingkindness and my fortress, My stronghold and my deliverer, My shield and He in whom I take refuge, Who subdues my people under me.” (Psalms 144:1-2)

Jesus, the Lamb of God

Under the Old Covenant, the Israelites obtained atonement by God for their unintentional sins by sacrificing an animal; when they sinned, blood needed to be shed and applied to the horns of the altar for atonement; the animal had to be perfect (no defects, blemishes, etc), a clean animal, and it had to be innocent of all wrong (like goring, etc). With sin comes death, for sin and death go together; death trails behind sin, and the end of sin is death; so death is needed for atonement, blood needed to be shed for forgiveness. When the substitute sacrifice of an animal occurred, the one offering was covered of his fault; afterwards, it was like the fault had never occurred, for it was forgiven - the wrong was offset. The blood does nothing if it's in the animal; the animal needed to be slain and dealt with according to the law. God established these laws for Israel so they may be forgiven. The Israelites could only give a sacrifice for unintentional sins: that is, sins that were done in ignorance. There was no sacrifice under the Law for intentional sins. These sins were expiated in another way: excommunication (being cut off from Israel), or punishment, or even death. Rebellion was also not forgiven, for this is the nature of rebellion: you know what is right, but you don't do it, or, you know what is wrong, but you do it anyway; as it says in the Old Covenant, Behold, I am going to send an angel before you to guard you along the way and to bring you into the place which I have prepared. Be on your guard before Him and obey His voice; do not be rebellious toward Him, for He will not pardon your transgression since My name is in Him. And the same in the New Covenant, For if we go on sinning willfully after receiving the knowledge of the truth, there no longer remains a sacrifice for sins, but a terrifying expectation of judgment and THE FURY OF A FIRE WHICH WILL CONSUME THE ADVERSARIES. For all rebellion is willful, and such sins are punished through chastisement; but, When we are judged, we are disciplined by the Lord so that we will not be condemned along with the world. “And we are ready to punish all disobedience, whenever your obedience is complete.” (2 Corinthians 10:6) So the Israelites had to sacrifice a perfect lamb or which-ever clean animal prescribed by the law for forgiveness of unintentional sin; it is like this: the clean animal cleansed them from their filth, the unblemished beast bore their blemish. The sacrifice did not cleanse the offerer's conscience though because they still had guilt, for an innocent animal had to die in their place; the sacrifice did not take away their sin, but only covered their sin temporarily; as it says in Hebrews, But in those (sacrifices) there is a reminder of sins year by year. For it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins. For a goat is not equivalent to a human, so it cannot be substituted for a human; but rather, life shall be for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot.

The animal was the offerer's substitute; and certain sin offerings were brought into the Holy Place when the congregation as a whole sinned, and the bodies of those animals were burned outside the camp and not eaten by the priest; as it says, Although the high priest brings the blood of animals into the Holy Place as a sacrifice for sin, the bodies are burned outside the camp. And so Jesus also suffered outside the city gate, to sanctify the people by His own blood. Therefore let us go to Him outside the camp, bearing the disgrace He bore. For here we do not have a permanent city, but we are looking for the city that is to come. All these testify to Jesus; and of Him is said, Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world! He was sacrificed on our behalf as our substitute, for the forgiveness of sins - not a sacrifice that needed to be offered over and over, but once for all; and He was the sin offering for the whole world, not just the congregation of Israel; as it is written, “These of the sons of Gad were captains of the army; he who was least was equal to a hundred and the greatest to a thousand.” (1 Chronicles 12:14) And, "You (David) should not go out; for if we indeed flee, they will not care about us; even if half of us die, they will not care about us. But you are worth ten thousand of us." (2 Samuel 18:3) If David is as ten thousand, and the greatest of Gad a thousand, how do you suppose is Christ, the Son of God, except as the whole world? He is one, yet given for all, being God's own Son. And those offerings for the whole congregation of Israel were not eaten; but yet, We have an altar from which those who serve the tent have no right to eat. And the blood of every offering in the Old Covenant was never eaten; but this is a reminder He has given us, a reminder of His grace towards us: Communion (Matthew 26:26-28), His flesh and His blood.

Furthermore, we don't have guilt in that our sins have caused Christ to die, for He lives, and forevermore, seated in the heavens; by His resurrection, we have justification with God. His one act of obedience is the work that matters in the sight of God, united with our work of faith; for God shows us the way of atonement in the law. These things came not from man, although they were spoken by the mouth of Moses; they came from God, this is God's way. Everything Moses spoke, God spoke to him to tell the people. These things he spoke were also written down by the commandment of God. And by reading these things, we have faith; for faith comes by hearing and hearing the Word of Christ. So it's by faith, apart from our works, and it is accounted to us as grace, for grace is a gift; and if it's by our works, then grace is no longer grace, but due wages. For those who work deserve wages, but he who receives a gift has done no work for it. So Christ was sacrificed apart from our works by the grace of God to be received by faith. And He, being the High Priest, brought an offering to God, offering Himself to sanctify for-ever those who are being saved. And thereafter, having been raised, He has been exalted as Lord of all, especially to those who believe. The work of the obedience of Christ forgives, making many who believe righteous and justifying the many by His one act. And He proves to us these facts through the testimony of the Psalms and the Prophets, the book of the Law, which were all written beforehand, foretelling God's plan, which is now revealed in Christ; as Jesus even says, And He said to them, "O foolish men and slow of heart to believe in all that the prophets have spoken! Was it not necessary for the Christ to suffer these things and to enter into His glory?" Then beginning with Moses and with all the prophets, He explained to them the things concerning Himself in all the Scriptures. And the testimony of the Apostles, who seen the risen Lord, testifying to what they seen and heard, namely, Jesus, the Savior of the world; so now, we may have confidence to approach the living God through His Way, which is through Christ's body; as it says, Now where there is forgiveness of these things, there is no longer any offering for sin. Therefore, brethren, since we have confidence to enter the holy place by the blood of Jesus, by a new and living way which He inaugurated for us through the veil, that is, His flesh, and since we have a great priest over the house of God, let us draw near with a sincere heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled clean from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water. For this is God's appointed Way, and there is no other; as Jesus testified and His testimony is true, I am the way, and the truth, and the life; no one comes to the Father but through Me.

Furthermore, before the giving of the Law, when the Israelites were in slavery in Egypt, the Israelites had to sacrifice a lamb and paint the doorpost of their house with its blood. The angel of death, sent by God, came through the city and slayed the firstborn of every house whose doorpost was not marked with the lamb's blood; and the death angel was commanded to pass over the house marked with blood and not to harm anyone inside. This is remembered now by the Israelites, even to this day, as the Passover. For seven days after the Passover, they cleaned out all leaven from their homes; even to this day, they celebrate this as the Festival of Unleavened Bread. In the same way, Paul says, Therefore purge out the old leaven, that you may be a new lump, since you truly are unleavened. For indeed Christ, our Passover, was sacrificed for us. Therefore let us keep the feast, not with old leaven, nor with the leaven of malice and wickedness, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth. And so, we have been rescued from our slavery to Egypt by the Passover; which is to say: we have been redeemed from our slavery to sin by the blood of Jesus Christ, our Passover. By His cross, we have been crucified to the world and the world crucified to us; similarily, Israel crossed through the sea on dry ground while their enemies perished. So in Christ, the world and everything it esteems, is brought to nought through the cross; for, "God has chosen the foolish things of the world to shame the wise, and God has chosen the weak things of the world to shame the things which are strong, and the base things of the world and the despised God has chosen, the things that are not, so that He may nullify the things that are.” (1 Corinthians 1:27-28) And so if anyone who has love of this world in them, the love of the Father is not in them; for how can we love what God hates? As the prophet asks, "Should you help the wicked and love those who hate the LORD and so bring wrath on yourself from the LORD?” (2 Chronicles 19:2) Everything that is of the world is contrary to God, and everything men value is detestable in the sight of God (Luke 16:15). But we who love the Father have been rescued from the world's lusts. This is the sacrifice that God desires: not of bulls or goats or lambs, but of setting apart our sins for destruction, crucifying the misdeeds of the body, devoting them to death, so that we may not be destroyed with them, and fleeing from this world so that we would not be punished with it; this is the sacrifice of death that we may live, which is initiated by God's Sacrifice of Atonement; for even if we purge out every crumb of leaven in our lives, if we do not have blood, our work is in vain. This is the thing Solomon seen when he prayed after the completion of the permanent temple of God; instead of praying, asking for the LORD God to forgive the people according to the ordinances of sacrifice even though they were written in the law, he instead says, If they return to You with all their heart and with all their soul in the land of their captivity, where they have been taken captive, and pray toward their land which You have given to their fathers and the city which You have chosen, and toward the house which I have built for Your name, then hear from heaven, from Your dwelling place, their prayer and supplications, and maintain their cause and forgive Your people who have sinned against You. For, Has the LORD as much delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices As in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, And to heed than the fat of rams. So now, purge out the old leaven from the home in haste, for in haste we were brought up out of Egypt to a new home.

Jesus, the Door of Eternal Salvation

The world was judged the world before by water, a massive flood; historians know that the world went through some catastrophic change, but their speculations vary. But the Truth is found in the Word of Truth: God repented that He created man and was sorry that He created them, so He warned Noah and commanded him to build an ark; Noah's actions testified to the people of the coming destruction. God was going to send a flood of water, which was something never seen, for water never came from the sky, but mists came up from the ground to water the earth (Genesis 2:6). The people presumed, "Something like this has never happened ever-since all the days of our life, ever-since the days of old, water has come up from the earth and everything has gone on as it should." However, despite what they believed in their hearts, water came from the sky and God released the water from inside the earth to flood all the land, to destroy all having the breath of life; however, all who entered in through the door of the ark was saved, which was Noah and his family, eight in all and two of every kind of animal with some extra animals for sacrifice; for animals were not afraid of man in those days because they were not killed by man for food. And all these point to the present time, for whoever enters in through the Door, that is to say, through Jesus Christ, will be saved from the flood of God's wrath which is yet to come, which is coming upon the earth, which is judgment not of water, but of fire. As it says, “For this you know with certainty, that no immoral or impure person or covetous man, who is an idolater, has an inheritance in the kingdom of Christ and God. Let no one deceive you with empty words, for because of these things the wrath of God comes upon the sons of disobedience.” (Ephesians 5:5-6) And, “Therefore consider the members of your earthly body as dead to immorality, impurity, passion, evil desire, and greed, which amounts to idolatry. For it is because of these things that the wrath of God will come upon the sons of disobedience, and in them you also once walked, when you were living in them.” (Colossians 3:5-7) But Christ has become unto us who believe a refuge, a shelter, from God's wrath; through God's provision of grace, by His Son, He spares many who believe, who trust in Him, for He is trustworthy. God has given us His Son and declares in Him salvation, and entrance into Him is by faith. Noah was spared for a short while from flood waters, but later died naturally; however, those who enter into Christ will be saved eternally through life everlasting, the life of Christ. Noah inherited a new earth, purged from evil by water; how much greater do you suppose the inheritance of God, the new heaven and the new earth, is to those who persist in well-doing, which will be purified by raging fire? For only that which can withstand the fire endures, being made pure by refinement; and the rest which cannot withstand is burned up; and the city of God is of pure gold. If we go on sinning, living as one with this world, we are surely not saved from the wrath to come, for only those who entered into the ark were saved; saying, You know that He appeared in order to take away sins; and in Him there is no sin. No one who abides in Him sins; no one who sins has seen Him or knows Him. But who knowing and believing these things does not flee to Christ for saftey to abide in Him? As the admonishment says, Come out of her, my people, lest you share in her sins, and lest you receive of her plagues.

After the great flood, God made a covenant with Noah, This is the sign of the covenant which I am making between Me and you and every living creature that is with you, for all successive generations; I set My bow in the cloud, and it shall be for a sign of a covenant between Me and the earth. It shall come about, when I bring a cloud over the earth, that the bow will be seen in the cloud, and I will remember My covenant, which is between Me and you and every living creature of all flesh; and never again shall the water become a flood to destroy all flesh. When the bow is in the cloud, then I will look upon it, to remember the everlasting covenant between God and every living creature of all flesh that is on the earth. During the flood, water was used to punish the world, but Noah was saved through water; likewise in regard to the end, "Many will be purged, purified and refined, but the wicked will act wickedly; and none of the wicked will understand, but those who have insight will understand.” (Daniel 12:10) The testimony will condemn the unbelieving, but will save the believing. So then, the righteous will be saved through fire. But then, after the flood of water, God caused the rain to pour down on the righteous and the wicked alike, showing grace to all despite their wickedness; so with the everlasting covenant of salt came grace and peace from God; then pertaining to this very time, God says through Isaiah, "For this is like the days of Noah to Me, When I swore that the waters of Noah Would not flood the earth again; So I have sworn that I will not be angry with you Nor will I rebuke you. For the mountains may be removed and the hills may shake, But My lovingkindness will not be removed from you, And My covenant of peace will not be shaken" Says the LORD who has compassion on you. So much so, Paul closes his letters with the phrase, "Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ." Likewise He says, "Let your speech always be with grace, seasoned with salt, so that you will know how you should respond to each person." (Colossians 4:6) And salt through the covenant of salt God has made with us, that of peace, that of forgiving our sins by Christ's blood, that of His grace, so that we would be reconciled to Him in love. But I warn, that after the flood those who knew of God's lovingkindness and forbearance in making a covenant with every living creature through Noah, and who knew of His wrath against sin in flooding the world with water, but sinned blatantly despite these things, as it was with Sodom, as it was with Gomorrah, God judged, not with water, but with fire and brimstone, rainning it down from the sky, as it is with water, as a warning in the latter days of the judgment of fire to those who refuse to obey the truth, living ungodly lives; and fire, namely, a lake of fire, just as it is with water; and fire, namely, raining down, just as it is with water; and fire to drink, just as it is with water. "Oh, but today we have never seen the water as fire, ever since the beginning of the world these things have always been the same." But with the revelation of the grace of God comes the revelation of the wrath of God; for Christ was the first to preach openly about Gehenna and He was the first reveal the Good News of God's grace. The world as we know it is passing away and anyone found of it in the end will be found deserving judgment with it, but God calls us out ahead of time, warning us to get in Christ. He wills that none should perish, but all come to everlasting life; so much so, He has made peace ahead of the time with us through Jesus. Greater is the Spirit in us than the spirit in the world; the Spirit in us is the Spirit of Christ Jesus, and He has overcome the world; therefore, we have strength in Christ to flee the world and its lusts and pride. Those who entered into the ark floated above the destruction of the earth by water; so it will be for those who enter into Christ; we will be caught up with Jesus when He returns, saying, Then we who are alive and remain will be caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air, and so we shall always be with the Lord. I put only parts of Holy Scripture in here, but I encourage you to read the whole of them for yourself.

Jesus testifies that He is the door into the sheepfold and the Good Shepherd; as He says, Truly, truly, I tell you, I am the gate for the sheep. All who came before Me were thieves and robbers, but the sheep did not listen to them. I am the gate. If anyone enters through Me, he will be saved. He will come in and go out and find pasture. The thief comes only to steal and kill and destroy. I have come that they may have life, and have it in all its fullness. I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down His life for the sheep. And again, Ezekiel, the prophet, spoke of the Messiah, who is God Himself, saying, For thus says the Lord GOD, "Behold, I Myself will search for My sheep and seek them out. As a shepherd cares for his herd in the day when he is among his scattered sheep, so I will care for My sheep and will deliver them from all the places to which they were scattered on a cloudy and gloomy day. I will bring them out from the peoples and gather them from the countries and bring them to their own land; and I will feed them on the mountains of Israel, by the streams, and in all the inhabited places of the land. I will feed them in a good pasture, and their grazing ground will be on the mountain heights of Israel. There they will lie down on good grazing ground and feed in rich pasture on the mountains of Israel. I will feed My flock and I will lead them to rest," declares the Lord GOD. "I will seek the lost, bring back the scattered, bind up the broken and strengthen the sick; but the fat and the strong I will destroy. I will feed them with judgment."

Jesus, the Lion from the Tribe of Judah

Then Samson went down with his father and mother to Timnah, and they came to the vineyards of Timnah. And behold, a young lion came toward him roaring. Then the Spirit of the Lord rushed upon him, and although he had nothing in his hand, he tore the lion in pieces as one tears a young goat. But he did not tell his father or his mother what he had done. Then he went down and talked with the woman, and she was right in Samson's eyes. After some days he returned to take her. And he turned aside to see the carcass of the lion, and behold, there was a swarm of bees in the body of the lion, and honey. He scraped it out into his hands and went on, eating as he went. And he came to his father and mother and gave some to them, and they ate. But he did not tell them that he had scraped the honey from the carcass of the lion. And, Then Samson said to them, "Let me now propound a riddle to you; if you will indeed tell it to me within the seven days of the feast, and find it out, then I will give you thirty linen wraps and thirty changes of clothes. But if you are unable to tell me, then you shall give me thirty linen wraps and thirty changes of clothes." And they said to him, "Propound your riddle, that we may hear it." So he said to them, "Out of the eater came something to eat, And out of the strong came something sweet." But they could not tell the riddle in three days.

A lion is strong and powerful, no doubt. No-one certainly takes a lion into their home so that their children can play with it - no, they don't. A lion is dangerous. A lion isn't sweet; it won't let you pet it and it won't give you any kisses (not without taking off some of your flesh with a lick). In a similar way, Jesus Christ is mighty and powerful; He is the Son of God,with the exact likeness of God, with the name which is above every other name, with one of His names, the Lion from the Tribe of Judah; as it says, Then one of the elders said to me, 'Do not weep! See, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has triumphed. He [Jesus, the Lamb] is able to open the scroll and its seven seals.' He walked in power while on earth; He healed many, He spoke with authority, and by the power of His authority in His Word the devils cried aloud and were driven out of people; the people trembled behind Him as they followed Him. Yet, He died a criminal's death; when they came to arrest Him, “He said to them, "I am He," they drew back and fell to the ground.” (John 18:6) Although He was not a criminal and there was no fault in Him, He handed Himself over willingly; His life was not taken from Him, but He went to the cross; as He was saying, Or do you think that I cannot appeal to My Father, and He will at once put at My disposal more than twelve legions of angels? How then will the Scriptures be fulfilled, which say that it must happen this way? Although He is powerful, He willingly became weak for our sakes. He did many mighty miracles; He was walking on water, hushing the wind and waves, sending for a miraculous catch of many fish, and many other fearful things. But, "From the Strong came something sweet", and this is the sweetness of the Gospel: by His death and resurrection, we have been reconciled with God and justified by faith through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead. Bees, which have a stinger, can also produce honey in all its sweetness; God created the bee with its stinger, and He created the bee to make honey. In the testimony, the bees made their honey in the carcass of the lion; the bees did not sting the lion, for the lion was dead; instead, they made their honey in the lion's carcass. “The sting of death is sin, and the power of sin is the law;” (1 Corinthians 15:56) And Jesus is the Lion; He tasted death for us to remove the sting of death from us by His death; for the sting of death only applies when one is living. And, “One died for all, therefore all died;” (2 Corinthians 5:14) And He is the one who died for us so that He "might free those who through fear of death were subject to slavery all their lives.” (Hebrews 2:15) And "so that by the grace of God He might taste death for everyone.” (Hebrews 2:9) And from His death brings forth a substance of sweetness by the wisdom of God and the power of God that rose Christ from the dead. For God has made His wisdom and power to be salvation unto us who believe; “For the word of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God.” (1 Corinthians 1:18) And, "I WILL DESTROY THE WISDOM OF THE WISE, AND THE CLEVERNESS OF THE CLEVER I WILL SET ASIDE." (1 Corinthians 1:19) So to those who disbelieve, God's wisdom makes fools of them and His power is against them, just as it was with Daniel's enemies in the lion's den (Daniel 6:7-28).

And, "Out of the eater came something to eat". Whenever a sacrifice was given to the LORD, He consumed it, He consumed it with fire; for He is a consuming fire; and all sacrifices were burnt on the altar unto God as a soothing aroma. Those sacrifices were as food to Him; as it says, “'The priest shall offer them up in smoke on the altar as food, an offering by fire for a soothing aroma; all fat is the LORD'S.” (Leviticus 3:16) And so, out of the Eater of sacrifices came a Sacrifice. For men usually give sacrifices to God, but God gave sacrifice to man, His own Son, and so that we may offer righteous sacrifices to God, namely, our lives which are lived for Him. For from His death comes forgiveness of sins and justification with God and our friendship restored with God and the seal of the Holy Spirit and everlasting life to come. Although Jesus is powerful as a lion, He also is meek and lowly as a lamb; Jesus is the Great Shepherd of the flock, and He is also called, "the Lamb of God". Do not be afraid; I am the first and the last, and the living One; and I was dead, and behold, I am alive forevermore, and I have the keys of death and of Hades. Although God is the Eater of sacrifices, He doesn't need us to eat, nor does He need our service, for He needs not anything from us, but He gives us all things; and furthermore, He gave us a Sacrifice so that we would honor Him with righteous sacrifices; as it says in the Psalms, Hear, O My people, and I will speak; O Israel, I will testify against you; I am God, your God. I do not reprove you for your sacrifices, And your burnt offerings are continually before Me. I shall take no young bull out of your house Nor male goats out of your folds. For every beast of the forest is Mine, The cattle on a thousand hills. I know every bird of the mountains, And everything that moves in the field is Mine. If I were hungry I would not tell you, For the world is Mine, and all it contains. Shall I eat the flesh of bulls Or drink the blood of male goats? Offer to God a sacrifice of thanksgiving And pay your vows to the Most High; Call upon Me in the day of trouble; I shall rescue you, and you will honor Me. And as the riddle is saying, Out of the eater came something to eat; out of the strong came something sweet. So God, the Father, the consumer of sacrifices, gave a Sacrifice, which is Christ, for us to eat, namely, His flesh and His blood (which is Communion). Although He is greatly kind towards us, that is not an excuse for us to be slugglish or lazy; for He is powerful, but became weak for our sakes; therefore, we ought not to see His kindness as weakness; He is mighty in His dealing with us, specifically for our building up, not for our tearing down. If we neglect His cloak of righteousness for a cloak of maliciousness, certainly He won't treat us differently than the Gentiles, would He? Let me say: when in the military, we had drill instructors and officers. The officers were not like the drill instructors, for the drill instructors screamed at us, spiting saliva; they were not afraid to get in our face. They would lay hands on us; often times disciplining us with intense training. But the officers were kind and spoke tenderly to us. One time, we were sitting in class, learning by an officer. We began to slouch in our seats, some of us began to sleep. Then, yelling more ferociously than any drill instructor we have heard, he shouted, "Do not take my kindness for weakness!" All of us sat up straight in our seats, our spirits reviving in us; those sleeping were shocked with a jolt. Before, he would call for readers, and there would be hessitation; by obligation, one person would stand up; but now, a handful of volunteers stood up and battled for a chance to read. For we seen he was kind, but he was even more powerful than a drill instructor. “The LORD will stretch forth Your strong scepter from Zion, saying, "Rule in the midst of Your enemies." Your people will volunteer freely in the day of Your power; In holy array, from the womb of the dawn, Your youth are to You as the dew.” (Psalms 110:2-3)

And in reference to the Tribe of Judah, Israel had twelve sons, and each son became a branch of Israel. And Jesus, as far as His human nature is concerned, came from the tribe of Judah. In Egypt, Judah offered himself in the place of his younger brother, Benjamin; as it is written, Indeed, your servant guaranteed the boy’s safety to my father, saying, ‘If I do not return him to you, I will bear the guilt before you, my father, all my life.’ Now please let your servant stay here as my lord’s slave in place of the boy. Let him return with his brothers. For how can I go back to my father without the boy? I could not bear to see the misery that would overwhelm him. But Judah never did give his life for his brother; but Jesus would do so later with His substitution for us that He would make willingly; He interceded and did what no-one else could do, He stepped down. Reuben was firstborn of Israel, but was rejected; but Judah, "Who shall go up first for us to battle against the sons of Benjamin?" Then the LORD said, "Judah shall go up first." And, "Who shall go up first for us against the Canaanites, to fight against them?" The LORD said, "Judah shall go up; behold, I have given the land into his hand." Or even, "The total of the numbered men of the camp of Judah: 186,400, by their armies. They shall set out first.” (Numbers 2:9) For Judah was chosen by God, and chosen as the place where His temple would dwell; saying, “He also rejected the tent of Joseph, And did not choose the tribe of Ephraim, But chose the tribe of Judah, Mount Zion which He loved.” (Psalms 78:67-68) And Judah remained during Israel's rebellion, for they were kept by God's grace, by His promise, although the kings of Judah were unfaithful to the LORD (2 Kings 8:19, 2 Chronicles 21:7). “In the same way then, there has also come to be at the present time a remnant according to God's gracious choice.” (Romans 11:5)

Jesus, the Scapegoat

At the appointed time on the day of Atonement, the high priest confessed the people's sins onto a goat and laid his hands on its head. That goat was to carry the sins far away to a remote place in the wilderness and die, bearing the sins of the people upon itself as an ordinance given by God. This didn't happen whenever they wanted, but at the appointed time, according to the set time by God. And likewise, "While we were still still, at the right time"; as it says, For while we were still weak, at the right time Christ died for the ungodly. For scarcely for a righteous man will one die; yet perhaps for a good man someone would even dare to die. But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. Much more then, having now been justified by His blood, we shall be saved from wrath through Him. For if when we were enemies we were reconciled to God through the death of His Son, much more, having been reconciled, we shall be saved by His life. And not only that, but we also rejoice in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom we have now received the reconciliation. He removed our sins from us to a remote place, not to be remembered, passing them off to a distant land, outside the city; as it says, Therefore Jesus also, that He might sanctify the people through His own blood, suffered outside the gate. As the Psalmist says, He has not dealt with us according to our sins, Nor rewarded us according to our iniquities. For as high as the heavens are above the earth, So great is His lovingkindness toward those who fear Him. As far as the east is from the west, So far has He removed our transgressions from us. Just as a father has compassion on his children, So the LORD has compassion on those who fear Him. Jesus is the scapegoat who took upon Himself the blame of us, being for us a guilt offering for sin; as the prophet, Isaiah, foreseen by the Spirit of God, But the LORD was pleased To crush Him, putting Him to grief; If He would render Himself as a guilt offering, He will see His offspring, He will prolong His days, And the good pleasure of the LORD will prosper in His hand. For the unclean person defiled by death was sprinkled with the ashes of the heifer on the third and the seventh day; for the ashes were mixed with water; and on the seventh day, the unclean washed his clothes and his body with water. For if the blood of goats and bulls and the ashes of a heifer sprinkling those who have been defiled sanctify for the cleansing of the flesh, how much more will the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered Himself without blemish to God, cleanse your conscience from dead works to serve the living God? For we are defiled by death, by dead works, but sanctified to God by the blood of Jesus, mixed with the water of the Word and united to our conscience by faith, cleansing us by faith. So therefore, let us draw near with a sincere heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled clean from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water. Oh, the depths of the mercy and grace of the Almighty God, who loved us by the death of His Son and continues to love us through the life of His Son; may it never be said or thought of again, "The Lord God doesn't love me." Oh! How He demonstrated His great love for us on the cross at Calvary! He didn't merely say, "I love you", but proved His love in the greatest way possible by giving up His own life in the Man of His Presence, choosing us unto adoption as sons. Oh, that we would fear God, so that we would stay away from all appearances of evil and never depart from Him; oh, that we would know what is good for us, for the fear of the Lord is to depart from iniquity; saying, By lovingkindness and truth iniquity is atoned for, And by the fear of the LORD one keeps away from evil.

The Lord Jesus is one, and what He did was for many; and the scapegoat for Israel was one goat, but was for the whole nation of Israel. And the LORD God, Most High, was pleased with one man, Noah, the man of righteousness, and blessed the whole world on account of him; as it says, And the LORD smelled a soothing aroma [of Noah's sacrifice]. Then the LORD said in His heart, “I will never again curse the ground for man’s sake, although the imagination of man’s heart is evil from his youth; nor will I again destroy every living thing as I have done." Again, He was pleased with another man and blessed the world on account of him, that is, Abraham, the man of faith. In your seed all the nations of the earth shall be blessed, because you have obeyed My voice. And Jesus Christ, being the fulfillment, is the One who fulfills these things; He, having obeyed God at the cross brings salvation to many, un-doing the one act of the one man, Adam, who sinned in the beginning, and brings salvation to the whole world, whoever will come; as Paul was saying, Therefore, just as sin entered the world through one man, and death through sin, and in this way death came to all people, because all sinned— To be sure, sin was in the world before the law was given, but sin is not charged against anyone’s account where there is no law. Nevertheless, death reigned from the time of Adam to the time of Moses, even over those who did not sin by breaking a command, as did Adam, who is a pattern of the one to come. But the gift is not like the trespass. For if the many died by the trespass of the one man, how much more did God’s grace and the gift that came by the grace of the one man, Jesus Christ, overflow to the many! Nor can the gift of God be compared with the result of one man’s sin: The judgment followed one sin and brought condemnation, but the gift followed many trespasses and brought justification. For if, by the trespass of the one man, death reigned through that one man, how much more will those who receive God’s abundant provision of grace and of the gift of righteousness reign in life through the one man, Jesus Christ! Consequently, just as one trespass resulted in condemnation for all people, so also one righteous act resulted in justification and life for all people. For just as through the disobedience of the one man the many were made sinners, so also through the obedience of the one man the many will be made righteous. The law was brought in so that the trespass might increase. But where sin increased, grace increased all the more, so that, just as sin reigned in death, so also grace might reign through righteousness to bring eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord. And, For since by a man came death, by a man also came the resurrection of the dead. For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive. And who can say otherwise while mentioning Phinehas, the man of zeal? He being an archetype of Christ, as one man, being zealous for God, in his act of zeal obtained favor for his descendants forever (Numbers 25:7-13); as it says in the Psalms, Then Phinehas stood up and interposed, And so the plague was stayed. And it was reckoned to him for righteousness, To all generations forever. For in regard to Christ, it is said, “For zeal for Your house has consumed Me, And the reproaches of those who reproach You have fallen on Me.” (Psalms 69:9) And who can forget David, the man after God's own heart? He was given a promise and covenant by God concerning His descendants forever; for in his freewill he looked for a dwelling place for the LORD God of Israel. And so by one Man, Jesus Christ, blessing has also come to all nations - not Jews only, but also Gentiles; for His one act is for all. The promise given to Abraham was to him and His Seed, which is Christ; and the covenant given to David was to him and his Descendant, which is the King to come, Jesus. And we are descendants of David, descendants of Abraham, if we be like them; for as the father is, so is the son. So all who are like faithful Abraham or willing and pure-hearted David, all who are like the Son of God (for they prophesied to Christ) are indeed sons of God, made sons by the Spirit of the Son; as it says, “For you are all sons of God through faith in Christ Jesus.” (Galatians 3:26) And, “For all who are being led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God.” (Romans 8:14) So in Christ the eternal blessing of the Jews comes to the Gentiles; this is made possible by His one act which is for all, by the irrevocable covenants of God. For what matters in God's sight is faith and a pure conscience.

When the animal was brought to the temple, the Israelites confessed their error; for they brought the beast and told the priest what it was for: a sin offering, a guilt offering, etc. And by laying their hands on its head, they acknowledged the animal was for them. So there are two confessions: one of sin, another of atonement. It is written by the apostle John, who seen the risen Lord and rested at His bosom, If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us. If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness. If we say we have not sinned, we make Him out to be a liar, and His word is not in us. For this is also how we abide in the truth; this is how we remain in the light. It is possible to deceive ourselves, but these things are written so that we would not deceive ourselves. When we claim we have not have sinned, there is no forgiveness since we do not own our sins, even though we have truly sinned. For what could we be forgiven of, if we affirm that we have not sinned? How can we be forgiven of sin that we reject? Let us be truthful and confess our sins, remaining in the light, so that we may be forgiven and cleansed; for with sin comes darkness and death; in the dark we are not able to see clearly. Everyone has been declared guilty by the law, but Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes; He grants to us righteousness by faith. But even in Christ, the law brings knowledge of sin; but in Christ or without, no-one can be declared righteous by the law; for the law declares everyone as sinners; but in Christ, even apart from the law, He makes many righteous by His obedience act. What is it then, are we righteous sinners? Of course not! The law gives sin it's power, but Christ justifies us apart from the law, therefore sin has lost its strength; as the apostle proclaims, "The sting of death is sin, and the power of sin is the law; but thanks to God, which giveth us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ." (1 Corinthians 15:56-57) So while we abide in the grace of God, which is through faith, we have no obligation to serve sin; if we slip or fall, we confess; we know our sins are forgiven, for He will cleanse our guilty consciences. We know we have forgiveness because our guilt is removed and our hearts will no longer condemn us. Jesus took upon Himself our burdens, our guilt and shame, so that we would not have to; therefore, confess! God has promised; and it is impossible for Him to lie; His promises are a sure foundation to build upon. Let us not walk around with our burdens, but instead freely, giving them to Him. Let us not walk with a hunch because of our burden, but uprightly; who do we hurt by not confessing? I bring about by way of reminder, the Psalmist, who had great anguish because he did not confess his iniquity; as David says, When I kept silent [about my sin,] my body wasted away Through my groaning all day long. For day and night Your hand was heavy upon me; My vitality was drained away [as] with the fever heat of summer. Selah. I acknowledged my sin to You, And my iniquity I did not hide; I said, "I will confess my transgressions to the LORD"; And You forgave the guilt of my sin. And, O LORD, rebuke me not in Your wrath, And chasten me not in Your burning anger. For Your arrows have sunk deep into me, And Your hand has pressed down on me. There is no soundness in my flesh because of Your indignation; There is no health in my bones because of my sin. For my iniquities are gone over my head; As a heavy burden they weigh too much for me. My wounds grow foul [and] fester Because of my folly. I am bent over and greatly bowed down; I go mourning all day long. For my loins are filled with burning, And there is no soundness in my flesh. I am benumbed [deprived of sensation] and badly crushed; I groan because of the agitation of my heart. Lord, all my desire is before You; And my sighing is not hidden from You. My heart throbs, my strength fails me; And the light of my eyes, even that has gone from me. My loved ones and my friends stand aloof from my plague; And my kinsmen stand afar off. Those who seek my life lay snares [for me;] And those who seek to injure me have threatened destruction, And they devise treachery all day long. But I, like a deaf man, do not hear; And [I am] like a mute man who does not open his mouth. Yes, I am like a man who does not hear, And in whose mouth are no arguments. For I hope in You, O LORD; You will answer, O Lord my God. For I said, "May they not rejoice over me, [Who,] when my foot slips, would magnify themselves against me." For I am ready to fall, And my sorrow is continually before me. For I confess my iniquity; I am full of anxiety because of my sin. But my enemies are vigorous [and] strong, And many are those who hate me wrongfully. And those who repay evil for good, They oppose me, because I follow what is good. Do not forsake me, O LORD; O my God, do not be far from me! Make haste to help me, O Lord, my salvation!

Jesus, the True Food & the True Drink

The food we eat goes into the mouth, into the stomach and out into the sewer; and yet, we constantly strive for such food, although we are never fully satisfied. The spiritual food we eat goes in through the eyes and hears, into the heart and out of the mouth: a spiritual digestive system. Jesus says, The eye is the lamp of the body; so then if your eye is clear, your whole body will be full of light. But if your eye is bad, your whole body will be full of darkness. If then the light that is in you is darkness, how great is the darkness! So, what we see resides in the body; similarily, Does not the ear test words, As the palate tastes its food? And, the things that proceed out of the mouth come from the heart, and those defile the man. Live in God; abide in Him for life, filling up with His Word, so that through His Word He would abide in us. He has given us assurance, Abide in Me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself unless it abides in the vine, so neither [can] you unless you abide in Me. I am the vine, you are the branches; he who abides in Me and I in him, he bears much fruit, for apart from Me you can do nothing. And, If you continue in My word, then you are truly disciples of Mine; and you will know the truth, and the truth will make you free. When we eat food, it fills up and gives strength for a short while; likewise, God's grace is our strength, our food for the spirit for ever. But when does food work best? When you are full or when you are empty? When you are hungry a piece of bread is like a steak! So it says, A satisfied soul loathes the honeycomb, But to a hungry soul every bitter thing is sweet. And similarily Christ says, My grace is sufficient for you, for power is perfected in weakness. So it is when we are empty, for God opposes the proud, but gives grace to the humble. Being proud is being full and God's grace is not useful to a proud soul, for he tramples over it; however, if the proud soul empties himself in humility, God's grace works best; a humble man is filled up, not with the air of himself, but with the grace of Christ; as the writter of Hebrews says, Do not be carried away by varied and strange teachings; for it is good for the heart to be strengthened by grace, not by foods, through which those who were so occupied were not benefited. We have an altar from which those who serve the tabernacle have no right to eat. For God's grace is truly our food, and the table of His altar is the cross. As athletes say, "I eat and drink the gym", meaning, "That's all I do." If an athlete competes, training in such a way for a crown that perishes, how much more we, "eat and drink Christ", for an eternal crown of life that never perishes? His testimony is true, Truly, truly, I say to you, unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood, you have no life in you. Whoever feeds on my flesh and drinks my blood has eternal life, and I will raise him up on the last day. For my flesh is true food, and my blood is true drink. Whoever feeds on my flesh and drinks my blood abides in me, and I in him.

How is it that Moses fasted for 40 days and 40 nights without food or water? Because he had nourishment from the Father, a nourishment that we know nothing about. When we eat food in this world, we eat something that has died: a plant that has been plucked, an animal that has been slain, or a piece of fruit that has been harvested. Everything that is cut off from its life undergoes death; afterwards, the food begins to rot and grow hairs of mold, being consumed slowly, since it is plucked from its life, since it is not comsumed by the living before its time; while it is on the tree, in the ground, or has the breath of life, it doesn't perish, for life preserves it. So if we live by eating the death of a thing, how much more do you suppose by the life of Christ, who is Life Himself? Everyone who eats on earth, eats death; but we who come heavenward, eat life. And He gives to us the righteousness by which we are truly satisfied; as He says, Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they shall be satisfied. And, “For we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand so that we would walk in them.” (Ephesians 2:10) And the righteousness of God is contrary to food which says, “All a man's labor is for his mouth and yet the appetite is not satisfied.” (Ecclesiastes 6:7) When an animal is sacrificed on the altar, the priest eats the meat of the sacrifice and gains strength for a short while, living by the strength of the meat; how much more do you suppose we live, who eat the Sacrifice of God, and the grace thereof, who eat the Lamb which has been slain, rather, who has been raised? If we know a person by their works, or a tree by its fruit, what type of tree do you discern Jesus resembles by His fruit, except the Tree of Life? As Moses prayed, Now therefore, I pray You, if I have found favor in Your sight, let me know Your ways that I may know You, so that I may find favor in Your sight. And by the fruit of Jesus Christ, by His work on the cross, we have been saved through the knowledge of Him, our God and our Savior, who is to us the Tree of Life, which yields food imperishable to everlasting life; he who knows the fruit knows the Tree and finds favor to life. He is to us the River of Living Water, that is, His Spirit, which is living drink that we may never thirst again. They who eat and drink the things of this world, eat and drinks death; but we who eat and drinks Christ, eat and drinks life. They who live by the things which are seen, which are wasting away, likewise waste away, for everything which is seen is perishing; but we who live by Him, who is not seen, will live forever; while we are wasting away, we are being renewed by the Spirit day by day, to be made more like Him and made worthy of eternal life to be revealed when He is revealed to the whole world. The things of this world, which seem to be living, when put alongside Him seem to be dead, for He is exceedingly living; seek and search Him, living Life, above! We who sow to the Spirit reap life, and the mind set on the Spirit is life and peace.

See a tremendous thing: He has given us a tangible act of communion with Him in which we eat bread, which is His body, and drink wine, which is His blood. Hence, food and grace again. And we are commanded to do communion as often as we like so that we do not forget His gracious work on the cross; for we have come to know the truth through understanding, so let us not forget, for forgetfulness renders our knowledge void. The festivals and holy days prescribed by the Law were commanded on set days; these are all fulfilled in Christ, and He has set up another day which is "as often as you like"; so it is not on a certain day like the law, but as often as we like. For the law given by Moses was binding by compulsion, but the law of Christ has set us free by willingness, that we may offer to Him in a willing heart. The holy days in the Law offered as reminders at the anniversary of an event; but communion is not given on a day, but any day; likewise it says, "Today if you hear His voice..." (Hebrews 3:15) And, "AT THE ACCEPTABLE TIME I LISTENED TO YOU, AND ON THE DAY OF SALVATION I HELPED YOU." Behold, now is "THE ACCEPTABLE TIME," behold, now is "THE DAY OF SALVATION" For His favor is available everyday for us to abide, if only we are willing; we should fear always, lest we harden ourselves toward God, being ignorant of His ways, erring in our heart and out of the way. Remember His ways; declare His works. Remember Christ, the Way. This is the key to reamin: remember. And the key to abiding: not forgetting. God's grace is a gift to us, but to the Father, costly. He gave up His Son; He lost Him for a short while; He gained Him back forever. Do not receive God's grace in vain by forgetting these wonderful truths, but treat them as precious and hold fast to them. When faced with death, I will not shake; I know Him that makes the earth-quake; hold fast dearly beloved, hold fast; for in vain it does not say, You have not yet resisted to bloodshed, striving against sin. And, And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony, and they did not love their lives to the death.

The Spirit and the Bride says, "Come!" All come! As it says, Come, everyone who thirsts, come to the waters; and he who has no money, come, buy and eat! Come, buy wine and milk without money and without price. Why do you spend your money for that which is not bread, and your labor for that which does not satisfy? Listen diligently to me, and eat what is good, and delight yourselves in rich food. Incline your ear, and come to me; hear, that your soul may live; and I will make with you an everlasting covenant, my steadfast, sure love for David. And, "Many waters cannot quench love, Nor will rivers overflow it; If a man were to give all the riches of his house for love, It would be utterly despised." Jesus, the Bronze Serpent

God sent fiery serpents to punish the people in the wilderness for their sins, then gave them provision through His grace so that they would be rescued from the sting of death; as it states, Then the Lord sent fiery serpents among the people and they bit the people, so that many people of Israel died. So the people came to Moses and said, “We have sinned, because we have spoken against the Lord and against you; intercede with the Lord, that He will remove the serpents from us.” And Moses interceded for the people. Then the Lord said to Moses, “Make a fiery serpent, and put it on a flag pole; and it shall come about, that everyone who is bitten, and looks at it, will live.” So Moses made a bronze serpent and put it on the flag pole; and it came about, that if a serpent bit someone, and he looked at the bronze serpent, he lived. God did not remove the serpents as the people asked, but He opened up a way by grace through which the people could believe in His Word and look upon the bronze serpent in faith and be saved. It's through faith since they had to look at the serpent and believe that by so doing, they would be saved; they were hoping for what they did not yet have, namely salvation, for they were dying from the sting of the serpents. The bronze serpent was made of bronze and so was the altar in the courtyard of God's tent, showing us it's through sacrifice we enter into God's sancturary; there is no other way, only through sacrifice; as Jesus confirms, Just as Moses lifted up the snake in the desert, the Son of Man must also be lifted up. So that everyone who believes can have eternal life in him. God saved the Israelites from the string of the serpents through the bronze image of the same serpent; the image is dead, but they hoped and believed it would give life. The living serpents were the ones that stung to death, but this one bronze serpent was dead, for it was an image, but supposed to bring life; so the thing which was alive bit them to death, but the thing which is dead was to give life. They had to believe the dead image would give life, and by God's promise it did! The ones who looked upon the bronze serpent lived. And by looking upon Christ's death on a cross, lifted up, we will have life, but not His death alone, but rather, His resurrection. For if Christ remained dead, we would be no better than a medium who consults the dead, we would be no better than an idolater who worships a dead image; as it says, Should not a people inquire of their God? Why consult the dead on behalf of the living? But the Israelites who came later looked upon the image of life to death, for they considered the image more important than God; as it says, the bronze serpent that Moses had made, for until those days the sons of Israel burned incense to it; and it was called Nehushtan. Likewise certain people today worship the symbol of the cross as god rather than God who gives life to all things; He chooses to give life to an image in the wilderness, and He chooses to give life through Christ. And Christ, who was dead, is now alive, so consult God through Him. And I beheld, and, lo, in the midst of the throne and of the four beasts, and in the midst of the elders, stood a Lamb as it had been slain As He says, I am He who lives, and was dead, and behold, I am alive forevermore. Amen. And I have the keys of Hades and of Death.

God commanded the people not to make an image and bow down to worship it; but here, God commanded Moses to make an image of a fiery serpent, the same serpent in which the people feared because of its sting, but it is only sin if it is worshiped. For in the days of Solomon, Solomon made bronze oxen and lions, but the people did not worship them; this was by the commandment of God through David to make the oxen. The representation or image is not the same as the thing it represents, for whenever an image is made, life is taken from it; an image is made by the skill of man where-as God creates all things; thus, man creates copies of what God creates, for an image is only a copy, a replica. So therefore, God sent the visible image of Himself, Jesus, into the world, being the Son of God, being in the exact likeness of the Father God, to be ransom to whoever-so will look upon Him for salvation; for in Him dwells the fullness of the Diety in bodily form; He emptied Himself to take on the form of man to rescue those who are made in the image of God; as it says, Therefore, since the children share in flesh and blood, He Himself likewise also partook of the same, that through death He might render powerless him who had the power of death, that is, the devil, and might free those who through fear of death were subject to slavery all their lives. For Jesus became man to rescue mankind; He died a death to rescue from death; and the bronze image was a serpent to save from the serpents. Now, just as a man seems mad when he utters his dream, so it is with explaining these things; these are all parables pointing to Christ, a picture, an image of Him. Through these pictures of Him, which are in the mind by hearing God's Word, we can see Christ. These are not Christ in themselves, but only images, pictures, by which we see Christ, by which we are saved if we have faith; as He says, How shall we picture the kingdom of God, or by what parable shall we present it? So as they were saved through the image of a serpent, we are saved through parables, similitudes, through the testimonies of God concerning His Son. You see?

The representation on the stick which Moses lifted up was a serpent; and the one that has been rendered powerless by the cross is satan, the serpent, that ancient dragon of old. For although Christ was lifted up on the cross, He did not suffer defeat by the cross, but the serpent was defeated; for Christ was raised to new life, and it was the serpent who suffered the head wound. So, this is why we see a serpent lifted up during the time of Israel's wanderings in the desert; for Christ was glorified through the cross, and satan was defeated by the cross. We may be full of fear looking upon Christ on the cross because of our great sin and God's wrath toward sin, but we must look and trust God that if we look upon Jesus Christ and Him crucified, we will be saved from sin and death; we will surely live. I want to look away, I see my failings, I see my fault; I see death, and I am uncomfortable; I see my burden of sin, my punishment for sin; I see Jesus for me; I see how great His love. I see deliverance from sin; He has made me right. Now, we will all die one day, but those who die in Christ sleep and will awake on the last day. Everyone dies; but He who looks upon Jesus, through the eyes of faith, when he dies, he sleeps; for he is alive to God by faith, having loved Him although not seeing Him; the one who loves God is known by Him; and the one known by Him is His child; such a one will be raised up on the last day to eternal life as promised. The eyes of faith are sharpened through hearing God's Word; and our faith in Him who is unseen should be as strong as if we have seen Him; as Paul said to the Galatians, For the meaning of Jesus Christ's death was made as clear to you as if you had seen a picture of his death on the cross. Faith is the conviction of things unseen and the assurance of things hoped for. Don't be led astray by seeking for images of Christ and thinking that is faith - it is not. For faith comes from hearing and hearing the Word of God; we walk by faith, not by sight, so faith and sight are opposites. Faith is from hearing God's Word; if God is gracious to us, perhaps we may see a miracle, increasing our faith; however, it's unbelieving to seek for such miracles and signs, for everything we need is already given. There is a great blessing to us who believe without seeing; saying, Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed.

We should not be distressed or angry with ourselves on account of Christ's death because it was according to God's predetermined plan; also, He was raised from the dead and He no longer suffers death. Rather we should fear God, knowing the penalty for our sins that He Himself bore; we should hate the sin which crushed Him, for when we sinned, He was bruised; He did this on account of His great love towards us, to rescue us from bondage. Joseph, the son of Jacob, prophesied to Christ with his life; for he was sold into slavery to Egypt by his own brothers. However, before these things occurred, God gave Joseph a dream of his brothers bowing down to him; at that time, the meaning of the dream was hidden from his eyes. But we see through the words of Joseph that it was God's good plan to send him to Egypt, to preserve life, not to destroy; as it is written, I am your brother, Joseph, whom you sold into Egypt. And now do not be distressed or angry with yourselves because you sold me here, for God sent me before you to preserve life. For the famine has been in the land these two years, and there are yet five years in which there will be neither plowing nor harvest. And God sent me before you to preserve for you a remnant on earth, and to keep alive for you many survivors. So it was not you who sent me here, but God. And so likewise, Christ was betrayed by His own brethren, the Jews, and they turned Him over to the Gentiles to be crucified - all foretold by God beforehand, but the meaning was hidden. It was God's predetermined plan, having testified beforehand by the Psalms and the Prophets, by acts of the men of old, for prophets also performed acts before the people. God sent Jesus for our good, to save life rather than to destroy. He left witness for us to receive Christ by faith in these latter days, having also preserved the testimonies of the apostles who seen firsthand the risen Lord. Likewise, Isaiah wrote of Christ, saying, But the LORD was pleased To crush Him, putting [Him] to grief; If He would render Himself [as] a guilt offering, He will see [His] offspring, He will prolong [His] days, And the good pleasure of the LORD will prosper in His hand. "He was pleased to crush Him", for it was God's good plan to send Christ into the world to die for us. So although we hate ourselves because of our sins, what it has done to Christ, we find power in the reality that Christ is alive, seated at the right hand of Power on High, that it was according to His plan. Even if our eyes may be veiled, they become unvieled when we turn to the Lord. Therefore, unveil your eyes and see that your Brother is now alive, second in command, mediator between you and God, that He is very much alive and your guilt has been removed; for Joseph's brothers had guilt as long as they thought Joseph was dead, but the moment they seen Joseph alive, their guilt was removed and their hands cleansed from his blood. See now, that Jesus is for you, and does not seek revenge now because of your transgressions that He bore, but even intercedes on your behalf before the Father? So prove yourself innocent in the matter by fleeing to Him for refuge in the heavenly places where He is seated, setting your mind on things above, not on things below, so that we may see that faith is truly sight indeed.

Jesus, the Victor

God sent Samson to Israel to be a judge. He was a mighty judge in the land of Israel, killing many of their enemies, the Philistines. Before he was born, His parents were given the command not to eat anything from the grapevine and have no razor put to his head, for his strength was his Head, that is to say Christ. He killed many of the Philistines, even once with the jawbone of a donkey, striking down a thousand men. But later, He was decieved and tricked by the one he loved, and she shaved off the seven locks of his head, and the LORD who gave him his strength left; then he was subdued by his enemies, they gouged out his eyes and put him into prision, where his hair began to grow again. The time came when the Philistines, merry with wine, called for Samson to make a spectacle of him since he was their enemy, but Samson prayed to the Lord; as it is written, Then Samson called to the Lord, saying, “O Lord God, remember me, I pray! Strengthen me, I pray, just this once, O God, that I may with one blow take vengeance on the Philistines for my two eyes!” And Samson took hold of the two middle pillars which supported the temple, and he braced himself against them, one on his right and the other on his left. Then Samson said, “Let me die with the Philistines!” And he pushed with all his might, and the temple fell on the lords and all the people who were in it. So the dead that he killed at his death were more than he had killed in his life. So Samson made a spectacle of them, by destroying them.

And Jesus, having removed many demons from people who were oppressed, subduing them, freeing them who were held captive by satan; at the end of his life was betrayed by Judas, His beloved friend, and turned over to the Gentiles for mockery; just as it was written in the prophet's Psalm, Even my close friend in whom I trusted, Who ate my bread, Has lifted up his heel against me. He was crucified, and made a public spectacle of by the Romans as they called a legion of soldiers to formation and mocked Him publically and beat Him; then, making a spectacle of Him through the cross, lifted Him up for all to see; the written charge against Him, "JESUS THE NAZARENE, KING OF THE JEWS". But it was in His death, that He disarmed more of the enemies of satan than He did in His life, defeating satan and rendering him powerless. For the power of a lion is in its mouth, for it has sharp and powerful teeth, and the power of satan is in his lies; but we have been set free by the Truth of God, namely Christ Jesus, the righteousness of God, received by us by faith - in Him, there is no condemnation; for if we walk according to the Spirit, we are no longer under law, but grace, and in Him, there is no sin; so while we are in Him, we are free from sin, for where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom. While the rulers of this world thought they were destroying Him, they were destroying themselves, being cast down. And later, as prophesied, the accusations of the enemy will also cease; as the Revelation says, Now the salvation, and the power, and the kingdom of our God and the authority of His Christ have come, for the accuser of our brethren has been thrown down, he who accuses them before our God day and night. And they overcame him because of the blood of the Lamb and because of the word of their testimony, and they did not love their life even when faced with death. For this reason, rejoice, O heavens and you who dwell in them. Woe to the earth and the sea, because the devil has come down to you, having great wrath, knowing that he has [only] a short time. And the prophesy also describes satan's rule ending, and Christ's kingdom coming. For Jesus fought the battle for us and we enter into His victory; for our God is for us, doing on our behalf, fighting for us. Just as the Israelites entered into the Promised Land by the mighty hand of God, so it is with us: we enter by His powerful arm. Other gods require that you do something for them, stand them up, feed them, dust them off, but they are no gods at all; the one and only God, does on our behalf, waging war for us, defeating our foes. He defeated them through the cross and we enter into His victory through faith. If we dwell in lies, being deceived or deceiving ourselves, we are under oppression, but the truth makes free.

Likewise, Haman was an enemy of the Jews who sought to kill innocent Mordecai and with him, the Jewish nation. He payed money to the king to establish a decree: on a certain day, all Jews would be killed. Furthermore, he had set up a pole by his home to impale Mordecai on it; and what was preventing him? He needed the permission of the king. Before all this, Mordecai saved the same king from being killed by spies; while Haman went to go ask the king for permission to impale Mordecai, the king discovered from the book of the Chronicles that Mordecai saved the king; so the king asked Haman while he came in to ask for Mordecai's death, "What should be done to the one whom the king is pleased", and Haman thinking it was for himself gave a favorable answer. The king then told him to do all which he said unto Mordecai, the Jew, his enemy. So the good Haman intended for himself went to Mordecai, his enemy. And later at a banquet, Ester, the Queen, who was a Jew and the neice of Mordecai, spoke of Haman's decree to kill the Jews which was approved by the king in ignorance, for he did not know the Queen was a Jew, for she kept her nationality hidden. And the king was furious because Haman sought to kill the Queen and he had Haman instead killed, impalled on the pole which he set up for Mordecai, for it was told to him of Haman's plan. So the evil Haman intended for Mordecai, the innocent one, was poured out on his own head. Then another decree was setup that allowed for the irrevocable decree previously made to be fought against: namely, on the day the Jews were to be slaughtered, they could defend themselves. To this day, the Jewish nation celebrates this as the Day of Purim as a testimony, which means "lots", because it was by lot that day was chosen.

Now, Christ looked to be defeated on the cross, but He was made weak willingly; moments before His crucifiction He testified, Do you think I cannot call on my Father, and he will at once put at my disposal more than twelve legions of angels? But how then would the Scriptures be fulfilled that say it must happen in this way? Also He says, The reason the Father loves Me is that I lay down My life in order to take it up again. No one takes it from Me, but I lay it down of My own accord. I have authority to lay it down and authority to take it up again. This charge I have received from My Father. If the rulers of this world would have known what they were doing, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory; but their minds were darkened. The rulers of the people wanted for themselves the inheritance, the kingdom of Israel (as with the parable of the vinyard written about in Matthew 21:33-46); but by the cross, Christ suffered only a wound to His heel, and satan to his head (for we wrestle not against flesh and blood). We know that Christ did not remain in the grave forever, but only three days; and the judgment upon the ruler of this world is pronounced and has been executed. For who was really defeated by the cross? If satan was judged at the fall, and was made to grovel on his belly, how much more is he rendered powerless through the cross of Christ? When the rulers of this world made a public display of Him, He made a public display of them through the cross; as Paul writes, When you were dead in your transgressions and the uncircumcision of your flesh, He made you alive together with Him, having forgiven us all our transgressions, having canceled out the certificate of debt consisting of decrees against us, which was hostile to us; and He has taken it out of the way, having nailed it to the cross. When He had disarmed the rulers and authorities, He made a public display of them, having triumphed over them through the cross. So we see, the good the enemy intended for himself was given to Christ; and the evil the enemy intended for Christ was unto himself. As with Haman, he was impalled on the tree, not Mordecai - likewise, it was the devil who was defeated by the cross, that was the judgment. However, it was Christ who was crucified, but He now lives forevermore. The devil's final end is decreed in the Book. The glory and honor which the enemy wanted for himself went to Christ, the King of Kings and the Lord of Lords; the jugement which the enemy intended for Christ was poured out upon his own head. Now judgment is upon this world; now the ruler of this world will be cast out. And I, if I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all men to Myself. And, concerning judgment, because the ruler of this world has been judged.

The devil now has no hold on you who believe, and his lies have no power in you who belong to Christ Jesus; fight against him with the Sword of the Mouth of God; and your defense of armor is from the Lord, your offensive weapon from the Lord; and you, a warrior in the kingdom of God - therefore, fight! For He has fought for us. He gives us the Spirit that we may defend ourselves against the law of sin and death. Does it mean he no longer loves us if we have trouble or calamity, or are persecuted, or hungry, or destitute, or in danger, or threatened with death? (As the Scriptures say, "For your sake we are killed every day; we are being slaughtered like sheep.") No, despite all these things, overwhelming victory is ours through Christ, who loved us.

Jesus, the Son of God

Jesus is the Son of God; someone may say, "What does that mean?" We have a proverb that says, "Like mother like daughter; like father like son." So it is with Jesus, the Son of God; He is like the Father God, and perfectly so; and so much so, He says, Whoever has seen Me has seen the Father. How can you say, "Show us the Father"? Do you not believe that I am in the Father and the Father is in Me? The words I speak are not My own, but My Father who lives in Me does His work through Me. And, The Son is the visible image of the invisible God, the firstborn over all creation. And who is it that is called Everlasting Father? For a child will be born to us, a son will be given to us; And the government will rest on His shoulders; And His name will be called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Eternal Father, Prince of Peace. And what was the work the Father did in Christ? in Christ God was reconciling the world to himself, not counting their trespasses against them, and entrusting to us [apostles] the message of reconciliation. He demonstrated His love for us on the cross, not while we were good people, but while we were yet sinners. For He is gracious to the ungrateful and the evil And, For He makes His sun rise on the evil and on the good, and sends rain on the just and on the unjust. If this is how God treats His enemies, how much more kindly does He deal with them who have been reconciled to Himself through the cross of Christ? By the mercies of Him, go to Him! Negligence will be wrath in the end. For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of His Son, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by His life.

When Abraham offered His son Isaac on the altar to God, by God's own command, not that this thought came from God, but the enemy planted it; as He says, And have built the high places of Baal to burn their sons in the fire as burnt offerings to Baal, which I did not command or decree, nor did it come into My mind. So the thought did not come from God, but the command did; all such thoughts come from the enemy, for God is good, and nothing evil ever crosses His mind. However, when Abraham offered His son Isaac on the altar in obedience, God used it to test Abraham and point to Christ Jesus to come; as it says, In your seed all the nations of the earth shall be blessed, because you have obeyed My voice. When it says, "in your seed", it does not say, "seeds", as with many, but one, which is Christ; so because of the obedience of one man, comes a blessing to the whole world; and that blessing which was promised, came from Christ, the Seed to come; and Christ, being one, blesses the whole world by His one act of obedience. When Abraham was going to sacrifice his son, God provided another sacrifice in place of his son, a ram caught in the thicket; so Abraham received his son back from the dead; as it says, He [Abraham] considered that God is able to raise people even from the dead, from which he also received him [Isaac] back as a type. And Abraham, being a prophet, saw this was going to happen beforehand and said, God will provide for Himself the lamb for the burnt offering, my son. And, Stay here with the donkey, and I and the lad will go over there; and we will worship and return to you. Also thereafter, looking in the future to Christ, Abraham said, Abraham called the name of that place The LORD Will Provide, as it is said to this day, "In the mount of the LORD it will be provided." No-one took Christ's life, but He laid it down willingly, as Isaac did; Christ laid down His life as commanded by the Father. And it is not unrighteousness on God's behalf, for Christ was handed over to death for our sakes, once for all, as the propitiation for our sins. Everything Christ did was as if the Father was doing it through Him, for He does the commands of the Father perfectly, resembling His exact likeness.

And Jacob, who came from Isaac, who came from Abraham, also let go of his only son, although he had many other sons, he had one and only son from his beloved wife Rachel: Benjamin. Jacob had two sons from Rachel, but to him, Joseph was dead, although he was only in Egypt. But Jacob let Benjamin, his only beloved son, go into the hands of his brothers to save his family from the famine, for they were going to Egypt to buy food. So just as Jacob gave up his one and only son Benjamin and gained him back from the dead along with another, Joseph, the Fahter God gave up His Son, Jesus, to receive Him back again from the dead with many other descendents; as it says, But the LORD was pleased To crush Him, putting Him to grief; If He would render Himself as a guilt offering, He will see His offspring, He will prolong His days, And the good pleasure of the LORD will prosper in His hand. And, Behold, I and the children whom the LORD has given me are for signs and wonders in Israel from the LORD of hosts, who dwells on Mount Zion. For we who believe have been made sons of God through faith in Jesus Christ, being made alive together with Him; just as the apostle says, even when we were dead in our transgressions, made us alive together with Christ (by grace you have been saved) And, When you were dead in your transgressions and the uncircumcision of your flesh, He made you alive together with Him, having forgiven us all our transgressions And later, Jacob considered Joseph's two sons, Ephriam and Manasseh, his own; likewise God sent His Son into the world and gained many more descendants through Him, even by His descendants amongst many nations of the world, even to the offspring of His offspring, those begotten by the Word of Truth.

The Father grafts us, even Gentiles, into God's household, the body of Christ, through faith in Jesus; He makes us sons of God by the Spirit of His Son, conforming us into His likeness. Now, the body preforms the acts of the mind, the head, and Christ is our Head; a member that goes rouge has lost connection with the Head, as it says, Let no one keep defrauding you of your prize by delighting in self-abasement and the worship of the angels, taking his stand on visions he has seen, inflated without cause by his fleshly mind, and not holding fast to the head, from whom the entire body, being supplied and held together by the joints and ligaments, grows with a growth which is from God. So we see our victory through Christ's blood and abiding in Christ: setting our mind on the things of the Spirit. For if we set our minds on spiritual things, the members of our bodies will likewise conform to the message of our head. Our hand does what the mind says, and our foot does what the mind says, even to every finger and every toe. So if we set our mind on spiritual things, namely Christ, our Head, He will give us strength to present our members as servents to righteouness (Romans 8:5-8, Philippians 4:8, Colossians 3:2). And this is the way of faith: setting our mind on Jesus.

Now, a son conforms Himself to the pattern of the father, for a son does the things that the father does; as it says, By this we may know that we are in him: whoever says he abides in Him ought to walk in the same way in which he walked. So let us not be decieved: if we act like a devil, we show ourselves sons of the devil, but if we pattern ourselves after God, we show ourselves truly sons of God (John 8:30-47). This proves us and gives us confidence on the day of Salvation, and we prove ourselves constantly in many watchings and prayer. We really are sons of God if we have believed, and true sons pattern themselves after the Father; and we have been begotten of God by the eternal Word of truth at faith. When a son does not act like the father, the father says, "You are no son of mine", although, in truth, this is their son; but what they are really meaning is, "You were raised better; you're not acting as you've been taught"; that is, you're not acting like your father. A son can rebel against the father and go his own way (Luke 15:11-32), but the son can never stop being a son; but, in such a state, the son is dead to the father, and the father to the son. It's not how we start that matters, but how we finish. And Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob received the testimony being "alive to God" even while dead, for they died in faith. Do not be deceived, if we die in our sins, even after being made sons, we will be condemned, for God shows no favoritism. Do you not know that God first rescued His firstborn Israel from Egypt by a mighty hand, and then later, when they hardened their necks and kicked against Him for years, rebelling, He handed them over to the Babylonians and the Assyrians, even defiling His own temple on their account? God is not mocked; we will reap what we sow. We were once slaves of sin, but have now been made sons of God; and if sons, heirs of the promise; so why would we willingly nullfiy the work wroght in us by God, hating our own selves by our own works when God's work is for us? For Jesus has loved us, even more than Himself, giving up Him body for us; so therefore, As obedient children, do not be conformed to the former lusts which were yours in your ignorance, but like the Holy One who called you, be holy yourselves also in all your behavior; because it is written, "YOU SHALL BE HOLY, FOR I AM HOLY." If you address as Father the One who impartially judges according to each one's work, conduct yourselves in fear during the time of your stay on earth; knowing that you were not redeemed with perishable things like silver or gold from your futile way of life inherited from your forefathers, but with precious blood, as of a lamb unblemished and spotless, the blood of Christ.

Finally, Christ gave the good testimony before Pontius Pilate even though he had power to crucify Him and also power to let Him go; still Christ stood for the truth and testified to the truth - no one takes His life, but He laid it down willingly. He said to the people numerous times, "I came from the Father", "the Father and I are One". And when the time came, the very hour which He came for, He confirmed His testimony, even with His own blood; as prophesied by Job, And the indictment which my adversary has written, surely I would carry it on my shoulder, I would bind it to myself like a crown. For Jesus was blameless, but the indictment which the adversary had written against Him, JESUS OF NAZARETH, THE KING OF THE JEWS And Christ bore on His shoulder the cross, and on His head He wore the crown of thorns; and He went to the cross, confirming His testimony in His own blood. This is what it means when it is said, "This is the one who came by water and blood. There are three that testify: the Spirit, the water, and the blood." (1 John 5:6-9) He was blameless, even Pilate could find fault in Him for he was determined to release Him, but the accusation they had written against Him, He affirmed as true, even though it meant death. Only the new creation will inherit the kingdom of God - flesh and blood cannot enter. In the beginning, God created the present world and humanity and all flesh in six days; He will also create the new heaven and the new earth, which He has promised, making all things new, as a place where righteousness dwells; for this present heavens and earth are corrupt and everything rots and decays over time because of the dysfunction of the fallen human nature and the death & destruction that comes with it; but the new heavens and the new earth will be a place that does not decay nor wear out like a garment, a place where righteousness dwells: permanent, forever. The present world is predominantly evil since this world belongs to satan, but the world to come, belongs to Christ and God, and righteousness will only dwell there. Just as God did away with the ancient world with a flood and afterward only Noah and his family (who were righteous) survived, so it will be when God floods the world again - with fire - preserving the righteous who will inherit the new earth. Just as Noah went into the arc and got out of the arc and the world was like new, for it had all been washed away with water, so it will be after the fire burns the earth and the heavens, it will be rendered new there-after. For the present earth underwent a flood and wiped away the wicked, but Noah was caught up, floating above the water in the arc - at the end, only Noah and his family were saved, so only righteousness was on the earth after that time (for a short while, for sin began to reign again shortly after). It was like the world was made new after the flood and Noah inherited it. This is a picture of how the end will be, but with a more severe end - of fire - and a more better beginning - an unending one. It is by the things that have happened in the past that we can see the future; as it says, Let them bring forth and show us what will happen; Let them show the former things, what they were, That we may consider them, And know the latter end of them; Or declare to us things to come. And, But as the days of Noah were, so also will the coming of the Son of Man be.

The first of the new creation is Jesus Christ, who is the firstfruits of the resurrection, the firstfruits of the new creation; the new creation is not of the will of man, but of the will of God. Everything of the old is passing away and only the work of the new will remain; we who are created anew in Christ Jesus will inherit all new things. In the beginning, God created everything that we see by His Word and now, He creates a new creation by the same Word, a creation that we do not presently see which begins in human hearts; as it is written, For God, who said, “Light shall shine out of darkness,” is the One who has shone in our hearts to give the Light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Christ. Paul correlates the first creation with the new creation - which starts in human hearts, which finishes with the new heaven and the new earth. If the old were perfect, then there would be no reason for a new; but the old is imperfect and there is fault with it; as Christ testifies, And from the days of John the Baptist until now the kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and the violent take it by force. We know that the earth is fallen, but this Scripture suggests that the present heaven has faults, albeit it is God's abode. The apostle says, For we do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this age, against spiritual hosts of wickedness in the heavenly places. So it is necessary that the heavens be destroyed too, so that all may be made new. First came the first-begotten from God, Jesus, who was the first to preach this new covenant, which creates anew by its testimony the new creation to everyone who believes unto a living hope, a new life, an eternal dwelling with God in a new heaven and a new earth; these coming last are by no means lesser, but greater, just as John preached first, then came Him who he pointed to - and He being much greater than any prophet or angel, being the Father Himself among us, Jesus, the angel of God's presence.

We are God's house. God's building, being built up and edified as living stones into a spiritual house, the new Jerusalem that will come out of heaven in which we will dwell as a holy priesthood, a chosen race, a peculiar people for God's own possession; for she is our mother, she bears us as we inhabit her; as the Truth living in Peter says, And coming to Him as to a living stone which has been rejected by men, but is choice and precious in the sight of God, you also, as living stones, are being built up as a spiritual house for a holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ. And, but the Jerusalem above is free, which is the mother of us all. In the Revelation of Jesus Christ, the wall of the city of the new Jerusalem has twelve foundation stones, precious stones, written on them the names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb. So, quite literally, stones are set in place to make a dwelling in which God dwells. Presently, we being the church, He inhabits us, making us into a temple of the living God in which He dwells; but then, quite literally, we will dwell in the city and our names on the stones of the new Jerusalem, Mount Zion, and we will walk with God: He will be our God and we will be His people. He promised to give us who overcome a white stone written on it a new name that no one knows except the one who receives it. I believe this stone will be used in the building of the wall of the new Jerusalem which we will be granted to enter - our names written in the Book of Life, our names written on the walls of the new city which comes from heaven.
