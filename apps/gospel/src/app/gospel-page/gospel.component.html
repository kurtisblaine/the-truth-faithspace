The Danger - Futility

Futility of Pleasure

Alcohol and food in excess, which are perverted by the flesh into drunkenness and gluttony, and drugs used for pleasure as an escape from reality, and entertainment which is vain, and any other pleasure which come from our lust, do not sustain us - they only mask the deeply rooted issue we have and make life bearable for us only for a short time. It is like hitting the snooze button on an alarm clock: you feel better now, but you will have to deal with the same problem later, but it has gotten worse. Even if we are 'satisified' or 'fulfilled' with these things, it would never be enough: it is like we are feeding a beast within us, the more we feed it, the stronger and bigger it gets.
See more about the nature of sin here.
We eat food, but we are not content; we drink, but we are not sustained; we eat, but we are hungry again later; we drink and become thirsty again. No matter how much the eye sees, it is not enough; no matter how much the ear hears, it is not content. If these pleasures truly make us happy, why wouldn't we express it outwardly? If we were truly happy, shouldn't our happiness resemble a child's happiness? Children are overcome with laughter and express joyfulness and run around expressing their happiness, jumping with joy. If these things truly make us happy, we surely would show it. Most often we are chained by such things: we look miserable outwardly, but inwardly, we become less miserable. These only mask our pain for a short while. In the beginning, we used to be happy when sin wasn't alive, in the innocence of our childhood, but later, sin came alive and we died. All of us have become enslaved to our own desires which corrupt; for all lust corrupts. This leads to a cycle of dependence; and we think we need these things to keep us in check: "I don't feel normal without them", we think. I've been chained by many things for years of my life and looking back at my experience, surely that was not life. Whatever a person is overcome by, that he is also enslaved. I was enslaved to everything a man could be ensalved by: food, alcohol, drugs, & many others. I couldn't get out. It was dark and cold: loveless. I wanted to get out, but I couldn't: it was surely a jail cell. I wanted to stop my sinning, but couldn't; I wanted out, but couldn't redeem myself. Hope, I had none. "Where is real pleasure and satisfaction?", I pondered. And worse yet, what I experienced I didn't know how to utter it; for perhaps if I could tell someone, they could help? But instead, guilt and shame keep me from sharing, as if I were the only one on the planet dealing with such things. Just as Solomon, who had great wisdom from God, wrote, All things are full of weariness;a man cannot utter it; the eye is not satisfied with seeing, nor the ear filled with hearing.
Solomon in his wisdom wrote, I said in my heart, “Come now, I will test you with pleasure; enjoy yourself.” But behold, this also was vanity. I said of laughter, “It is mad,” and of pleasure, “What use is it?” I searched with my heart how to cheer my body with wine—my heart still guiding me with wisdom—and how to lay hold on folly, till I might see what was good for the children of man to do under heaven during the few days of their life. (...) And whatever my eyes desired I did not keep from them. I kept my heart from no pleasure, for my heart found pleasure in all my toil, and this was my reward for all my toil. Then I considered all that my hands had done and the toil I had expended in doing it, and behold, all was vanity and a striving after wind, and there was nothing to be gained under the sun. Wisdom is insight, speaking of the things which are from within with the correct words. Everything begins with lust; and how computer technology entices the eyes! It is satisfying while on it, but afterwards, it's like waking from a sleep. We live in an age where media and screens are everywhere: it started with televisions in the living room then escalated to monitors in our pockets. These surely feed our eyes. A computer can create entire simulations of this world for people to live a new life inside. Can the copy ever be as good as the real? Which do you like better: name brand goods or knock offs? Yes, knock offs are cheaper and require less of us, but they are not better, even in the slightest. In the same way, these simulations are cheaper: you can sit on the couch and be at a sports game!

The natural use is better than the unnatural; the real is better than the copy; good is better than evil, even in the meanwhile if you think, "Ah! It feels so good", in the end you will not be thinking that! Everything in which we sow, we will also reap. A person does not sow an apple and get an orange tree; neither does a person breed pigs and get cows; so why do we think, "I will sow corruption and get life"? Everything corrupts because of sin; and all sin is dysfunctional; and a dysfunction is useless: a new sofa will become fettered, a new car will be a tin of rust, even our bodies are becoming old and gray and worn out and will soon be in the grave. Evil will inherit evil in the end and goodness will inherit goodness. This is true, even if, in the meanwhile, we see the wicked prospering and the righteous suffering; everyone will get what their deeds deserve in the end because God will cause His righteous judgement to be upheld. Take heed! But she who lives in pleasure is dead while she lives.

Futility in Toil

Our lives are filled with work and toil. We work and then sleep to do it again the next day. Our days are short, yet we spend them in much pain and agony. We work for things that will not matter in the future; we work for objectives that are not ours; they will not matter years from now as everything will become old and outdated. In the future, people will look back and not care what we are presently doing, just as we presently do not care what happened in times past. We strive and labor and don't receive our due wages; he who does little work, harvests much fruit. We work hard and it seems there are holes in our pockets. We plant and the locusts eat; we plant much, but gather little. Who has dealt this tragic existence on the human race? And I set my mind to seek and explore by wisdom concerning all that has been done under heaven. It is a grievous task which God has given to the sons of men to be afflicted with. We were born into travail by God, so that we would search and seek for Him: for He afflicts and He also heals. All our days are stricken with evil. We re-live the same things over and over like a rat in a maze; this would be okay, if our lives were pleasant, but they're not. We don't know where we are going; we don't remember where we have come from; we aren't fully aware even of today - we're in a box without the ability to look out. And even if we had 1000 lives of 1000 years, we wouldn't be able to discover all that is around us - how much less the things we don't see! We chase things that we think will satisfy us, but we end up forever chasing them without ever catching up to them. Are we truly better off than the animals? It says, For what happens to the sons of men also happens to animals; one thing befalls them: as one dies, so dies the other. Surely, they all have one breath; man has no advantage over animals, for all is vanity.
Everything that we work for, we do not keep. We strive for beauty, but it is fading away. Our possessions will become the possessions of another. Even our own lives are borrowed, for we will return to the dust. King Solomon wrote towards the end of his life, I hated all my toil in which I toil under the sun, seeing that I must leave it to the man who will come after me, and who knows whether he will be wise or a fool? Yet he will be master of all for which I toiled and used my wisdom under the sun. This also is vanity. So I turned about and gave my heart up to despair over all the toil of my labors under the sun, because sometimes a person who has toiled with wisdom and knowledge and skill must leave everything to be enjoyed by someone who did not toil for it. This also is vanity and a great evil. What has a man from all the toil and striving of heart with which he toils beneath the sun? For all his days are full of sorrow, and his work is a vexation. Even in the night his heart does not rest. This also is vanity. Futility is worthlessness; it is emptiness, not full; aimless, not having a point or direction, wandering about in blackest darkness; not seeing; doing much, but accomplishing nothing; speaking much, but getting nothing done; living, yet for what?

Life is short, but it feels long. In life, we despair; without it, there is no hope. We spend our lives wasting on things that will pass away. We are passing away. In the place of the dead, where we are going, there is nothing: no toil, no labor, no speech, no sight, nothing; it is a void. Yes, the Most High God ordained for us this life because of the disobedient sin of Adam and Eve in the garden; all peoples on earth come from them and we inherit their evil; here is the judgement He pronounced upon them: Cursed is the ground because of you; In toil you will eat of it all the days of your life. Both thorns and thistles it shall grow for you; And you will eat the plants of the field; By the sweat of your face You will eat bread, Till you return to the ground, Because from it you were taken; For you are dust, And to dust you shall return. The LORD God says, See now that I, I am He, And there is no god besides Me; It is I who put to death and give life. I have wounded and it is I who heal, And there is no one who can deliver from My hand. And, I am the LORD, and there is no other; Besides Me there is no God. (...) The One forming light and creating darkness, Causing well-being and creating calamity; I am the LORD who does all these. Accept your lot in life, be happy with it, and search for Him; acknowledge your futility and seek for fullness. Repent and believe in Him. See that mankind takes all of the curses which God gave and tries to reverse them: God gave woman pain in child-bearing, but mankind gives woman numbing shots; God made woman desire for her husband, and he rules over her, but mankind turns women into feminists; God cursed the ground so that in toil it will produce its fruit, but mankind has technology; God makes man return to the dust in old age, but mankind has beauty products and wrinkling agents and plastic surgery. But whatever we do, we cannot reverse death: all of us will return to Him in the end. Repent to Him which there is still day: He seeks for those who seek for Him.

Futility of Wealth

Here is a parable, a dark secret which light reveals: A man goes into a pub with everything he owns. He sits down at a gambling table to gamble what he owns. At first, he bets only a little of what he has. He rolls the dice and a miracle happens: all the dice line up. The man yells, "Today is my lucky day. I will bet even more next time!" So he rolls again, but this time, with more of his possessions on the table. A miracle happens again: all the dice line up. The man continues this over and over many times and every time, he wins; eventually, he has everything he owns on the table even the shirt off his back. He thinks to himself, "Surely I have rolled these dice over and over, and they haven't failed me yet!" However, the dealer of the dice sees the man and thinks to himself, "You fool! One day these dice will not line up and everything you have will be mine. For I have worked a long time in this pub and I see it happen everytime. The dice will one day not line up and everything that you have will be mine." Then the day came when the dice did not line up and everything on the table was taken from him. Then, because the man didn't have enough money for even a beer, he was cast out of the pub into darkness where he wept because everything he had was gone. So it is with every man who thinks only of this life and possessions and money.

Solomon says, He who loves money will not be satisfied with money, nor he who loves wealth with his income; this also is vanity. When goods increase, they increase who eat them, and what advantage has their owner but to see them with his eyes? Sweet is the sleep of a laborer, whether he eats little or much, but the full stomach of the rich will not let him sleep. There is a grievous evil that I have seen under the sun: riches were kept by their owner to his hurt, and those riches were lost in a bad venture. And he is father of a son, but he has nothing in his hand. As he came from his mother's womb he shall go again, naked as he came, and shall take nothing for his toil that he may carry away in his hand. This also is a grievous evil: just as he came, so shall he go, and what gain is there to him who toils for the wind? Moreover, all his days he eats in darkness in much vexation and sickness and anger.
Look around you: point to something that you can take with you when you leave earth. Not even your own body can go with you when you die: it goes back to the dust from which it came; as it is written, Then the LORD God formed man of dust from the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living being. The spirit will return to God who gave it. When we return to Him, it will be a single moment to Him, but a lifetime to us. Everything we think we have, we don't have - we leave it to someone after us who did not work nor toil for it; and even then, the things passed on, do not really remain thereafter; for, The earth is the LORD's and the fullness thereof, the world and those who dwell therein We are merely stewards. Riches do not deliver on the day of wrath; all the silver in the world cannot forgive even a single sin. Amassing riches to our hurt and trusting in them is a futile attempt for saftey; many people pierce themselves with much trouble chasing after riches, as it says, Like the partridge that gathers a brood that she did not hatch, so is he who gets riches but not by justice; in the midst of his days they will leave him, and at his end he will be a fool. Hording wealth and being rich towards oneself, keeping one's fat, and not sharing with others, displeases God; and who do we think we hurt by doing so? If we give, it is to the benefit of others; if we withhold, it is to the hurt of others; even yet, we bring harm to ourselves through dishonest gain as it says, There is a grievous evil that I have seen under the sun: riches were kept by their owner to his hurt, and those riches were lost in a bad venture. And, Your gold and silver have corroded, and their corrosion will be evidence against you and will eat your flesh like fire. You have laid up treasure in the last days. Behold, the wages of the laborers who mowed your fields, which you kept back by fraud, are crying out against you, and the cries of the harvesters have reached the ears of the Lord of hosts. You have lived on the earth in luxury and in self-indulgence. You have fattened your hearts in a day of slaughter.

Futility in Death

There once was a man who lived in a tent. He was in anguish in his tent because it was not comfortable. The man hoped forward to his permanent home which was far away in a distant land. Night and day he longed for it and thought about it. So finally, the day came when the man's tent tore, and he went to his permanent home. The tent was destroyed and the man went to his real home where he rejoiced and spent the rest of his days.

We have this present life, but it is not ours to keep for eternity; if this life were for eternity, then we wouldn't have to die. But because we will one day die, this shows this isn't our home. Our lives are entrusted to us for only a little while. Since we know we will one day die, we look forward to our next life: our permanent home. We live worthy in our tents since we know that evil deserves punishment and good deserves reward. The time in our tent is a time of testing. We always have two choices: good or evil. If there were only one choice, we wouldn't have free will since we would be forced into choosing it, but there are two choices. We will reap what we sow; we gain what we have put in; we will be recompensed according to what we have done in our bodies. There is life after life, which is eternal; and there is death after death, which is the second death; the second death is hell: the lake of fire. Anyone whose name is not written in the Book of Life will die a second time. Paradise is the Kingdom of God: the new Jerusalem, Mount Zion, the new heaven and the new earth, where righteousness dwells. The present heaven and earth are subjected to evil and violence; and all will soon pass away as it is currently passing away. The new heaven and the new earth will never pass away: a kingdom that cannot be shaken. After the great judgement, when the LORD will judge the living and the dead according to their deeds, and repay, we will go to our permenant place. He will reward all who do good for the good done and repay evil for the evil done. For certain, God has sown everlasting life in us so that we would covet life; but, we die. We desire life, yet we are without knowledge. As it says, He has made everything appropriate in its time. He has also set eternity in their heart, yet so that man will not find out the work which God has done from the beginning even to the end. (...) I said to myself, “God will judge both the righteous man and the wicked man,” for a time for every matter and for every deed is there. I concluded that God is testing us, to show us that we are no better than animals. After all, the same fate awaits human beings and animals alike. One dies just like the other. They are both the same kind of creature. A human being is no better off than an animal, because life has no meaning for either. They are both going to the same place — the dust. They both came from it; they will both go back to it. How can anyone be sure that the human spirit goes upwards while an animal's spirit goes down into the ground? I have seen that nothing is better than that man should be happy in his activities, for that is his lot. For who will bring him to see what will occur after him?
In the state we die is the same state that we will be raised: if a sinner dies, he will be raised a sinner and if a righteous man dies, he will be raised a righteous man. The LORD shows us through sleep what death is like: we sleep and hours have passed without us knowing. In sleep, we've accomplished nothing, and we've done nothing, except rest and dream; in the place of the dead, there is nothing. While we live, there is hope for repentance; but in the place of the dead, there is no hope or anything any longer, as it says, Also, the hearts of the children of man are full of evil, and madness is in their hearts while they live, and after that they go to the dead. But he who is joined with all the living has hope, for a living dog is better than a dead lion. For the living know that they will die, but the dead know nothing, and they have no more reward, for the memory of them is forgotten. Their love and their hate and their envy have already perished, and forever they have no more share in all that is done under the sun. (...) Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with your might, for there is no work or thought or knowledge or wisdom in Sheol, to which you are going. We see generations of our descendants grow up before our eyes and at the prime of our joy and delight, we're required to leave. Not one of us chose our family; no-one chose the body in which they would be born; not one chose what country they would live; yea, no one even asked to be born! We are all given what we have with no say in what was given; and it is all taken away, without our gesture. We have all been dealt cards that cannot be taken back; and we will all be judged based on what God has given to us. Do we dare quarrel with our Maker? Woe to him who strives with Him who formed him, a pot among earthen pots! Does the clay say to him who forms it, ‘What are you making?’ or ‘Your work has no handles’? Woe to him who says to a father, ‘What are you begetting?’ or to a woman, ‘With what are you in labor?’ It was by grace God gave us our lives. If we are dissatisfied with our cards, do we try to take them back? Knowing where they come from, can we even take them back as goods bought in a store? Can we have a do-over in this life or trade our cards in for someone else's cards? Surely, God is just: wouldn't He will give us a way out. But one day we will all return our cards to the Dealer: our hand is only for a short while. Shall we go all in for something which is temporary? No! Rather, for that which is everlasting! But where are the paths of truth?

The Danger - Sin

God, a Consuming Fire

We want to do good in our minds, but in our bodies, we don't do the good we intend to do. We see it every year: we make a new year's resolution and say, "This year will be nothing like the last, I am going to do this and that!" And we do 'this' and 'that' very well for the first few months, but the time comes when we inevitably go back to our old ways. In our minds we want to do good, but in our flesh, we don't do the good we want; we have good intentions, but the power of our sinful desires carry us away. If we did everything we wanted to do every new year, we would be made more and more perfect as time goes on; but in fact, we become more and more sinful. We sin and store up more and more transgression into our accounts which is a debt against us. We are all in a great debt and God's anger burns against our sins, for He Himself is a consuming fire. God is a just judge, And God is angry with the wicked every day. Someone might say, "Isn't the devil fire, but not God?" The devil masquerades himself as an angel of light - himself, truly being darkness, but God being light, shrouds Himself in a dark cloud. The devil doesn't rule over hell, but God prepared hell for the devil and his angels. Our sins requires punishment since God is just; He cannot let sin go unpunished. But you might say, "Sin in me causes me to do wrong, but I myself want to do good. How is God just in punishing me?". Sin is inseperable from the one who commits sin: the sinner allows sin to be committed, being led astray by his sinful desires. Our sin should bring us to sorrow, just as someone sinning against us makes us sorrowful; and all sin is against God - sin is transgression against God's Law. But on this one will I look: On him who is poor and of a contrite spirit, And who trembles at My Word.
God loves truth and justice and righteousness, but hates evil and sin and wrong-doing. He gives us His commands so that we would know good and walk in His ways. God never changes: He is now, was, and is yet to come. He fills all things and is in all things, yet in all creation nothing compares to Him; yet, there is one thing in all of creation which He is like: a consuming fire. This is the fear of the LORD: to abhor evil. If you love your life, you will fear the LORD. If you want wisdom, Solomon in His wisdom says, The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom This is how we obtain wisdom, and also how we perfect wisdom: by fearing God. The more we fear Him, the wiser we are. The wisest thing we can do is fear God; the wisest thing to do is to get wisdom. Revere Him like you would a Father or a King: He is the Father of fathers and the King of kings, the Ancient one, who establishes kingdoms. And on the other hand: the one who does not fear God is foolish - for only a fool destroys himself. The left hand is the weaker of the two hands, so strive for what is right. Just as light is better than darkness, so wisdom is better than folly. The one who is wise has eyes in his head and sees, walking in the light; a man in evil, walks in darkness, he does not know where he is going.
There is a healthy fear of the Lord, and an unhealthy fear...

God is the First and the Last, yet He has no beginning nor end; He is the Beginning and the End, He lives from everlasting to everlasting. All things He has created. He only has power to take the sword to His creation, yet He gives life and relents in destroying the wicked - He is patient with the wicked everyday. He sets all things upright and gives them a habitation to live: He gives the fish to the sea, the birds to the air, and the mammals to the land. He created them and gives them their food; He doesn't create them so that they would be in want, but everything is established from the beginning of creation. He created once by wisdom, and all things have been established through wisdom; yet all things that we presently see, are passing away - they will all come to an end. The end has been mentioned from the beginning; all ages have spoken about the end of the world. But, The Lord is not slow about His promise, as some count slowness, but is patient toward you, not wishing for any to perish but for all to come to repentance So God has shown us grace and He has given us time so that we would repent: His goodness leads us to repentance. God is not taught good, but He is good; God is not taught love, but He is love; God was not taught anything, for He knows all things and all things come from Him. He is served by no-one as if He needed anything, but He gives life and breath to all things. He has all power and authority to do as He pleases, yet He chooses good - for He is good. Jesus Christ, the Lamb of God

Under the Old Covenant, people were forgiven by God for their sins by sacrificing an animal. When they sinned, blood needed to be shed for cleansing: the animal had to be perfect (no defects, blemishes, etc), it also had to be a clean animal, and it had to be innocent of all wrong (like goring, etc). Blood is cleansing to the spirit just as water is to the body. With sin comes death, sin and death work hand-in-hand because they are friends, just as righteousness and life are friends; death is the result of sin and life is the result of righteousness. And when the substitutionary death of an animal is given, the Israelites could only give it for unintentional sins: sins that were done in ignorance. There was no sacrifice under the Law for intentional sins. These sins were expiated in another way: either excommunication, punishment, or death. There were also certain sins that could not be atoned for other than in these ways. As it says in Exodus, Behold, I am going to send an angel [Christ] before you to guard you along the way and to bring you into the place which I have prepared. Be on your guard before Him and obey His voice; do not be rebellious toward Him, for He will not pardon your transgression since My name is in Him. And all rebellion is willful and such sins are punished. As it says, For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal but mighty in God for pulling down strongholds, casting down arguments and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ, and being ready to punish all disobedience when your obedience is fulfilled. So we see that the Israelites had to sacrifice a perfect lamb or a similar animal to be cleansed from their unintentional sins. The sacrifice did not cleanse the offerer's conscience though, because they still had guilt since the innocent animal had to die in their place; the sacrifice did not take away their sin, but only covered their sin temporarily, as it says in Hebrews, But in those [sacrifices] there is a reminder of sins year by year. For it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins. A goat is not equivalent to a human, so it cannot be substituted for a human. But rather life shall be for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot.
The animal was the offerer's substitute and the sin offering was brought into the Holy Place when the congregation as a whole sinned, and the bodies of the animals were burnt outside the camp and not eaten by the offerer. As it says, Although the high priest brings the blood of animals into the Holy Place as a sacrifice for sin, the bodies are burned outside the camp. And so Jesus also suffered outside the city gate, to sanctify the people by His own blood. Therefore let us go to Him outside the camp, bearing the disgrace He bore. For here we do not have a permanent city, but we are looking for the city that is to come. Here is the point to all this: all these things were given to testify to Jesus who was to come. Jesus, the Lamb of God, was sacrificed on our behalf as our substitute, for the forgiveness of our sins, not a sacrifice that needed to be offered over and over as was with everytime an Israelite sinned, but once for all; and He was the sin offerring for the whole world, not just for the congregation of Israel. As John the Baptist testified, Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world! The offerrings of those sacrifices by Israel for the whole congregation were not eaten by the offerer; but yet, We have an altar from which those who serve the tent have no right to eat For Christ gives us His flesh and His blood to eat; and unless we eat the flesh and drink the blood of the Son of Man, we have no life in us. For His flesh is true food and His blood is true drink. And this is a reminder of His grace towards us: communion.

What's more? We do not have guilt over the fact that our sins have caused the innocent Christ to be slain, for He yet lives and that, forevermore, seated in the heavens; by His resurrection, we have justification with God. His one act of obedience is the work that matters in the sight of God, united with our work of faith. And since He lives, He is Lord of our life who believe. The work of the obedience of Christ forgives sins and makes many righteous: His one act. God doesn't see our sin anymore, for they have been put out of sight. He proves to us through the testimony of the Psalms and the Prophets and the book of the Law which were all written beforehand, and also the testimony of the Apostles who had seen the risen Lord, testifying to us what they have seen and heard, namely, that Jesus is the Son of God, the Messiah. Now, we have confidence to approach God, through His Way, which is through Christ's body, as it says, And every priest stands ministering daily and offering repeatedly the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins. But this Man, after He had offered one sacrifice for sins forever, sat down at the right hand of God, from that time waiting until His enemies are made His footstool. And this way, entices holy fear in us, that we would not sin, because Christ has been made weak for us, although He is very strong.
Before the giving of the Law, when the Israelites were in slavery in Egypt (in the Book of Exodus), the Israelites had to sacrifice a lamb and paint their doorpost of their house with its blood. The angel of death, sent by God, came through the city and slayed the firstborn of every house whose doorpost was not marked with the lamb's blood -- the death angel was commanded to pass over the house marked and not to harm anyone inside. This is now remembered by the Israelites, even to this day, as the Passover, a testimony to all people. For seven days after Passover, they cleaned out the leaven from their homes. In the same way, Jesus was sacrificed, and we have been sprinkled with His blood and purified by it, and our guilty consciences has been cleansed. Therefore purge out the old leaven, that you may be a new lump, since you truly are unleavened. For indeed Christ, our Passover, was sacrificed for us. Therefore let us keep the feast, not with old leaven, nor with the leaven of malice and wickedness, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth. We bear His blood on the doorpost of our hearts, by which, we open up to let Him in; we have been rescued from our slavery in Egypt by Christ. By the cross, we have been crucified to this world and this world crucified to us, for anyone who has love for this world, the love of the Father is not in them; for everything that is of the world is contrary to God and everything men value is detestable in the sight of God - it's this ungodly world that God is coming to judge. But we who love the Father, have been rescued its lusts. Flee for refuge in the Son of God! This is the sacrifice that God desires, not that of bulls or goats, but of setting apart our sins for destruction so that we may not be destroyed with them, fleeing from this world, so that we would not be punished with it; this is the sacrifice of death, so that we may live. Even if we purge out every crumb of leaven in our lives, if we have not the blood, we do so in vain. How glorious is God's provision of grace! Now, we purge out the old leaven from our homes.

Jesus Christ, the Lion from the Tribe of Judah

Then Samson went down with his father and mother to Timnah, and they came to the vineyards of Timnah. And behold, a young lion came toward him roaring. Then the Spirit of the Lord rushed upon him, and although he had nothing in his hand, he tore the lion in pieces as one tears a young goat. But he did not tell his father or his mother what he had done. Then he went down and talked with the woman, and she was right in Samson's eyes. After some days he returned to take her. And he turned aside to see the carcass of the lion, and behold, there was a swarm of bees in the body of the lion, and honey. He scraped it out into his hands and went on, eating as he went. And he came to his father and mother and gave some to them, and they ate. But he did not tell them that he had scraped the honey from the carcass of the lion. (...) Out of the eater came something to eat. Out of the strong came something sweet.
A lion is strong and powerful, no doubt. No-one certainly takes a lion into their home so that their children can play with it. No, they don't. A lion is dangerous. A lion isn't sweet; it won't let you pet it and it won't give you any kisses (not without taking off some of your flesh first). In a similar way, Jesus Christ is mighty and powerful being the Son of God in the exact likeness of God with the name which is above every other name, and one of His names: the Lion from the Tribe of Judah. As it is written, Then one of the elders said to me, 'Do not weep! See, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has triumphed. He [Jesus] is able to open the scroll and its seven seals.' He came down into this world and died a criminal's death although He was not a criminal and there was no fault found in Him; but He died and was raised so that we would be made right with God. His life was not taken from Him, but He laid it down willingly and He had authority to take it back up again. Before He was captured He said, Or do you think that I cannot appeal to My Father, and He will at once put at My disposal more than twelve legions of angels? How then will the Scriptures be fulfilled, [which say] that it must happen this way? So although He is powerful, He willingly became weak for our sakes. He did many mighty miracles: walking on water, healing the sick, rebuking evil spirits, and many others. Yet, from His power comes something sweet: and what's sweeter than honey? The sweetness of the gospel - eternal life as a gift in His name.
The Father is the Eater of sacrifices. Whenever a sacrifice was given to the LORD, He consumed them. For He consumes them with fire; for He is a consuming fire; and all sacrifices were burnt on the altar unto God as a soothing aroma. So you see, from the Eater of sacrifices, came a Sacrifice; from the death of the Mighty came something sweet. From His death came forgiveness of sins and our justification with God, our friendship restored to God, the promise of the Holy Spirit, and everlasting life to all who persist. Although He is as a powerful lion, He also became as a lamb which is meek; Jesus is the Great Shepherd of the flock and He is also the Lamb of God. So although God is the Eater of sacrifices, He doesn't need us to eat, nor does He need us to serve Him as if He needed anything from our hands, but rather, He gives us a Sacrifice so that we would honor Him with righteous sacrifices, as it is written, Hear, O My people, and I will speak; O Israel, I will testify against you; I am God, your God. I do not reprove you for your sacrifices, And your burnt offerings are continually before Me. I shall take no young bull out of your house Nor male goats out of your folds. For every beast of the forest is Mine, The cattle on a thousand hills. I know every bird of the mountains, And everything that moves in the field is Mine. If I were hungry I would not tell you, For the world is Mine, and all it contains. Shall I eat the flesh of bulls Or drink the blood of male goats? Offer to God a sacrifice of thanksgiving And pay your vows to the Most High; Call upon Me in the day of trouble; I shall rescue you, and you will honor Me. As the riddle from Samson says, Out of the eater came something to eat; out of the strong came something sweet. God, who is the consumer of sacrifices by fire, gave a Sacrifice, which is Christ, for us to eat: His flesh and His blood (which is Communion, but not in the literal sense as the Catholics believe). He being strong as a lion, has been 'sweet' to us through Christ Jesus. Although He is greatly gracious toward us, that is not an excuse for us to do evil, for He is powerful, but became weak for us; therefore, do not take His kindness for weakness -- He's mighty in His dealing with us, specifically for our building up, and not for our tearing down; but He will, if we neglect His cloak of righteousness for a cloak of maliciousness, destroy us. Know the peaceful fruit of righteousness and the abounding grace of Christ! His kindness causes us to repent.

And in reference to the Tribe of Judah, Jacob had 12 sons and each son became a tribe of Israel. Jesus, as far as His human nature is concerned, came from the tribe of Judah. Later in life when in Egypt, Judah offered himself in the place of his younger brother, Benjamin, as it is written, Indeed, your servant (Judah) guaranteed the boy’s safety to my father, saying, ‘If I do not return him to you, I will bear the guilt before you, my father, all my life.’ Now please let your servant stay here as my lord’s slave in place of the boy. Let him return with his brothers. For how can I go back to my father without the boy? I could not bear to see the misery that would overwhelm him. This foreshadows what Jesus would do later with His substitution for us that He would make willingly: He interceded and did what no-one else could do; He stepped into our world, being the Mighty one who existed from the beginning, and offered Himself as a sacrifice for sin to rescue us from the pentatly of our slavery to sin. And we are His younger brothers in God's family, that is, if we do the will of God, as Christ says, "Who is My mother and who are My brothers?" And stretching out His hand toward His disciples, He said, "Behold My mother and My brothers! For whoever does the will of My Father who is in heaven, he is My brother and sister and mother."

Jesus Christ, the Scapegoat

At the appointed time, on the day of Atonement, the high priest confessed the people's sins onto a goat by the laying on of hands. That goat was to carry the sins far away to a remote place in the wilderness. In the same way, while we were still helpless, at the appointed time, Christ died for the ungodly, For while we were still weak, at the right time Christ died for the ungodly. For scarcely for a righteous man will one die; yet perhaps for a good man someone would even dare to die. But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. Much more then, having now been justified by His blood, we shall be saved from wrath through Him. For if when we were enemies we were reconciled to God through the death of His Son, much more, having been reconciled, we shall be saved by His life. And not only that, but we also rejoice in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom we have now received the reconciliation. He has removed our sins from us to a remote place, not to be remembered, passing them off to a distant land. As it says in the Psalms, He has not dealt with us according to our sins, Nor rewarded us according to our iniquities. For as high as the heavens are above the earth, So great is His lovingkindness toward those who fear Him. As far as the east is from the west, So far has He removed our transgressions from us. Just as a father has compassion on [his] children, So the LORD has compassion on those who fear Him. He is our scapegoat who took the blame for us. He takes our sins away, freeing us from the penalty of them! Oh, the depths of the mercy and grace of the Almighty God, who loved us and gave Himself for us; let it never be said or even thought of again, that the Lord God doesn't love us. Oh! How He demonstrated His great love for us on the cross at Calvary! He didn't merely say, "I love you", but proved His love in the most greatest way possible, by giving up His own life. Oh, that we would fear God, so that we would stay away even from all appearances of evil; for the fear of the Lord is to depart from iniquity.

The Lord Jesus is one, but what He did was for many. Just like the scapegoat for Israel, it was one goat, but for the whole nation. The Lord God likewise was pleased with one man and blessed the whole world: Noah. And the LORD smelled a soothing aroma. Then the LORD said in His heart, “I will never again curse the ground for man’s sake, although the imagination of man’s heart is evil from his youth; nor will I again destroy every living thing as I have done. Again, He was pleased with another man and blessed the entire world as well: Abraham. In your seed all the nations of the earth shall be blessed, because you have obeyed My voice. But the gift is not like the trespass. For if the many died by the trespass of the one man, how much more did God’s grace and the gift that came by the grace of the one man, Jesus Christ, overflow to the many! Nor can the gift of God be compared with the result of one man’s sin: The judgment followed one sin and brought condemnation, but the gift followed many trespasses and brought justification. For if, by the trespass of the one man, death reigned through that one man, how much more will those who receive God’s abundant provision of grace and of the gift of righteousness reign in life through the one man, Jesus Christ! Consequently, just as one trespass resulted in condemnation for all people, so also one righteous act resulted in justification and life for all people. For just as through the disobedience of the one man the many were made sinners, so also through the obedience of the one man the many will be made righteous. The law was brought in so that the trespass might increase. But where sin increased, grace increased all the more, so that, just as sin reigned in death, so also grace might reign through righteousness to bring eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.
A great principle is found here: our sins can only be forgiven when we own them -- how can we be forgiven of a fault if we claim we have not done it? If we confess our sins and He is faithful to forgive us of our sins; God has proven our forgiveness by sending His Son; but we have to come to Him to be forgiven. If we are guilty, and we do not confess our sins, we make Him out to be a liar, claiming we have not sinned, and the truth is not in us. How can we be forgiven of sin that is not ours when we reject it belonging to us? Therefore, let us be truthful and confess our sins to God and to each other so that we may be healed; for with sin, comes death and everyone has been declared guilty by the Law. Afterwards, we will know that our sins are forgiven, for He will purge our guilty coinsciences. God has promised - it is impossible for Him to lie; His promises are a sure foundation to build upon. We will know we have been forgiven because our guilt will be removed and our conscience will no longer condemn us. Jesus took upon Himself our burdens, guilt, and shame, so that we would not have to - confess! Let us not walk around with our burdens, but walk freely, giving them to Him! Let us not walk with a hunch because of our burdens, but uprightly. Who do we hurt by not confessing? There is no soundness in my flesh because of Your indignation; There is no health in my bones because of my sin. For my iniquities are gone over my head; As a heavy burden they weigh too much for me. Jesus Christ, the True Food and the True Drink

Everlasting Life

We, being in the tent of our body, look forward to our permanent home and eternal life which God has promised to us in Christ Jesus. The tent is not our permanent home, but the tent is temporary and taken down; so it is with our bodies, but we look forward to our heavenly home and eternal life in our new bodies. We will be clothed with our heavenly dwelling and heavenly body. Following God will make you different, being in the midst of a crooked and perverse generation; nonetheless as the One who called you is holy, be holy. This is what is holiness: different, special, or set apart. The world also has a word for this: weird. They say it derogatorily. However, if the world is from below, and we are from above, certainly we will be as aliens or foreigners to them, a strange and peculiar people. As the Spirit of God says in Hebrews, All these died in faith, without receiving the promises, but having seen them and having welcomed them from a distance, and having confessed that they were strangers and exiles on the earth. For those who say such things make it clear that they are seeking a country of their own. And indeed if they had been thinking of that country from which they went out, they would have had opportunity to return. But as it is, they desire a better country, that is, a heavenly one. Therefore God is not ashamed to be called their God; for He has prepared a city for them. and as Peter says, Beloved, I urge you as aliens and strangers to abstain from fleshly lusts which wage war against the soul. For this present life is not our home - we are only passing through for a short while as a foreigner. We have received the truth and have come to know the truth. There is a lie that our hearts deceive us with, Their inner thought is that their houses will last forever, Their dwelling places to all generations; They call their lands after their own names. But knowing the truth, we look forward to an everlasting dwelling which is beyond corruption and decay, which is new and will remain new forever.

Know that there is only one Way: that is Jesus Christ. There are many other paths, but all those paths lead to an end which is a dead end - they are not eternal and do not go on forever. However, there is a Way which is eternal and the Way is narrow. The Way is Christ Jesus, and He has gone before us to be the first to be raised from the dead - and He lives forever more. This gives us assurance that if we live as He lived, we will also inherit the same life - we follow Him and receive the same reward. Those who believe in Him, also trust and follow Him; and those who follow Him, do as He did; and those who do as He did, will receive the reward He received. Those who do not pick up their cross and follow Him, are not worthy of Him (Matt 16). Not many will follow after Him, but we are not like the many, but like the few who persist in faith unto the end. As it is written, Enter through the narrow gate. For wide is the gate and broad is the road that leads to destruction, and many enter through it. But small is the gate and narrow the road that leads to life, and only a few find it. and For the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord. There is one life and the life is in the Son. If we be in the Son, then we have the life; if we be without the Son, we do not have the life. Live in God, abiding in Him until the end. Living in Him is life indeed.
There is a walk by faith - but a walk does not imply that it will be easy. The way of faith is also called a run: we run the race of faith, keeping our eyes on Jesus. This concept is similar to the concept of spiritual milk: milk is good when you're young, but when you are mature in Christ, you move onto solid food. Likewise, as children, we first learn how to walk, then run. In the faith, we first walk, then as we walk in the light and gain confidence, we begin to run the race of faith. Walking may be easy, but when you begin to run, you're exerting a lot more effort. And those who run in a race, do so to win a crown in the end that will perish, but we run for a crown of everlasting life that will never perish. Let us really consider how hard it is to be saved, but how great the reward is to those who persevere till the end; as it is written, For it is time for judgment to begin with the family of God; and if it begins with us, what will the outcome be for those who disobey the gospel of God? And, If it is hard for the righteous to be saved, what will become of the ungodly and the sinner? But also be encouraged by remembering what the disciples asked the Lord in regard to it being hard for the rich man to enter into the kingdom of heaven: "Who then can be saved?". But Jesus answered, With man this is impossible, but with God all things are possible. And, the apostle, I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me. I believe that we will be put to shame in the next life, having complained even a complaint in this life because the surpassing greatness of the heavenly life does not even compare with the present sufferings of this life! This life is replaced by the next life, but the next life is replaced by no-thing. This is what Solomon meant in his wisdom when he said, The end of a thing is better than its beginning; The patient in spirit is better than the proud in spirit. Because the end is forever, but the beginning has an end; patience is much better, waiting for the eternal life to come, than it is to make our home here on earth now. The proverb that says, "Better is a bird in the hand, than two in the bush", is of more weight with the children of unbelief than it is for us who believe - for with patience, we will inherit the things that God has promised, being much better than anything we can receive today. As it says in Kings, Then he said to him, “Did not my heart go with you when the man turned back from his chariot to meet you? Is it time to receive money and to receive clothing, olive groves and vineyards, sheep and oxen, male and female servants? And as Christ promised us, Assuredly, I say to you, there is no one who has left house or brothers or sisters or father or mother or wife or children or lands, for My sake and the gospel’s, who shall not receive a hundredfold now in this time—houses and brothers and sisters and mothers and children and lands, with persecutions—and in the age to come, eternal life.
See, I have set before you today life and prosperity, and death and adversity; in that I command you today to love the Lord your God, to walk in His ways and to keep His commandments and His statutes and His judgments, that you may live and multiply, and that the Lord your God may bless you in the land where you are entering to possess it. But if your heart turns away and you will not obey, but are drawn away and worship other gods and serve them, I declare to you today that you shall surely perish. You will not prolong your days in the land where you are crossing the Jordan to enter and possess it. I call heaven and earth to witness against you today, that I have set before you life and death, the blessing and the curse. So choose life in order that you may live, you and your descendants, by loving the Lord your God, by obeying His voice, and by holding fast to Him; for this is your life and the length of your days, that you may live in the land which the Lord swore to your fathers, to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, to give them.

New Heaven & New Earth

For behold, I will create new heavens and a new earth. The former things will not be remembered, nor will they come to mind. But be glad and rejoice forever in what I create; for I will create Jerusalem to be a joy and its people to be a delight. I will rejoice in Jerusalem and take delight in My people. The sounds of weeping and crying will no longer be heard in her.
Then I saw a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and earth had passed away, and the sea was no more. I saw the holy city, the new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband. And I heard a loud voice from the throne saying: “Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man, and He will dwell with them. They will be His people, and God Himself will be with them as their God. ‘He will wipe away every tear from their eyes,’ and there will be no more death or mourning or crying or pain, for the former things have passed away.” And the One seated on the throne said, “Behold, I make all things new.” Then He said, “Write this down, for these words are faithful and true.”
We have been created new in Christ Jesus if He indeed has given us His Spirit & cut away our old nature with a circumcision not done by human hands. Only the new creation will inherit the kingdom of God - flesh and blood cannot enter the kingdom of God. In the beginning, God created the present world and humanity and all flesh in six days; He will also create the new heaven and the new earth, which He has promised, making all things new, as a place where righteousness dwells; for this present heavens and earth are corrupt and everything rots and decays over time because of the dysfunction of the fallen human nature and the death & destruction that comes from it; but the new heavens and the new earth will be a place that does not decay nor wear out like a garment, a place where righteousness dwells. The present world is predominantly evil because this world belongs to satan, but the world to come, belongs to Christ and God, and righteousness will only dwell there. Just as God did away with the ancient world with a flood and afterward only Noah and his family (who were righteous) survived, so it will be when God floods the world again - with fire - preserving the righteous who will inherit the new earth. Just as Noah went into the arc and got out of the arc and the world was like new, so it will be after the fire burns the earth and the heavens, it will be rendered new after. For the present earth underwent a flood and wiped away the wicked, but Noah was caught up to heaven, floating above the water in the arc - in the end, only Noah and his family were saved, so only righteousness was on the earth after that time (for a short while, for sin began to reign again). It was like the world was made new after the flood and Noah inherited it. This is a picture of how the end will be, but with a more severe end - of fire - and a more better beginning - an unending one. It is by the things that have happened in the past that we can see the future; as it says, Let them bring forth and show us what will happen; Let them show the former things, what they were, That we may consider them, And know the latter end of them; Or declare to us things to come. And, But as the days of Noah were, so also will the coming of the Son of Man be.
The first of the new creation is Jesus Christ, who is the firstfruits of the resurrection, the firstfruits of the new creation; the new creation is not of the will of man, but of the will of God. Everything of the old is passing away and only the work of the new will remain; we who are created anew in Christ Jesus will inherit all the new things. In the beginning, God created everything that we see by His Word and now, He creates a new creation by the same Word, but a creation that we do not presently see which is in human hearts; as it is written, For God, who said, “Light shall shine out of darkness,” is the One who has shone in our hearts to give the Light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Christ. Paul correlates the first creation with the new creation - which starts in human hearts, which finishes with the new heavens and the new earth. If the old were perfect, then there would be no reason for a new; but the old is imperfect and there is fault with it. As Christ testifies, And from the days of John the Baptist until now the kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and the violent take it by force. We know that the earth is fallen, but this Scripture suggests that the present heaven has faults, albeit it is God's abode. The apostle says, For we do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this age, against spiritual hosts of wickedness in the heavenly places. So it is necessary that the heavens be destroyed too, so that all may be made new.

We are God's house. God's building, being built up and edified as living stones into a spiritual house, the new Jerusalem that will come out of heaven in which we will dwell as a holy priesthood, a chosen race, a peculiar people for God's own possession; for she is our mother, she bears us as we inhabit her. As the Truth living in Peter says, And coming to Him as to a living stone which has been rejected by men, but is choice and precious in the sight of God, you also, as living stones, are being built up as a spiritual house for a holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ. And, but the Jerusalem above is free, which is the mother of us all. In the Revelation of Jesus Christ, the wall of the city of the new Jerusalem has twelve foundation stones, precious stones, written on them the names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb. So, quite literally, stones are set in place to make a dwelling in which God dwells. Presently, we being the church, He inhabits us, making us into a temple of the living God in which He dwells; but then, quite literally, we will dwell in the city and our names on the stones of the new Jerusalem, Mount Zion, and we will walk with God and we will be His people. He promised to give us who overcome a white stone written on it a new name that no one knows except the one who receives it. I believe this stone will be used in the building of the wall of the new Jerusalem which we will be granted to enter - our names written in the book of life, our names written on the walls of the new city which comes from heaven. Glory be to God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, who loved us and granted to us every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places.
